Copy.ai was acquired by Fullcast, a revenue-operations company, on October 15, 2025. So the real question in 2026 is not "which AI writes better copy." Both tools route your prompt to the same family of frontier models. The question is **who is the tool built for**: one person who writes and follows up with people, or a sales team that wants workflows. Answer that and the choice makes itself.

## What each one is in 2026

### Rytr: a writer plus a follow-up assistant

Rytr now has two halves. **Create** is the AI writer most people know: 40+ use cases (emails, product descriptions, social posts, blog outlines), tone settings, rewrite and expand tools, 30+ languages. **Engage** is the new part: it connects to your email, Slack, calendar and meeting notes, tracks people and promises, gives you a daily briefing, and drafts follow-ups in your voice. Rytr says nothing is sent without your approval.

That matters for the comparison because Rytr's plans are no longer priced on writing. Writing is unlimited from the first paid tier. What changes between Lite, Personal and Pro is how many relationships, connected sources and Engage actions you get.

### Copy.ai: a chat front door to a RevOps platform

Copy.ai's self-serve product is **Chat**: unlimited words, unlimited chat projects, access to models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google Gemini, and five seats. Everything that made Copy.ai distinctive in 2024 — the workflow builder, agents, bulk runs, API access, CRM integrations — now sits behind sales-led plans that start around $1,000 a month on yearly billing. Fullcast has been folding the product into its own stack (industry trackers report a "Fullcast Propel, powered by Copy.ai" branding), and the help docs now live on support.fullcast.com.

Put bluntly: Copy.ai's $29 plan is a team chat app with good model choice. The thing reviewers praise on G2 — workflows for SDRs — is a different, much more expensive product.

## Usage limits: where the meter sits

**Rytr meters relationships, not words.** On Free you get 10,000 characters a month — generated and processed text both count, no rollover. That's roughly 1,500 words, enough to test, not enough to work. From Lite up, characters are unlimited. The meters that remain are Engage meters: connected sources (1 / 2 / all), active relationships (25 / 250 / 1,000), Engage actions per month (25 / 250 / 2,000) and person research runs (3 / 10 / 50).

**Copy.ai meters seats and workflow credits.** Chat words are unlimited, but seats stop at five and workflows aren't included at all. On the sales-led plans the meter is workflow credits (Growth lists 20,000 a month), and running out means a conversation with an account manager.

The practical read: neither tool will ration your writing on a paid plan. They ration the *other* thing they actually sell — Rytr your contacts, Copy.ai your automation.

## Capability: who wins what

### Short-form copy quality

A tie that isn't really a contest. Copy.ai lets you pick among OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini models in Chat, which is genuinely useful if one model's tone annoys you. Rytr's templates give you the right *shape* faster — a product description, a cold email, a LinkedIn post — with tone controls you set once. In our testing, the difference in raw output quality was smaller than the difference between a good prompt and a lazy one.

**Edge: Copy.ai for model choice, Rytr for speed-to-first-draft.**

### Long-form and SEO content

Neither is the right tool. Rytr is strongest under 500 words. Copy.ai's Chat will write long, but it has no SEO scoring, no SERP research, no content briefs. If long-form SEO is the job, look at our [Rytr vs Writesonic](/blog/rytr-vs-writesonic-2026) breakdown or a dedicated SEO tool.

### Follow-ups, briefings and people

**Rytr, uncontested.** Engage is the one thing in this comparison with no counterpart on the other side at the self-serve level. A daily briefing of who you owe a reply to, plus a drafted follow-up you approve, is a real time-saver for freelancers, consultants and founders who sell through relationships rather than sequences. It's new, though — almost all Reddit discussion about Rytr predates Engage, so there is little independent evidence yet on how reliable it is.

### Sales workflows and CRM

**Copy.ai, if you pay for it.** The workflow builder and HubSpot/Salesforce integrations are why Copy.ai keeps strong G2 scores (around 4.5–4.7). But none of that is on the $29 plan. Judge Copy.ai Chat on what it is, not on reviews of a product tier you're not buying.

### Team collaboration

**Copy.ai.** Five seats on one $29 bill with shared projects beats Rytr's per-person plans for small teams.

## Cons: where each one breaks

### Rytr

- **No refunds on subscriptions.** Multiple Trustpilot reviewers describe being refused a refund the day after an annual renewal they forgot about. Set a calendar reminder before renewal.
- **Lifetime-deal buyers felt abandoned.** A June 2026 review calls the handling of early lifetime-account holders "terrible"; independent reviewers note those buyers were moved to a plan with fewer features.
- **Engage is unproven.** The product pivot is weeks to months old. Independent review coverage of the follow-up assistant is thin.
- **Brand voice is shallow.** It's tone mimicry from a sample, one voice on the entry tiers — fine for a solo writer, not enough to keep a team consistent.
- **Free tier is a trial in practice.** 10,000 characters a month runs out in an afternoon.

### Copy.ai

- **No free plan, and a 5-day refund window.** Terms give a full refund only if you cancel within five days of the first purchase.
- **Trustpilot 1.8/5 (196 reviews).** Complaints cluster around billing after cancellation and support that goes silent — one February 2026 reviewer reports no response "for months" after losing account access.
- **The $29 → ~$1,000 cliff.** Six seats or one workflow and you're in an enterprise sales cycle.
- **Roadmap belongs to Fullcast now.** The acquirer's public messaging is about enterprise RevOps. Small-team chat is not where the investment is going, and that's a discontinuation-style risk worth pricing in.
- **Chat is a commodity.** Unlimited multi-model chat for $24/seat-group is good value, but ChatGPT Business, Claude Team and Gemini cover the same ground with bigger ecosystems.

## Who should pick which

**Pick Rytr if:**
- You're a solo writer, freelancer, Etsy/Shopify seller or consultant who writes short copy every day and wants it for $9 a month or less.
- You live in your inbox and the follow-up briefing sounds useful — that's a feature Copy.ai simply doesn't sell at this price.
- You want to start free and only pay when you hit the limit.

**Pick Copy.ai if:**
- You're a team of two to five that wants one shared, multi-model AI chat on a single bill.
- You're a sales org already evaluating Fullcast and want Copy.ai's workflows as part of a RevOps platform (budget five figures).

**Pay for both?** Rarely makes sense. The one case: a small agency on Copy.ai Chat for the team, plus Rytr Personal for the founder who handles client relationships.

**Pick neither if** you need long-form SEO content, or if what you really want is a frontier model — in that case read [Rytr vs ChatGPT](/blog/rytr-vs-chatgpt-2026) first.

## Bottom line

These tools stopped competing for the same buyer. Rytr is the cheapest serious AI writer for one person, with a follow-up assistant bolted on that may or may not become its best feature. Copy.ai is a $24-a-month team chat that sits in front of a RevOps platform priced for enterprises.

If you're one person, start with Rytr's free plan, move to Lite the first time you run out, and only consider Personal if Engage earns its keep. If you're a five-person team that just wants shared multi-model chat, Copy.ai Chat is a fair price — just cancel inside five days if it's not right, because you won't get a second chance.
