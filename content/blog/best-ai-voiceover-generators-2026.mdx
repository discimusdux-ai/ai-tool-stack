# Best AI Voiceover Generators 2026: Characters, Minutes, Seconds or Credits?

There is no single best AI voiceover generator, because the six serious options don't sell the same thing. ElevenLabs sells characters. WellSaid sells *downloaded* minutes. Murf sells hours of generation per year. Speechify Studio sells seconds. Fliki sells credits that are priced by a color code next to each voice. The raw OpenAI and Azure APIs sell characters at a price that makes everything else look expensive.

So the useful question isn't "which voice sounds best?" (listen to the free demos, your ears beat our adjectives). It's **"what unit am I being billed in, and what happens to that unit when I edit?"** Voiceover is an iterative job: you re-record a line, change a pause, swap a voice. The tools differ enormously in what that costs.

We read each vendor's live pricing page and help docs, converted every plan to an approximate cost per hour of finished audio, and collected the complaints. Where a vendor page and a third-party site disagreed, we say so.

## The framing: what is the meter, and what does an edit cost?

Three architectures hide behind the same "AI voice" label.

1. **Character meters** (ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Azure, Murf's API). You pay per character of script. Simple, predictable, and an edited line costs the same as the first take.
2. **Output-time meters** (WellSaid, Murf Studio, Speechify, Fliki). You pay per minute, hour or second of audio. This is friendlier for budgeting a project but the fine print on regeneration differs: WellSaid counts what you *download*, not what you generate; Speechify charges again if you change pitch, speed or emotion.
3. **Bundled-video meters** (Fliki). The voice is one line item inside a credit pool that also pays for stock, AI clips and avatars.

Compare vendors only after converting to a common unit. We use roughly 900 to 1,000 characters per spoken minute, a normal narration pace, so treat character-based hour figures as estimates, not guarantees.

## 1. ElevenLabs: best voice quality, most ways to burn credits

**Best for:** audiobooks, character voices, cloning, dubbing, anyone who judges purely on realism.

**Price [elevenlabs.io/pricing, checked 2026-10-28]:** Free $0 (10,000 credits, non-commercial, attribution required), Starter $6/mo monthly or about $5 yearly (30,000 credits, commercial license, instant voice cloning), Creator $22 monthly or about $18.33 yearly (121,000 credits, professional voice cloning), Pro $99 ($82.50 yearly, 600,000), Scale $299 (1.8M, 3 seats), Business $990 (6M, 10 seats). Annual billing is advertised as two months free. There are first-month promos on Starter and Creator at the time of writing.

**What a credit buys:** for the V2 multilingual models, one character is one credit, and the Flash/Turbo models run 0.5 to 1 credit per character on the API. At our rough pace, Starter is about half an hour of narration a month and Creator a little over two hours. That puts Creator at roughly $9 to $10 per finished hour on monthly billing, competitive with the packaged studios and far above the raw APIs.

**The part nobody reads:** all products share one credit pool. Speech-to-text is 330 credits a minute, music 900, voice changer 1,000, and dubbing between 2,000 and 10,000 credits per minute depending on mode. A Creator plan that is two hours of narration is about a minute of Studio-grade dubbing. Credits roll over for up to two months (balance capped at three times your monthly quota), which is generous.

**Where it breaks:**

- **Billing surprises are on record.** A February 2026 Reddit thread described a $1,089 "Flexible Credit Usage" charge on an account that normally spent $20 to $30 a month, with no reply from support for weeks. It is one user's report, but it is the failure mode to guard against: check your overage and pay-as-you-go settings and turn on two-factor authentication, which the docs tie to purchase caps.
- **Free means attribution.** Free-plan output requires crediting elevenlabs.io in the title if published. Commercial use starts at Starter.
- **Credits are not minutes.** Because one pool covers everything, experimenting with music or dubbing quietly shrinks your narration budget.
- **No affiliate link from us.** ElevenLabs' PartnerStack program is frozen, so we review it independently.

## 2. Fliki: best when the voiceover is going into a video

**Best for:** faceless YouTube, explainers, blog-to-video, social clips where one tool should handle script, voice and visuals.

**Price [fliki.ai/pricing, last verified 2026-10-16]:** Free (3 credits a month, watermark, 720p), Standard $28 monthly or $21 yearly (180 credits, 1,000 voices, 1080p, voice cloning, commercial rights), Premium $88 monthly or $66 yearly (600 credits, 2,000+ voices, videos up to 40 minutes, API access), Enterprise custom.

**The meter:** voiceover credits are priced by a color indicator beside each voice. Per [Fliki's credit guide, updated 2026-10-20](https://fliki.ai/credits): blue is 0.25 credits per minute, green 0.5, yellow 1, purple 2. Replaying an unchanged video is free, editing text in one scene re-charges only that scene, and uploaded MP3/WAV costs nothing. On the cheapest band, Standard's 180 credits stretch to 720 minutes of voice; on yellow voices it is 180 minutes. That makes Fliki the only tool here where the price per hour depends on which voice you pick, and the best-value one for volume.

**Where it breaks:**

- **A voice can change price when you change plan.** Fliki says some voices run on a lighter engine on Free, Basic and lifetime plans and the higher-quality engine on Standard and Premium, with a different credit rate. The picker shows the rate you'll pay, so check it before batch work.
- **Credits are shared with video.** AI clips, avatars and music draw from the same pool, so a voiceover-only job is cheap and a video-heavy month is not.
- **Fewer audio-engineering controls** than a dedicated studio like WellSaid: you're editing scenes, not audio takes.
- **Free is a demo.** Three credits and a watermark.

Fliki is an active affiliate partner of ours, which is why it's featured first for video work. It also genuinely earns the slot: it's the cheapest way here to get a finished narrated video, not just an audio file.

## 3. Murf: the corporate-friendly studio with one editor seat

**Best for:** e-learning, product demos, presentations, internal training.

**Price [murf.ai/pricing via a July 2026 re-verification by ToolProven and an October 2026 Jellypod write-up; the live page showed only API rates when we read it]:** Free (10 minutes, no downloads, no commercial rights), Creator $29 monthly or $19 yearly ($228/year, 24 hours of generation a year), Business $99 monthly or $66 yearly ($792/year, 96 hours a year plus PowerPoint integration), Enterprise custom. That works out to about $9.50 per generated hour on Creator and $8.25 on Business at annual rates. Murf's API is separate: $0.03 per 1,000 characters for studio-quality voices ($30 per million), $0.01 per 1,000 for the low-latency conversational model, with $10 of free credit.

**Where it breaks:**

- **The sticker is the annual rate.** The $19 and $66 headline numbers sit behind a "Saved 33%" toggle. Pay monthly and it's $29 and $99.
- **One editor seat on every self-serve plan,** including Business. Teams hit this wall immediately.
- **Quality is uneven on basic voices.** Aggregated G2, Capterra and Trustpilot complaints describe robotic basic voices, pronunciation work on names and jargon, and heavy fine-tuning time. Treat that as reported sentiment, not a lab test.
- **API stability.** A July 2026 community thread described stricter API rate limits breaking an automated onboarding pipeline. One anecdote, but if you're building on the API, ask support for your limits in writing.

## 4. WellSaid: the cleanest rights story, the strictest meter

**Best for:** corporate narration, regulated industries, teams that care where voice data came from.

**Price [wellsaid.io/ai-voice-pricing, checked 2026-10-28]:** Free (3 download minutes a month, no commercial rights), Starter $10/mo billed yearly ($120/year, 240 minutes a year) or $19 monthly (20 minutes a month), Pro $33/mo billed yearly ($396/year, 2,160 minutes a year) or $49 monthly (180 minutes a month), Business $160 per user per month billed yearly (2,880 minutes a year per user, up to 5 seats), Enterprise custom.

**The meter changed in June 2026.** WellSaid's help center says individual plans were rebuilt into Starter and Pro and that usage is now measured in **downloaded minutes**. Generation stays unlimited on paid plans, so you can audition endlessly and only pay when you export. Annual customers get the full year's minutes up front. That's an unusually friendly model for perfectionists. The per-hour cost depends heavily on tier: Starter is about $30 per hour of downloaded audio, Pro about $11.

**Where it breaks:**

- **Starter is tiny.** 240 minutes a year is four hours total, enough for a handful of explainers.
- **English voices on individual plans.** The Pro tier lists "all English voices"; translation sits in custom plans.
- **Extra minutes are not self-serve yet.** The help center says add-on packs are "planned for a future release," so running out means renewing early or contacting support.
- **Shrinking free tier.** Three download minutes a month and no commercial use.

## 5. Speechify Studio: cheapest per hour, with a credit trap for editors

**Best for:** high-volume narration on a budget, creators who also want dubbing.

**Price [speechify.com/pricing-studio, checked 2026-10-28]:** Free (600 credits, no commercial rights), Starter $100 a year (86,400 credits), a $300-a-year plan (345,600 credits). Credits are seconds: voiceover costs 1 credit per second, dubbing 3, and avatars 30 (the latter two per the vendor's credit table as relayed by ToolProven). By that math Starter is 24 hours of voiceover for $100, about $4 an hour, and the $300 tier is 96 hours, about $3 an hour. That is the lowest cost per hour among the packaged studios here.

**Conflict to know about:** a July 2026 third-party write-up lists monthly prices of $19 and $49 with 7,200 and 28,800 credits a month. The credit totals match the official yearly numbers, but the dollar figures don't. Trust the pricing page you see at checkout.

**Where it breaks:**

- **Edits re-bill.** Speechify says any new generation costs credits, including the same script in a new voice, and changes to pitch, speed or emotion go back through the AI and cost credits (volume changes don't). Re-exporting unchanged audio is free.
- **Avatars are 30 times the cost of voiceover.** Fine to know before you click "video."
- **Free has no commercial rights.** Publishing anything needs a paid plan.
- **Studio and Reader are different products.** Don't pay for the wrong one.

## 6. OpenAI and Azure TTS APIs: the price floor

**Best for:** developers, audio for apps, bulk narration where you can live with less polish and less tooling.

**Price [OpenAI model docs and Azure rates as tracked by LiteLLM and TokenPrice.fyi, mid-2026; confirm before buying]:** OpenAI TTS-1 $15 per million characters, TTS-1 HD $30. Azure AI Speech neural voices $15 per million characters, HD neural $30. Google's standard (non-neural) cloud voices are around $4 per million. At our pace, $15 per million characters is roughly $0.90 per hour of audio, around ten times cheaper than the packaged studios.

**Where it breaks:**

- **No editor.** No timeline, no pronunciation UI, no collaboration. You write code or you use a wrapper.
- **No commercial voice library or cloning on tap.** Voice choice is what the provider ships.
- **Rate limits and policy are yours to manage,** and a deprecated model (one OpenAI TTS variant is flagged as deprecated on a pricing tracker) means rewrites.
- **Wrappers add their own meter.** If you buy a front-end for these APIs you're back to credits.

## Sidebar: the two names missing from this list

**LOVO (Genny)** is gone. Lovo Inc. filed for Chapter 7 in the Southern District of New York on 2026-05-27 (case 1:26-bk-11249, reported by Law360 and Bloomberg Law), amid a voice actors' class action over training data. Its last listed plans were $29/month Basic (2 hours) and $48/month Pro; none can be bought now. If you have exports, keep them.

**PlayHT** was folded into Meta in July 2025, when Meta acquired the PlayAI team (Bloomberg, 2025-07-12). We don't recommend building on a product whose parent has a different roadmap. Both are a free lesson: **voice vendors fail, get bought, and sunset plans.** Export your finished audio and keep your scripts.

## Bottom line

Don't ask which tool sounds best; ask which unit you're being billed in and what a retake costs. Characters reward short, finished scripts. Downloaded minutes reward perfectionists. Credit colors reward people who read the picker. And every vendor on this list can change the rules: WellSaid re-metered in June 2026, LOVO went bankrupt in May, PlayHT was absorbed by Meta. Pay yearly only for a tool you've already used for a month, and keep your scripts and exports somewhere you control.
