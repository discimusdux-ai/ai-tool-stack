There is no single best AI podcast tool in 2026, and any list that hands you one has quietly decided what kind of show you make. A solo audio essayist recorded in a closet and a two-camera interview show shipped to YouTube have almost nothing in common except an RSS feed. They break in different places, so they need different software.

What has changed this year is that the AI features stopped being the differentiator. Every tool on this page transcribes, removes filler words, cleans up room noise and writes your show notes. The differences that still cost you money are structural: where the recording is captured, what the meter counts, and what happens when a session fails.

## Quick verdict

| Best for | Tool | Price to actually use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remote video interviews | Riverside | $24/mo annual (Pro) |
| Editing audio as text | Descript | $24/mo annual (Creator) |
| Cheap emergency audio rescue | Adobe Podcast Enhance | Free, or $9.99/mo |
| Consistent, broadcast-grade loudness | Auphonic | Free 2 h/mo, then ~$11/mo |
| Synthetic voice, dubbing, narration | ElevenLabs | $22/mo (Creator) |
| Hosting with AI show notes attached | Buzzsprout | $19/mo + $5/mo Cohost AI |
| One tool for the whole workflow | Alitu | $38/mo, $32/mo annual |

## The question that actually splits this category

Before you compare features, answer one binary question: **does your show need to look good, or only sound good?**

It sounds trivial. It is the single decision that determines your bill. A video podcast needs local per-participant capture in 4K, which means Riverside or a similar recorder, which means you are starting at $288 a year before you have edited anything. An audio-only show can be recorded in a free tool, cleaned in a free tool, and hosted for $19 a month — total annual cost under $250 — and sound better than most video shows because nobody is spending attention on framing.

Most podcasters answer "video" reflexively because YouTube is where discovery happens. Fine. But then own the consequence: you have bought into the most expensive and least reliable part of this stack, and you should budget for a backup recorder. If your honest answer is "audio", you can skip half the tools below and keep the money.

## 1. Riverside — the remote recorder everyone uses and nobody fully trusts

**Best for:** remote interview shows that publish video
**Price:** Free (2 h/mo, 720p, watermark) · Pro $29/mo, $24/mo annual ($288/yr) · Grow $39/$34 ($408/yr) · Webinar $99/$79 · Business custom

Riverside's pitch has been the same for years and it is still correct: instead of recording the compressed Zoom stream, it records each participant locally at up to 4K video and lossless audio, then uploads the files afterwards. Your guest's rural broadband degrades the conversation you hear, not the file you edit. Nothing else in the consumer price range does this as cleanly, and once you have edited separate 48 kHz tracks you will not go back to a single mixed stream.

The AI layer around it has matured: unlimited transcription on paid tiers, text-based editing, and Magic Clips for cutting vertical social clips out of a long episode. Spotify for Creators now embeds Riverside recording directly, which tells you where the industry has landed.

**Where it breaks**

- **The plan lineup changed and the internet has not caught up.** Riverside's current tiers are Free, Pro, Grow, Webinar and Business. The old Standard plan at $15–19/mo is gone, yet review sites published in mid-2026 still quote it in their comparison tables. If you budgeted around a $180/year Standard plan, your real entry price is $288.
- **Recording hours are metered per month** — 5 hours of multi-track on Pro, 15 on Grow — and separate-track downloads are capped as well (roughly 15/20/25 hours per month across Pro, Grow and Webinar). A weekly two-hour interview show can exhaust Pro.
- **Reliability is the recurring complaint, not the pricing.** Reddit threads from 2024 through 2026 describe stuck uploads, sessions ending without notice, and audio/video sync drift discovered only in post. Third-party status tracking counted 42 incidents in a twelve-month window. Riverside's own help centre has dedicated articles for "my track is not uploading" on desktop, macOS app and mobile — a good sign for support depth, a bad sign for base reliability.
- **Chrome effectively required**, and there is no clean pause workflow.

The community's answer is the right one: run a local backup — QuickTime, Audacity, or a parallel Zoom recording — for any session you cannot recreate. That is not a knock unique to Riverside; it is what professionals do. But it means Riverside is one layer of insurance, not the whole policy.

## 2. Descript — editing audio the way you edit a document

**Best for:** anyone whose bottleneck is editing time, not recording quality
**Price:** Free (1 h media/mo, 100 one-time AI credits) · Hobbyist $24/mo, $16 annual (10 media hours, 400 AI credits) · Creator $35/$24 (30 hours, 800 credits) · Business $65/$50 (40 hours, 1,500 credits, team of 5)

Descript remains the fastest way to turn a raw two-hour conversation into a tight forty-minute episode. You delete a paragraph of text, the audio goes with it. Filler word removal, Studio Sound cleanup, Overdub-style corrections, multi-track sequencing and video export all live in the same timeline. For a talk-format show, the time saved is measured in hours per episode, not minutes.

The catch is the meter, and it is the most misunderstood billing model in podcasting.

**Where it breaks**

- **Media minutes are charged on import, not on export.** Every hour of raw footage you drop in counts, including the takes you throw away. A two-hour, two-camera interview can consume four media hours before you make a single cut. On Hobbyist's 10 hours a month, that is two and a half episodes.
- **Two separate currencies.** Media minutes cover transcription and recording; AI credits cover generative features. You can run out of one while sitting on a pile of the other, and neither rolls over month to month. Top-ups are available, which is the polite way of saying overage.
- **The November 2025 re-metering raised bills.** Descript moved to the media-minutes model and legacy users reported effective increases around 26%. If you are reading an older comparison, its Descript numbers are wrong.
- **Heavy projects strain the app.** Descript is a browser-era editor doing NLE work; long multicam projects get sluggish in a way Premiere or Resolve do not.

Descript's annual plans do come with one-time bonus top-ups (500 AI credits and 5 media hours on Creator; 1,000 and 10 on Business), which softens the first month but not the recurring math.

## 3. Adobe Podcast Enhance — the free tool that saves bad recordings

**Best for:** rescuing a guest track recorded on a laptop mic in a kitchen
**Price:** Free (30-min files, 500 MB, 1 hour/day, audio only) · Premium $9.99/mo or $99.99/yr (2-hour files, 1 GB, 4 hours/day, video support, batch uploads, enhancement strength slider); 30-day trial

Adobe Podcast is the highest-value item in this entire roundup because the free tier does the one job most podcasters cannot do themselves. Drop in a hollow, echoey, air-conditioner-hum recording and it comes back sounding like a microphone in a treated room. No plugins, no DAW, no chain of iZotope modules. Premium at $9.99/mo bundles Adobe Express Premium and has no annual discount worth waiting for — the $99.99/yr option is the same rate.

**Where it breaks**

- **The V2 model over-processes.** The consistent complaint across Reddit, Gearspace and Adobe's own community forums since the update: voices come back robotic or "text-to-speech"-like, audio gets muffled at clip heads and tails, and odd artifacts appear in silent passages. V1 was gentler; V2 prioritises noise removal over naturalness.
- **The strength slider is a paid feature**, which is precisely backwards — the users who most need to dial the processing down are the free-tier users hearing the artifacts.
- **It cannot handle multi-speaker single-track audio.** With two voices bleeding into one file, the algorithm produces distortion. Separate tracks at the recording stage remain the only real fix, which loops you back to Riverside or a local DAW.
- **Free tier limits are hard**: 30-minute files and one hour of processing per day means a weekly 45-minute episode does not fit without paying.

Use it as a repair tool, not a mastering chain. And process before you compress, never after.

## 4. Auphonic — the unglamorous one that makes you sound professional

**Best for:** consistent loudness, levelling and broadcast delivery specs
**Price:** Free 2 h/mo (with an Auphonic jingle, no speech recognition, multitrack capped at 20 min) · Recurring credits: S ~$11/mo annual for 9 h, M ~$25 for 21 h, L ~$49 for 45 h, XL ~$99 for 100 h, XXL for 250 h · One-time credits from ~$12 for 5 h, down to about $0.98/hour at volume

Auphonic is twenty years of audio engineering compressed into an upload box, and it is used by the BBC and iHeart precisely because it is boring. Loudness normalisation to broadcast targets, adaptive levelling across speakers of wildly different volumes, noise and reverb reduction, silence cutting, multitrack mixdown, chapter marks and metadata written into the file. If your co-host is always quieter than you and you fix it manually every week, Auphonic ends that chore permanently.

**Where it breaks**

- **Recurring credits reset monthly with zero rollover.** Skip two weeks of publishing and those hours evaporate. One-time credits never expire, which is why the smart configuration is a small recurring plan plus a one-time buffer — recurring burns first, one-time sits underneath.
- **Billing counts every file you send, not the episode you ship.** Add a 1-minute intro to a 20-minute episode and you are charged 21 minutes. Minimum billing is 3 minutes per production.
- **The free tier brands your output** with an Auphonic jingle at the head and tail and locks speech recognition, show notes, watch folders and batch processing. It is a genuine 2-hour-a-month utility, not a free plan you can build a weekly show on.
- **It does not edit.** No cuts, no text-based workflow, no video. It is the last step before upload and nothing else.

## 5. ElevenLabs — voice synthesis, dubbing and the credit meter

**Best for:** narration, translated versions of your show, intros and ad reads at scale
**Price:** Free 10,000 credits (non-commercial, attribution required) · Starter $6/mo ($5 annual, 30,000 credits) · Creator $22 ($11 first month, $18.33 annual, 121,000 credits) · Pro $99 (600,000) · Scale $299 (1.8 M, 3 seats) · Business $990 (6 M, 10 seats)

ElevenLabs is the only tool here that makes audio rather than fixing it. For podcasters the practical uses are narration of scripted segments, voice cloning for pickup lines you forgot to record, and dubbing an episode into other languages without re-recording. Quality at the top of the range is no longer the argument; pricing is.

Roughly 826–915 credits buy a finished minute of narration, so Starter is about 33–36 minutes a month, Creator about 2.4 hours, Pro about 12 hours. Annual billing is ten months' price for twelve, and unused paid credits roll over up to two months' worth (maximum balance three times your monthly quota). The free tier does not roll over and is not licensed for commercial use — and upgrading later does not retroactively license audio you already generated.

**Where it breaks**

- **Retakes rebill the full text.** Generate a 400-word script, dislike the delivery, regenerate — you paid twice. Budget accordingly; the sticker tier is not the spend.
- **A second, separate pay-as-you-go price list arrived on 7 May 2026**, so "what does ElevenLabs cost" now has two answers depending on how you consume it. Top-up credits expire 12 months after purchase.
- **Support and upgrade UX draw sustained complaints.** Reddit threads through mid-2026 describe upsell pop-ups that fire mid-click and route straight to payment without a confirmation screen, accounts locked behind unwanted invoices, and support emails going unanswered for weeks.
- **Downgrading or cancelling forfeits rollover credits** at the end of the cycle.

We were previously in the ElevenLabs affiliate programme through PartnerStack; that programme is frozen, so this is an independent assessment with nothing riding on it.

## 6. Buzzsprout — hosting with the AI bolted on where it belongs

**Best for:** podcasters who want show notes, chapters and social posts generated at upload
**Price:** Audio plans from $19/mo (monthly billing) with annual discounts; Buzzsprout's pricing page currently advertises annual-equivalent rates of $15/mo audio, $25/mo audio+video and $30/mo multi-podcast · Cohost AI add-on $5/mo on the $19 plan, $10/mo on the $39 plan, $20/mo on the $79 plan · Magic Mastering available as a separate add-on

Cohost AI is the right shape for a podcast AI feature: it triggers on upload, and by the time your episode has processed you have title suggestions, a drafted description, chapter markers, a blog post, three social posts and a transcript waiting for edit. No separate tool, no copy-paste, no second subscription. For a solo podcaster the twenty minutes a week this saves is worth more than most $30 tools.

**Where it breaks**

- **The AI is an add-on, and it scales with your plan, not your usage.** Bigger hosting plan, bigger Cohost bill — $5, $10 or $20 a month — even if you publish the same number of episodes.
- **Upload hours are the real cap**: 72 hours a year on standard plans, 180 on multi-podcast. Video shows chew through that.
- **Buzzsprout's own pages quote different numbers** depending on whether you land on the pricing page (which shows annual-equivalent rates) or the Cohost help article (which quotes the $19/$39/$79 monthly ladder). Check the billing toggle before you conclude you are getting a deal.
- **Disabling Cohost bills one more time.** It is charged at the end of the cycle, so turning it off still produces a final charge.

Captivate is the natural alternative if download caps matter more than AI: unlimited podcasts and storage on every tier, priced by downloads — its pricing page shows $17/$44/$90 per month paid yearly, while its help centre documents $19/$49/$99 monthly ($204/$528/$1,080 annual). Same story: verify against the live billing toggle.

## 7. Alitu — one subscription instead of five

**Best for:** solo podcasters who want to stop evaluating software
**Price:** $38/mo, $32/mo billed annually ($380/yr); GBP £32/£320. Second show at a $13/mo discount. Hosting included free to 1,000 downloads/mo, $10/mo to 10,000. 7-day trial, 30-day money-back guarantee

Alitu is the anti-stack. Record or import, and it automatically cleans and levels the audio, builds the episode from segments, adds your intro and outro with automatic fades, generates transcripts and show notes, and publishes to a built-in host. Every feature is on every plan; there is one tier. For someone who wants a podcast rather than a hobby in audio engineering, that is a genuinely defensible $380 a year.

**Where it breaks**

- **It is expensive against its own components.** Adobe Enhance (free) plus Auphonic S (~$11) plus Buzzsprout with Cohost ($24) comes to roughly $35/mo with more control at each step. You are paying Alitu for the integration, not the capability.
- **Editing is deliberately shallow.** No multitrack precision work, no text-based editing at Descript's level, no video-first workflow. That is the design, but it becomes a ceiling fast if your show gets ambitious.
- **The free hosting tier is small.** 1,000 downloads a month is a starter show; past that it is $10/mo on top.
- **Single price, no cheaper entry.** There is no $12 tier for a monthly show, so light publishers subsidise heavy ones.

## A note on Podcastle, and on cheap all-in-ones generally

Podcastle looks like Alitu's budget rival — Free (1 hour recording, 3 hours transcription), Storyteller $11.99/mo, Pro $23.99/mo with unlimited transcription and API access — and we cannot recommend it. The complaint pattern is too consistent and too severe: lost recording sessions and failed uploads reported as false completions, AI cleanup that degrades voices, no pause button during recording, Chrome-only reliability, no video upload (in-platform capture only), and a refund policy that grants nothing once an annual trial converts. Users in late 2025 also reported plan terms changing under them, with recording limits and storage cut sharply on existing subscriptions.

The broader lesson applies to every cheap all-in-one: the recording step is the only irreversible one. Save $12 a month on editing and you lose time. Save $12 a month on recording and you lose an interview you cannot get back.

## What we would actually buy

**Audio-only solo show, weekly, 30–45 minutes — $24/mo.** Record locally in Audacity or on your phone. Repair problem tracks in Adobe Enhance (free). Master in Auphonic S (~$11/mo annual). Host on Buzzsprout $19/mo — or drop Auphonic and add Cohost AI at $5. Total: $24–30/mo, around $300 a year, and it will sound better than most $100/mo stacks because the money went to levelling instead of features.

**Remote interview show that publishes video — $53/mo.** Riverside Pro ($24/mo annual) for capture, Descript Creator ($24/mo annual) for the edit, Adobe Enhance free for guest track rescue, and a free host to start. Total $48–53/mo, roughly $600 a year. Add a local backup recorder on every session; it costs nothing and it is the highest-return decision on this page.

**Narration or multilingual show — $46/mo.** ElevenLabs Creator ($22, or $18.33 annual) for voice and dubbing, Auphonic S (~$11) for consistent output loudness, Buzzsprout $19 with Cohost. Write scripts tightly — every retake is billed again.

**"I just want to press one button" — $32/mo.** Alitu annual, and stop reading comparison articles. That is a legitimate purchase, not a compromise, as long as you accept the editing ceiling.

## Bottom line

The AI in these tools is now commodity. What you are really buying in 2026 is a metering model, and the three models behave completely differently under pressure: Riverside and Buzzsprout charge for input hours, Descript and ElevenLabs charge for processing you may waste, and Alitu charges a flat rate that only pays off if you publish consistently. Match the meter to your publishing rhythm and the tool choice mostly makes itself.

If you take one thing from this page, take the recording-layer rule. Every tool here can be swapped next month except the one that captured the conversation. Spend there, back it up locally, and be cheap everywhere else.
