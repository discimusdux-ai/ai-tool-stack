Fliki and Synthesia show up in the same "AI video generator" lists all the time, but they make different kinds of video. Synthesia sells you a presenter: a photoreal avatar that delivers your script to camera in 140+ languages. Fliki sells you a narrator: a voice laid over stock footage, AI images and captions, made from a script, a blog post or a slide deck.

So the first question isn't which tool is better. It's this: **does your video need a face?**

If a person on screen is what makes the video work (onboarding, compliance training, a CEO update, a product walkthrough that has to feel human), you're shopping for Synthesia or something like it. If your viewer only needs to hear a clear voice and see relevant visuals (faceless YouTube, social explainers, blog-to-video, audiobook-style content), Fliki does the job for less money and turns out far more minutes per dollar.

We checked both pricing pages and help centers on October 16, 2026. That timing matters: **Synthesia rebuilt its plans on October 1, 2026**. Creator became "Pro", every plan moved to a shared credit balance, and the free tier changed. Many comparisons ranking today still quote the old minute caps.

## The framing: presenter video vs. narrated video

These two products are built around different raw materials, and that shows up in everything from pricing to the complaints.

**Synthesia's expensive part is the avatar.** Its pitch is studio-filmed, licensed actors turned into avatars, now running on the Express-2 engine (announced August 2025), which adds hand and body gestures to lip-sync. Synthesia 3.0 (September 2025) added prompt-generated avatars and Veo 3 B-roll of your avatar doing things. Rendering a convincing human costs compute, so Synthesia meters video at about **100 credits per finished minute**.

**Fliki's expensive part is generated media, and only when you choose it.** Fliki's credit guide lists voiceover at 0.25–2 credits per minute depending on voice tier, and stock or uploaded media at **0 credits**. AI images cost 0.05–1.5 credits each, and AI video clips cost **0.1–6 credits per second**. A stock-footage explainer is almost free to render. The same budget spent on AI video clips runs out in seconds.

That gives you the practical rule. **With Synthesia you pay for the face. With Fliki you pay for whatever you generate instead of sourcing.**

### The meter is the price, not the sticker

Both tools now bill in credits. That makes a side-by-side "cost per minute" possible, and it's the number to decide on:

- **Synthesia Starter, monthly ($29):** 10 free minutes + 1,250 extra credits ≈ **22 finished minutes/month**, or about **$1.30 per minute**. That's only if you don't spend credits on dubbing (76/min), surveys (350/response) or interactive avatars, which all draw from the same balance.
- **Synthesia Pro, annual ($64/mo US):** ~720 extra minutes/year + 120 free = ~840 min/yr, about **$0.91 per minute**.
- **Fliki Standard, annual ($21/mo):** a 5-minute stock-footage video with a mid-tier voice costs about 2.5 credits. 180 credits a month works out to roughly **350 narrated minutes**, a few cents each. Fliki's own FAQ is more conservative: it estimates 60–90 cumulative minutes a month, because re-editing a script re-renders the audio and charges again.
- **Fliki with AI video clips:** at up to 6 credits per second, those same 180 credits can buy **30 seconds** of generated footage.

The re-metering timeline is worth knowing too, because it shows how quickly these deals change. Synthesia went from minute caps (February 2024) to new annual rates (April 2024), to geo-tiered tables (May and July 2026), to a full credit system (October 1, 2026). Under that last change, the old Creator plan went from 3,600 to 6,500 monthly credits at the same price, and the Basic tier was rebuilt around 500 credits. Fliki updated its credit and fair-use guide on October 15, 2026, and runs default AI video models by plan: Seedance Pro Fast at 720p on Basic, MiniMax on Standard and Premium. Treat any per-minute figure, including ours, as valid for the current quarter only.

## Where Fliki wins

**Throughput.** A stock-footage video narrated by an AI voice is Fliki's best use case, and it costs almost nothing. If you publish daily faceless Shorts or turn every blog post into a video, nothing in Synthesia's lineup comes close on price.

**Inputs.** Blog URL, idea prompt, PPT and tweet-to-video are all built in. For a content marketer with an archive, Fliki is a repurposing machine.

**Voices.** 1,000 voices on Standard and 2,000+ on Premium, with color-coded cost bands so you can see what a voice costs before you use it. Fliki also exports audio only, so one subscription covers voiceovers and podcasts.

**Price of entry.** $21/month (annual) gets commercial rights, 1080p, no watermark and a voice clone.

## Where Synthesia wins

**The human on screen.** Express-2 avatars gesture, and personal avatars let your real trainer or exec appear without being filmed again. If viewers need to feel they're being addressed by someone, Fliki's avatars don't compete. Its own reviewers say so.

**Training workflows.** Interactive CTAs, branching, quizzes, surveys, and Interactive Avatar "Sessions" that coach or interview learners, plus a Roleplay add-on at $25 per learner per month. Synthesia says it's used by over 90% of the Fortune 100.

**Localization.** Lip-synced dubbing with voice and accent preservation, and 140+ languages. For a global onboarding library, that is the product.

**The free tier.** Basic's 10 monthly minutes is enough to build a real draft. You can't download it, but you can judge it.

## Where Fliki breaks

- **AI visuals with gibberish text.** One Capterra review from 2026 says every generated video carried "gibberish or random pseudo-text artifacts" in the visuals, and complains about slow support. Use stock footage, or check every AI frame before you publish.
- **Avatars are weak.** Even positive Capterra reviewers call the blinking and lip movement "exceptionally unnatural" and avoid the avatars entirely.
- **Lifetime-deal customers got burned.** AppSumo reviews describe accounts dropping to free-tier limits after Fliki added AI features. One buyer said it left them with "about 4.30 seconds of video." Others report waiting months for support replies. Don't buy Fliki through a third-party lifetime deal.
- **Re-edits cost credits.** Changing a script re-renders the audio and charges again, which is why Fliki's own estimate (60–90 min/month on Standard) is far below the theoretical maximum.
- **Automation gaps.** Reviewers report Auto-pick media giving up partway through long videos, and blog-to-video summarizing even when you choose "entire post".

## Where Synthesia breaks

- **Content moderation overreach.** This is the most repeated complaint across G2, Trustpilot and reviews. Legitimate healthcare, biotech and pharma scripts get flagged before rendering, without clear criteria. Teams in regulated industries should test their real scripts on the free tier first.
- **Uncanny valley in high-stakes content.** G2 reviewers praise the polish but say avatars "lack the nuanced emotional range" for emotional or persuasive material. Avatar limitations draw about 350 mentions on G2, and HeyGen outscores Synthesia on G2's avatar-quality rating (9.2 vs 8.3, per checkthat.ai's G2 summary).
- **The good stuff is Enterprise-only.** SCORM export, unlimited minutes, SSO, brand kits and 1-click translation all need a custom quote. L&D teams report finding out about the SCORM gate after they'd already built content.
- **One shared balance.** Since October 1, dubbing, surveys, Interactive Avatars, B-roll and the API all draw from the same credits as video. The headline "~720 minutes" on Pro assumes you spend credits on nothing else.
- **Trustpilot is noticeably lower than G2.** Synthesia sits at 3.9 on Trustpilot (about 1,800 reviews) vs 4.6 on G2, with moderation and refund complaints concentrated on Trustpilot.

## Who should pick which

**Pick Fliki if** you're a solo creator, faceless YouTuber, blogger or social marketer. You need volume, your viewer doesn't need a face, and you want voiceover and video under one subscription. Start with Standard on annual billing, stay on stock footage, and treat AI clips as a garnish.

**Pick Synthesia if** you're an L&D, HR, enablement or internal-comms team. A presenter makes the content credible, you need translations that lip-sync, and the video lives in an LMS or a training portal. Budget for Pro, and get an Enterprise quote early if SCORM or SSO is non-negotiable.

**Pay for both if** you're a small agency or a SaaS marketing team that makes both kinds of video: Synthesia for onboarding and product-update presenters, Fliki for top-of-funnel social and blog repurposing. On US annual list prices, Fliki Standard ($252/yr) plus Synthesia Starter (about $216/yr) costs about **$470 a year**. That's less than three months of Fliki Premium plus Synthesia Pro on monthly billing ($177/mo).

**Skip both if** you already have footage and just need clips. A clipping tool like [OpusClip](/blog/opus-clip-review-2026) fits that job better.

## Bottom line

This choice is easier than most comparisons make it. **If the face is the product, buy Synthesia; if the voice is the product, buy Fliki.** Fliki gives creators far more finished minutes per dollar as long as they stick to stock media. Synthesia is the better tool for a company that needs a believable presenter in 140 languages, but its best features sit behind an Enterprise quote, and its new credit system means dubbing and surveys come out of your video minutes.

Whichever you choose, test with your real scripts on the free tier first. Fliki's weak spot is AI visuals and Synthesia's is moderation, and both show up within an afternoon of testing.
