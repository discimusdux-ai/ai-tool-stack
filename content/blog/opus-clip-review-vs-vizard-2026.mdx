OpusClip and Vizard are the two names that come up every time someone asks "what should I use to turn my podcast into Shorts?" On paper they are almost the same product. Both take a long video, find the moments worth posting, reframe them to 9:16, burn in animated captions, and push them to TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts. Both charge **1 credit per minute of source video you upload**. Both list their main paid plan at **$29 a month, or $14.50 a month billed yearly**.

So this isn't a features shootout. The question is simpler and more annoying: **when the sticker price and the meter are the same, what are you actually choosing between?**

Our answer, up front: Vizard hands you **twice the source minutes** for the same money. OpusClip earns its place with a cheaper entry rung, a stronger scoring and posting workflow, and a better-documented product. Which one wins depends on how many hours of raw footage you feed it each month.

## The framing: you're buying source hours, not clips

Every number in this comparison comes back to one fact: **both tools meter the input, not the output.** Upload a 60-minute interview and you spend 60 credits whether the AI finds twelve great clips or zero. OpusClip's help center states it plainly: "processing clips will cost 1 credit per minute of the original video imported." Vizard's pricing page says the same thing in different words: "Every minute of video you upload will cost 1 credit."

That makes the real comparison a question of **how many hours of raw footage you push through each month**:

- **Under 2.5 hours/month** — OpusClip Starter ($15, 150 credits) is the cheapest paid plan either tool offers.
- **2.5 to 5 hours/month** — OpusClip Pro (300 credits) and Vizard Creator (600 credits) cost the same. Vizard gives you headroom; OpusClip gives you its workflow.
- **5 to 10 hours/month** — Vizard Creator covers you at the base price. On OpusClip you're buying a second 300-credit Pro pack.
- **More than 10 hours/month** — Vizard's credit slider runs up to 6,200 credits a month; OpusClip stacks 300-credit packs (the pricing page shows up to 15) before you're pushed to Business.

Both tools also let you trim the processing window before you upload. OpusClip calls it the "Processing timeframe" slider. If you only want clips from the middle 40 minutes of a two-hour stream, use it. It's the single easiest way to stop paying for intros and dead air.

## Usage limits and the fine print

**Rollover.** Both now roll monthly credits for one extra cycle (60 days). On yearly plans, OpusClip credits last 12 months and Vizard credits last 13. Neither one refunds leftovers when you cancel. Vizard's upgrade doc adds a trap: **upgrading from Creator to Business does not carry unused Creator credits over**. Burn them first.

**Yearly is the real price.** Both companies discount annual billing about 50%. Paying monthly for either Pro/Creator tier means paying double. OpusClip's Starter is the exception: it's monthly-only, so a light user can stay flexible.

**Free tiers are trials.** Both cap at 60 credits and expire projects after 3 days. OpusClip's free exports are watermarked and its terms restrict free accounts to non-commercial use. Vizard's free tier exports at 720p and caps exports at 10 minutes. Neither free tier works as a long-term workflow.

**X posting costs extra on OpusClip.** Publishing to X costs 3 credits per clip per account. If X is a core channel, that adds up.

## Where each one wins on capability

**Clip selection.** This is the real product, and nobody wins it outright. ScaleReach's May 2026 test of Vizard across six videos found 62% of clips publishable as-is, the same figure it reported for OpusClip. The difference was in the score. In that test, clips with high Vizard scores averaged about **1.6×** the views of low-scored clips, compared with **2.3×** for OpusClip's Virality Score. Translation: both tools pick *usable* moments, but OpusClip's ranking does a better job telling you which ones to post first. That's one tester's sample, so treat it as a direction rather than a law.

**Reframing and speakers.** The same tester rated Vizard's speaker detection best-in-class for one- and two-person shows, with only 4 of 71 clips needing a re-crop and roughly 85% correct tracking on a four-person panel. OpusClip's answer is breadth: genre-specific reframing, screen-share and gameplay layouts, and moving-object tracking on Pro.

**Polish features.** OpusClip has more of them: AI B-roll, speech enhancement, filler-word cleanup, voice-cloned dubbing, auto-censoring, and export to Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve on Pro. If you finish clips in a real editor, that handoff alone can decide it.

**Output quality.** Vizard exports in 4K on every paid plan. OpusClip's matrix focuses on import size (10GB on Starter, 30GB on Pro) and 9:16 / 1:1 / 16:9 aspect ratios. For phone-first Shorts, 1080p is plenty. For repurposing to other surfaces, Vizard's 4K is a real advantage.

**Teams.** Vizard Business is a clean team plan: shared workspace, brand kit, 20 social accounts, and cheap per-seat add-ons. OpusClip Pro includes 2 seats, and anything bigger means a sales call for Business.

## Where it breaks

### OpusClip
- **You pay for footage, not results.** A messy recording costs the same whether it produces ten clips or none. One Trustpilot reviewer in November 2026 described uploading the same video three times to fix audio, paying credits each time, and getting no reply on a refund ticket for weeks.
- **Billing complaints repeat.** Trustpilot sat at 4.0/5 from 521 reviews on 11 November 2026, split 63% five-star and 20% one-star. The one-star themes: charges after cancelling, slow support, and constant upsell prompts to buy more credits. OpusClip says some of those reviews are about Agent Opus.
- **No refunds on paid fees** under its terms, and yearly credits expire at 12 months. One reviewer lost nearly 3,000 unused credits at renewal.
- **The manual editor is thin.** AI-picked clips usually need trimming, and reviewers call the editor buggy and limited.

### Vizard
- **Selection is "usable," not "best."** The most common critique on G2, Trustpilot and Reddit is that Vizard picks technically clean moments that lack payoff, like the setup without the punchline. A high score doesn't mean a clip will perform.
- **Credits are forfeited on cancellation**, and some Trustpilot reviewers say features that look free, like transcript export, turn out to be paywalled after upload.
- **Light editor, slow long renders.** Software Advice reviewers cite slow processing on long uploads, clips cut a second early or late, and no dynamic subtitle positioning between layouts.
- **Opaque pricing page.** Prices load via JavaScript, and the seat price conflicts between Vizard's help doc and third-party trackers. You only see what you'll pay at checkout.

## Who should pick which

**Pick OpusClip if:**
- You process **under 2.5 hours a month**. Starter at $15 is the cheapest paid plan in this matchup.
- You want the **ranking to do triage for you**. Virality Score is the better "post these first" signal.
- You finish clips in **Premiere or Resolve**, or want dubbing, B-roll and speech cleanup in one place.
- You're a solo creator or a duo (Pro's 2 seats).

**Pick Vizard if:**

- You need **4K exports** or run a **team** that wants a shared workspace and brand kit without a sales call.

## Bottom line

When two tools charge the same sticker price on the same meter, the gap is in **how many minutes that price buys you** and **how much work the AI saves after the clips exist**. Vizard wins on the first by a factor of two. OpusClip wins on the second: a cheaper entry plan, a ranking signal that holds up better, and a deeper finishing toolkit.

Do the math on your own footage. Add up last month's raw hours. Under 2.5, start on OpusClip Starter. Between 2.5 and 5, choose on workflow, since both cost the same. Above 5, Vizard's credit allowance is hard to argue with unless OpusClip's polish features would save you an editor's time.
