"Text to video" now covers three different products that happen to share a prompt box. One kind **assembles** a video from your script: voiceover, stock footage, captions. One kind puts a **presenter** on screen to read your words. One kind **generates** brand-new footage from a sentence, frame by frame. They cost different amounts, fail in different ways, and almost no list tells you which kind you're looking at.

So there is no single winner here. The useful question is this: **are you assembling, presenting, or generating?** Answer that first and the shortlist drops from dozens of tools to two or three.

We checked vendor pricing pages and help centers between October 16 and October 23, 2026, then read recent Trustpilot, Capterra and Reddit complaints for each tool. Video AI reprices constantly, so every price below carries a billing view (monthly or annual) and a date.

## The framing: assemble, present, or generate

**Assemble** tools take a script and build a video from existing parts: a synthetic voice, licensed stock clips, images, captions and music. Rendering is cheap because nothing is invented. The output looks like a competent explainer, which is exactly what most marketing needs. Fliki, Pictory and the stock-footage side of InVideo live here.

**Present** tools render a person. Synthesia and HeyGen turn text into an avatar speaking to camera. This is the right call when a face builds trust (onboarding, compliance, sales outreach), and the wrong call when viewers only need to hear a clear voice.

**Generate** tools invent footage from a prompt. Runway, Kling and Google's Veo models make shots that never existed. The result can be stunning, but you pay per second, you throw away takes, and a 60-second video is stitched from a dozen short clips that you have to keep visually consistent yourself.

The meter follows the category. Assemblers charge you for voice minutes. Presenters charge for finished minutes of avatar. Generators charge for every attempt, including the ones you discard. Keep that in mind when you read the price table.

## 1. Fliki: best for faceless video from a script

**Best for:** YouTube channels without a face, social explainers, blog and slide-deck repurposing.
**Price:** Free (3 credits/month, watermark) · Standard $28/mo monthly or $21/mo annual ($252/yr, ~180 credits/month) · Premium $88/mo monthly or $66/mo annual ($792/yr, ~600 credits/month) [Fliki pricing and credit guide, 2026-10-16].

Fliki is the assembler we'd start with. You paste a script, a blog URL or a presentation and it builds scenes: a voice from a library of 1,000+ (Standard) or 2,000+ (Premium), matched stock footage, captions and music. Its credit guide prices voiceover at 0.25-2 credits per minute depending on voice tier, and stock or uploaded media at **0 credits**. A narrated stock-footage video costs a few credits and almost no money.

The meter bites when you choose generated media. AI images cost 0.05-1.5 credits each and AI video clips run **0.1-6 credits per second**. The same 180 monthly credits that cover hundreds of narrated minutes buy about 30 seconds of premium generated footage.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Re-edits cost credits.** Changing a script re-renders the audio and charges again. Fliki's own FAQ estimates 60-90 cumulative minutes a month on Standard, far below the theoretical maximum.
- **AI visuals show artifacts.** The generated-image and clip layer is the weakest part; stock footage matched to your script is hit-and-miss and you'll swap clips by hand.
- **Avatars are the weak spot.** If you need a convincing presenter, Fliki isn't the tool.
- **The free tier is a taste, not a trial.** Three credits barely cover one short video.

## 2. Synthesia: best for presenter-led training

**Best for:** L&D teams, onboarding, compliance, multilingual internal comms.
**Price:** Basic $0 (10 free video minutes/month + 500 credits, no downloads) · Starter $29/mo monthly, about $18/mo annual in the US (1,250 credits/mo on top of the free minutes) · Pro $89/mo, about $64/mo annual (6,000 credits/mo) · Enterprise custom [Synthesia pricing and help center, 2026-10-16].

Synthesia rebuilt its plans on **October 1, 2026**: Creator became Pro and every plan moved to a single credit balance, at roughly 100 credits per finished video minute, 76 per dubbed minute and 350 per survey. Express-2 avatars add hand and body gestures to lip-sync, and the library runs from 9 avatars on Basic to 240+ on Enterprise.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The good features are Enterprise-only.** SCORM export, unlimited minutes, SSO and one-click translation need a quote. L&D buyers report finding the SCORM gate after they've built content.
- **One shared balance.** Dubbing, surveys and interactive avatars all draw from the same credits as video. The headline minute count assumes you spend nothing else.
- **Prices are geo-tiered.** Our sandbox browser saw Starter at $14/mo and Pro at $59/mo billed yearly; the US list is $29/$18 and $89/$64. Check your own billing page before budgeting.
- **Content moderation flags.** Legitimate scripts get blocked and you wait on support.

## 3. HeyGen: best for marketing avatars and digital twins

**Best for:** sales outreach video, localized ads, creators who want to clone themselves.
**Price:** Free (3 videos/month, 1 minute each, watermark) · Creator $29/mo, $24/mo annual (600 credits, 1080p, Avatar III and IV, limited Avatar V) · Pro from $49/mo (1,000 credits, 4K) · Business $149/mo plus $20 per extra seat [HeyGen pricing and FAQ, 2026-10-22].

HeyGen is the main rival to Synthesia and, for many marketers, the more flexible one. Avatar IV and the newer Avatar V, a Video Agent that drafts a whole video from a prompt, and a custom avatar of yourself are all on the Creator plan. Plans moved to credits in May 2026, and HeyGen says Creator now has three times the monthly credits it did before.

**Where it breaks:**
- **"Unlimited" used to mean less than it sounded.** Reviewers on Trustpilot and Reddit describe the pre-credit Creator plan as unlimited only for older Avatar III and audio dubbing, with Avatar IV and 4K metered. One April 2026 reviewer called it "a total bait-and-switch".
- **Credits can burn on failed jobs.** Several reviews report credits consumed with no usable output. We could not verify the rate; treat it as a reported risk.
- **Support is the repeated complaint.** G2 scores are high (4.8 from roughly 1,500 reviews per one review site), but Trustpilot has far more billing and cancellation complaints. Read both.
- **Mobile and web plans can differ.** HeyGen's own help page says mobile may show a different plan type than web.

## 4. InVideo: best for "just make it from one prompt"

**Best for:** founders and social marketers who want an agent to do the first draft and an editor to fix it.
**Price:** Starter shown at $10 per seat/mo billed annually (first-time buyers, 50% off a $20 list), 400 credits/month · higher tiers scale credits and model access [invideo.io/pricing, 2026-10-23].

InVideo's pitch is a chat-style agent: describe the video, it writes the script, picks media and renders, then you edit on a full timeline with color, audio and 3D tools. Credits cover agent processing and image, video, voice and music generation, and the newest video models (Seedance 2.x) are gated to higher plans; Starter gets lighter agent access.

**Pricing conflict worth knowing:** UsagePricing logged an August 14, 2026 snapshot with four paid tiers (Plus $17/mo, Max $85, Generative $170, Elite $900, all billed annually) and no free plan, while the live page we read led with a $10 Starter. InVideo is changing its ladder, so confirm the plan name and credits before you pay.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Credits vanish on prep work.** Recent Capterra and PissedConsumer reviews describe the agent spending a month's credits on internal steps and clarifying loops with no finished video, and support citing "background operations".
- **Failed attempts can void refunds.** One Software Advice reviewer said generating without exporting made the account ineligible for a refund.
- **Upsell pressure.** Multiple reviewers report being pushed from a $20 plan to a $100 package within one project.
- **Support reachability.** Trustpilot praises support staff, while PissedConsumer filings say there's no phone line.

## 5. Pictory: best for repurposing long content

**Best for:** turning webinars, blog posts and long videos into short branded clips.
**Price:** Starter from $25/mo (Pictory shows $29 crossed out), 1 user, all AI models; Capterra lists $29 flat monthly with 200 video minutes, and Professional at $59 with 600 minutes; Teams from $119 [pictory.ai/pricing and Capterra, 2026-10-23].

Pictory is a script-to-scenes assembler with strong repurposing: feed it a URL or a recording and it highlights, captions and stocks scenes from Getty Images and Storyblocks libraries, with ElevenLabs voices. It's less flashy than the avatar tools and more predictable.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Plan pages disagree.** The live page, the embedded plan table and Capterra show different Starter prices and quotas. We could not reconcile them.
- **Looks like a template.** Scene matching is serviceable, but videos from Pictory share a visible house style.
- **Length limits.** The plan table caps videos at 30 minutes and the free trial at 5 minutes and 3 videos.
- **Generative features are thinner** than Runway, InVideo or Kling.

## 6. Runway: best for cinematic generation and control

**Best for:** filmmakers, ad creatives, anyone who needs shots that don't exist in stock libraries.
**Price:** Free (one-time 125 credits) · Standard $15/mo monthly or $12 annual (625 credits ≈ 52 seconds of Gen-4.5) · Pro $35 or $28 annual (2,250 credits ≈ 187 seconds) · Max $95 or $76 annual (9,500 credits) [runway.com/pricing and help center, 2026-10-23].

Runway puts several frontier models (Gen-4.5, Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.5, Nano Banana Pro) behind one credit pool, plus editing tools, lip-sync and custom voices. This is the tool with the most direct control over camera and motion.

**The meter is the price.** Standard's 625 credits are under a minute of top-model video. Runway is also replacing its Unlimited plan with Max: new subscribers got Max from May 29, 2026, and legacy Unlimited users move automatically on **November 30, 2026** after an extension announced August 27. The company says the shift buys speed with predictable pricing; to unlimited-plan heavy users it means a hard credit ceiling.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Seconds, not minutes.** A finished two-minute video can eat most of a Pro month before you count rejected takes.
- **Consistency is your job.** Characters and scenes drift between clips.
- **Credits are a pool shared across models,** so trying the pricier models drains the balance fast.
- **Unlimited is ending** for legacy subscribers on the date above.

## 7. Kling: best value for generative clips

**Best for:** experimenting with realistic generative video without a Runway-sized bill.
**Price:** Free credits · Standard $10 list, $8.80 renewal, $6.99 first month, 660 credits/mo · Pro $37 list, $32.56 renewal, 3,000 credits/mo · Premier $92-$428 (8,000-38,000 credits) [FluxGravity and Polymorf pricing guides, 2026-09-20/10-18; verify on klingai.com].

Kling models routinely rank near the top of generative-video comparisons, and its entry plan is the lowest serious price among the big generators. It also sits inside Runway's model menu, which tells you where it ranks.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Billing complaints.** Trustpilot's summary of 198 reviews (October 12, 2026) says most reviewers were let down, citing unexpected charges, renewal problems and refund trouble after buying credit packages.
- **You pay for throwaways.** One review roundup notes failed generations are refunded but disliked clips are not.
- **Peak-hour failures.** A pricing review reports 30-40% failure rates at peak, based on user reports we couldn't confirm.
- **Renewal prices jump after month one,** and credits expire unused.

## Sidebar: Google Flow and Veo

Google's Veo 3.1 model is available inside Flow for **Google AI Pro ($19.99/mo, 1,000 AI credits)** and Ultra (25,000 credits) subscribers, with top-ups valid for 12 months and monthly credits that don't roll over [Google One and Flow help, 2026-10-23]. It's the cheapest way to add a generative clip if you already pay for Google AI, but it's a clip generator, not a video builder. ## The meter is the price, not the sticker

Every tool here has re-metered in the past year. A few dated receipts:

- **Synthesia:** plans rebuilt around credits on 2026-10-01.
- **HeyGen:** credit-based plans from May 2026, with Creator credits tripled.
- **Runway:** Max replaced Unlimited for new buyers on 2026-05-29; legacy Unlimited ends 2026-11-30.
- **InVideo:** tier names and credit counts differ between its live page and an August 2026 snapshot.
- **Fliki:** credit and fair-use guide updated 2026-10-15.

The practical rule: price the **finished minute**, not the plan. For assemblers that's pennies. For presenters it's roughly a dollar. For generators it can be several dollars once you count rejected takes.

## Bottom line

For most people reading this, the right answer is an assembler. It's the cheapest category, the least risky to budget and the one that produces a finished video from a script. Pick a presenter tool when a face is the point, and a generator when you need footage that doesn't exist. Whatever you pick, run one real script through the free tier and price the finished minute before you commit to a year.
