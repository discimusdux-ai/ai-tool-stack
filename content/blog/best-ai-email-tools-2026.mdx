Yesterday, an AI email client that thousands of people had built their working day around stopped existing. Notion Mail closed on September 22, 2026 across web, desktop and iOS, on every plan. Mail-only data — custom views, AI auto-label instructions, snippets, reminders — was permanently deleted unless users exported it by September 21. The messages themselves survived, because they always lived in Gmail. Everything users built *on top* of Gmail did not.

That is the most useful thing that happened in this category all year, and it should change how you shop. There is no single best AI email tool, because the seven serious options are not the same kind of product. Two of them replace your email client. Three of them sit on top of the inbox you already have. One of them is the inbox you already have. And one of them does not write a single word for you.

This is not a roundup of whose AI writes the nicest reply. It is a roundup of what each one costs, what its meter is, and what you lose on the day you stop paying.

## How we picked

We included tools that do real AI work on a personal or team inbox — triage, drafting, search, summarization — and excluded email *marketing* platforms, which are a different purchase entirely (we cover those separately). All pricing was read from vendor pricing pages and help documentation in September 2026, with the billing view noted, because most of these vendors show a monthly-equivalent number that you cannot actually pay monthly. Cons come from vendor docs, Trustpilot, G2 and user forums, not from feature checklists.

We have no affiliate relationship with any of the seven tools below. Our Grammarly-side PartnerStack program — relevant now that Grammarly *is* Superhuman — has been frozen for months. Everything here is reviewed independently.

## The question that actually decides this: new inbox, or new layer?

Every comparison in this category ranks draft quality. Draft quality is the least durable thing you can buy here, because every vendor is renting the same three or four frontier models and swapping them out quarterly. Shortwave tells you outright that its intelligence levels map to Claude Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6. Whoever writes the best reply this quarter will not necessarily write it next quarter.

What is durable is architecture. The category has split into three:

**Client replacements** (Shortwave, Superhuman Mail, Spark) hand you a new application. Your mail still lives in Gmail or Outlook, but your interface, shortcuts, triage habits and AI rules live in the vendor. The AI is better here, because the vendor controls the whole surface. The exit cost is also highest: cancel and you are back in stock Gmail with your muscle memory gone and your custom views deleted. Notion Mail is the extreme version of that risk — the vendor can make the decision for you.

**Overlays** (Fyxer AI) leave Gmail or Outlook exactly where it is and add drafts, categorization and meeting notes around it. Setup takes minutes, cancellation takes minutes, and nothing about your daily interface changes. You pay for that in depth: an overlay sees threads, not your whole working context.

**Filters** (SaneBox) never write anything. They decide what deserves your attention and move the rest. It is the oldest architecture here, the cheapest, and the only one that makes your inbox *smaller* rather than making you faster at the same volume.

Ask which of those three you want before you compare a single feature. If the answer is "a layer, not a new app," four of the seven tools below are immediately irrelevant to you and you have just saved yourself $300 a year and a migration.

## 1. Shortwave — the best AI on a Gmail inbox

**Best for:** people whose entire job is email, on Google Workspace, who want AI that can actually reason over years of history.
**Price:** Business $30/seat/mo, Premier $45, Max $120, all billed monthly. 14-day trial on every tier. A free plan exists for individual use.

Shortwave is the one tool here that treats your mail archive as a database an agent can query. Natural-language search ("what did legal say about the Kirkland renewal") returns answers, not just matching threads, and the assistant drafts in a voice model built from how you actually write. In practitioner testing across this category, it is consistently the tool that produces output you can send without rewriting.

It is also the most honest about its meter, which we appreciate and which you should read carefully. The AI runs on a **daily quota**, not a monthly one. Business is documented at roughly 150-300 Standard-intelligence requests per day. Premier gives 2x the usage, Max 6x. The four intelligence levels cost different amounts of quota: Basic (Haiku 4.5) is free and does not count, Standard (Sonnet 4.6) is moderate, Advanced (Sonnet 4.6 with adaptive thinking) is high, Expert (Opus 4.6 with adaptive thinking) is very high.

**Where it breaks**

- **You cannot buy more quota.** Shortwave's own docs say it plainly: the quota resets every 24 hours and there is currently no way to purchase additional quota or top off mid-day. Hit the wall at 2pm on a Tuesday and your assistant is done until tomorrow.
- **Long conversations are expensive in a way people don't expect.** Each message reprocesses the full chat history, so one long thread with the assistant can burn more quota than several short ones. That is a usage pattern you have to learn, not a setting you can configure.
- **The tiers gate context, not just volume.** Business caps AI search at 50 threads per search and 5 years of AI search history; Premier gets 100 threads and unlimited history; Max gets 150. If your value case is "search my whole archive," the entry tier is not the product you were sold.
- **Gmail only.** No Outlook. That alone disqualifies it for a large share of businesses.

## 2. Superhuman Mail — fastest client here, and the only real Outlook option at the top tier

**Best for:** founders, salespeople and executives who live in the inbox and can honestly say speed is worth $300-400 a year.
**Price:** Starter $25/member/mo billed annually ($30 monthly); Business $33 annually ($40 monthly); Enterprise custom. No free tier; 30-day trial. Student and nonprofit discounts by request.

The speed is real, not marketing. Keyboard-first triage, split inbox, snippets, read statuses and follow-up reminders remain the most polished package in email, and Superhuman is now the only premium client here with credible support for both Gmail and Outlook. Starter includes Superhuman AI; Business adds the stronger model, Auto Drafts, Ask AI, custom AI auto-labels, an MCP server, and HubSpot/Salesforce integration.

Note the corporate plot twist: Grammarly acquired Superhuman in July 2025, then renamed *itself* Superhuman on October 29, 2025. The mail app is now one of four products in a suite alongside Grammarly, Coda and a cross-app assistant called Superhuman Go. Suite pricing is a separate ladder — Go is Free/$12/$33 per member per month on annual billing — and the help center confirms you cannot upgrade an existing Coda workspace into a bundle plan; you get a new workspace.

**Where it breaks**

- **The price never stops, and the exclusives are gone.** $300 to $396 per person per year, forever, for an email client. In 2019 nothing else had one-key triage, split inbox and read statuses on Gmail. In 2026, free Gmail has snooze, schedule send, smart compose and Gemini summaries, and extensions add most of the rest.
- **It makes you faster, not less busy.** The sharpest recurring criticism is that Superhuman speeds up decisions without reducing them. You process 150 emails faster; you still process 150 emails. Shortwave and Fyxer at least attempt to remove decisions.
- **You have to move house, and it's rented muscle memory.** New UI, new shortcuts, a real per-employee onboarding cost. Cancel after a year and you are back in a Gmail whose shortcuts you've forgotten.
- **No free tier at all**, which makes team rollouts an all-or-nothing budget conversation.

## 3. Gemini in Gmail — the AI you have probably already bought

**Best for:** any Google Workspace organization that hasn't checked what it's already entitled to.
**Price:** included in Workspace Business plans. Business Standard lists at $14/user/mo, with a $9.80 introductory rate for new customers, first 20 users, 12 months only.

This is the most under-used option in the category, for one reason: Google used to charge $20+ per user per month for Gemini in Gmail as an add-on, and in January 2025 folded it into Business and Enterprise editions instead, retiring the Gemini Business and Gemini Enterprise add-on SKUs. A lot of teams evaluating a $30/seat AI email tool are already paying for AI in Gmail and don't know it. Summarize a thread, find things in natural language, "help me write" a reply — it is all in the side panel on Business tiers.

**Where it breaks**

- **"Included" does not mean unmetered, and the caps are being switched on right now.** Google publishes per-feature monthly allowances by edition — and its own documentation notes that several features are in "promotional access," with limits enforcement beginning September 15, 2026 for Slides generation and October 1, 2026 for Workspace Studio. Free-feeling features are becoming counted features on a schedule.
- **Starter is a different product.** Business Starter gets Gemini in Gmail but is marked "no access" on a long list of AI features that Standard and Plus get, and only basic access to the Gemini app.
- **The upsell is a second subscription.** Want higher ceilings — image, video, translation, bigger monthly caps — and you are buying the AI Expanded Access add-on on top of your Workspace seats.
- **It is assistance, not triage.** Gemini will summarize and draft on request. It will not sort your inbox, learn your reply patterns, or clear clutter while you sleep. That gap is the entire business model of tools 1, 2 and 5.

## 4. Microsoft 365 Copilot in Outlook — two products, two very different prices

**Best for:** organizations already standardized on Microsoft 365, where the AI purchase is a licensing decision rather than a tool decision.
**Price:** Microsoft 365 Copilot Business **$21/user/mo paid yearly** (promoted at $18 for the first year on annual plans purchased by December 31, 2026), up to 300 users; Microsoft 365 Copilot enterprise **$30/user/mo paid yearly** ($31.50 monthly on an annual commitment). Bundled SKUs: Business Standard with Copilot $23.50, Business Premium with Copilot $32, both paid yearly. Copilot Chat is $0 with eligible Microsoft 365 subscriptions.

Most write-ups still print "$30 per user per month" as if it were the only number. It isn't. Microsoft now sells Copilot as two products at two prices, split by headcount at 300 users, and the add-on requires a qualifying Microsoft 365 subscription underneath — so the real bill is the suite plus the assistant.

The genuinely important detail for email buyers: since **April 15, 2026**, users without a paid Copilot license no longer see Copilot embedded in Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OneNote, but **Copilot in Outlook remains available to all Copilot Chat users** — summarizing mail, answering questions about your inbox and calendar, surfacing meeting context. If email is your only use case, the free tier may be most of what you wanted.

**Where it breaks**

- **A real governance failure, not a hypothetical one.** For weeks in early 2026, Copilot read, summarized and surfaced emails that organizations had labeled confidential. The bug — CW1226324, reported by customers January 21, 2026, with a fix only beginning to roll out in early February and remediation still incomplete in mid-February — let Copilot Chat's Work tab pull from Sent Items and Drafts despite sensitivity labels and DLP rules configured to block AI processing. The UK's NHS flagged it internally. Microsoft has not said how many organizations were affected. Every control that should have prevented it lived inside the same platform as the AI.
- **You cannot buy it standalone.** No qualifying M365 subscription, no Copilot.
- **The 300-user ceiling is enforced.** Microsoft's own FAQ says it can enforce a 300-license limit across the business plan family; above that you are on the $30 enterprise add-on, with volume discounts typically reserved for 250+ license enterprise agreements.
- **No free trial** on the paid tiers, which makes "does this justify $21 a seat" a question you answer after purchasing.

## 5. Fyxer AI — the overlay pick

**Best for:** people who will not switch email clients but want drafts, sorting and meeting notes in one subscription.
**Price:** Standard $22.50/user/mo billed annually ($30 monthly); Professional $37.50 annually ($50 monthly); Enterprise bespoke, minimum 50 users. 7-day trial.

Fyxer is the architectural outlier and the reason it's on this list: Gmail or Outlook stays exactly where it is. Fyxer organizes multiple inboxes, drafts replies in your voice, sends a notetaker into meetings, schedules across time zones, and answers questions from your inbox and meeting notes. Setup is the fastest of any paid tool in the category because there is nothing to migrate. Outlook support is a real differentiator against Shortwave.

**Where it breaks**

- **Sorting accuracy is the most common complaint, and it's the core feature.** Reviewers repeatedly report important mail landing in the wrong folders — one Trustpilot reviewer asked whether the app was "vibe-coded... it would explain why their main feature doesn't even work." If auto-sort misfires, you spend more time fishing mail out than the tool saves.
- **Billing complaints are specific and serious.** Multiple Trustpilot reviewers report being charged after cancelling during the trial, including one describing a $450 charge followed by a $900 charge with unanswered support emails and a card dispute. If you trial Fyxer, get cancellation confirmed in writing and watch the card.
- **Draft tone is competent but generic.** Drafts lean on the current thread rather than a per-contact model of how you write, which is exactly where Shortwave's voice matching pulls ahead.
- **No dedicated mobile app**, so on your phone you're in native Gmail or Outlook without the Fyxer experience. Gmail and Outlook only — no iCloud, Yahoo or IMAP. And 7 days is a tight trial for a tool that needs time to learn your inbox.

## 6. SaneBox — the only tool here that makes the inbox smaller

**Best for:** anyone whose problem is volume rather than writing speed, and anyone who wants AI email help without an AI reading and drafting on their behalf.
**Price:** Snack $9.49/mo, $65/year, or $109 for two years — 1 email account, choose 2 features. Lunch $15.99/mo, $109/year, $179/two years — 2 accounts, 6 features each. Dinner $42.99/mo, $319/year, $529/two years — 4 accounts, all features.

SaneBox predates the AI boom and is the cheapest genuinely useful thing in this roundup. It works at the server level on any provider, in whatever client you already use, and it never drafts a word. Mail you don't need goes to SaneLater, senders you never want to hear from again go to the Black Hole, and your inbox gets quieter. For a large share of people complaining about email, that is the actual fix.

**Where it breaks**

- **The pricing page rewards commitment and obscures the total.** Its default view shows two-year monthly equivalents ($4.54 / $7.46 / $22.04), which are comparison rates, not installments — you are charged $109, $179 or $529 up front. Read the checkout total, not the headline.
- **Tiers are metered in features, which is unusual and easy to get wrong.** Snack lets you pick *two* features for *one* account. People buy the cheap plan, then discover the specific filter they wanted is a third choice.
- **Refunds are not automatic and the trial length is inconsistent.** Cancelling does not refund the unused portion; the 30-day money-back guarantee has to be requested from support as a separate action. Meanwhile the site advertises a 14-day trial while a help article still says seven days.
- **Removal frustration is a recurring theme.** Trustpilot includes complaints about how hard it feels to fully unwind SaneBox once its folders are threaded through an account, including one user whose account was cancelled before they could export their training data.

## 7. Spark Mail — the cheap client where AI is opt-in

**Best for:** individuals and small teams who want a good modern client first and AI second, on a real free tier.
**Price:** Free; Plus $10/user/mo or $99/year (≈$8.25/mo); Pro $20/user/mo or $199/year (≈$16.58/mo).

Spark is the value play: a polished cross-platform client with a functioning free tier, where Plus adds the AI assistant, custom templates and 40 AI meeting notes, and Pro adds unlimited meeting notes, read statuses, HubSpot and a CLI. Crucially, **Spark +AI is disabled by default** and controlled in settings — the only tool here that treats AI as something you switch on rather than something that arrives already reading your mail.

**Where it breaks**

- **The Pro feature list sells the future.** Workflows and Auto-Labels/Auto-Drafts are both marked "SOON" on the pricing page. Pay for Pro today for features that are not shipped.
- **There is a monthly AI quota, and meeting notes eat it.** Vendor docs say meeting notes are unlimited in count but every transcription and summary draws from the plan's monthly Spark +AI quota, so heavy meeting use can exhaust the plan before it resets.
- **Legacy plan drift.** The pricing page notes that features and usage may differ for users on legacy plans — a quiet admission that what you bought is not necessarily what is sold now.
- **The AI is a notch below the leaders.** Spark is a very good client with AI attached; Shortwave and Superhuman are AI products with a client attached. If AI quality is the reason you're buying, that gap shows.

## Sidebar: the tool we can't recommend, because it no longer exists

Notion Mail was, for about seventeen months, the most interesting free AI email client available — Gmail-backed, with custom inbox views, AI auto-labels and snippets. It was free. It shut down on September 22, 2026 on all plans, with a September 21 preservation deadline, and an earlier June 30 cutoff for organizations relying on HIPAA coverage.

Read the shutdown notice as a shopping document. Gmail retained the messages. Users had to export drafts and scheduled emails, download snippet attachments separately, and **manually recreate** custom views, sorting and reminders — while existing synced email databases in Notion simply stopped receiving new mail. None of that was recoverable after the date.

That is the cost structure of every client replacement in this category, including the paid ones. The mail is portable because it lives with Google or Microsoft. The workflow you spent a year building on top of it is not, and it lasts exactly as long as the vendor's interest in the product. Price that in when you compare a $30 client to a $22 overlay or a $65-a-year filter.

## What we'd actually buy

**The Gmail power user, one seat.** Shortwave Business, $360/year. It is the only tool here whose AI search is worth the switching cost, and the daily quota is generous for one person who isn't running Expert-level reasoning all day. Check your Workspace entitlement first — if Gemini in Gmail covers your summarizing and drafting, you've saved $360.

**The five-person Microsoft team.** Nothing new. Copilot Chat is already $0 in your tenant and still works in Outlook after the April 2026 change; test whether summarizing and inbox Q&A is what you actually wanted before signing five seats of Copilot Business at $21/user/mo ($1,260/year). If it isn't enough, buy it for the two people who live in email, not the five.

**The consultant who won't leave Gmail.** Fyxer Standard at $22.50/user/mo annually ($270/year) — but use the full 7-day trial on a real inbox and audit the sorting before the card is charged, given the billing complaints on record.

**The person who just wants less email.** SaneBox Lunch, $109/year for two accounts and six features. It is the cheapest line item in this article and the only one that reduces volume rather than accelerating your response to it.

**The five-person team on a budget.** Spark Plus, $99/user/year — $495/year for five people with a real client, AI you can switch off, and 40 meeting notes each. That is one-quarter of the Superhuman bill.

## Bottom line

Stop comparing draft quality. The models rotate quarterly and everyone rents them from the same three labs. Compare the three things that persist: which architecture you're buying, what the meter is, and what evaporates when you leave.

On that basis: check your Google or Microsoft entitlement first, because a lot of teams are about to buy AI they already own. If you need genuinely better AI than the platform gives you and you're on Gmail, Shortwave is the pick, with the daily quota as the thing to test in the trial. If you won't change clients, Fyxer is the overlay, with sorting accuracy as the thing to test. If your problem is volume, SaneBox at $65-109 a year solves it more cheaply than anything else here. Superhuman is worth $396 to a specific person in a specific job, and to almost nobody else on their team.

And whatever you pick, assume it can be discontinued. Notion Mail's users found out on September 22.
