There is no best AI transcription tool. There is only the tool that matches the shape of your audio and the shape of your bill.

That sounds like a dodge. It isn't. Across every serious 2026 benchmark, the gap between the best and worst mainstream AI engines on clean audio is roughly 3–5 percentage points of word error rate — smaller than the swing you get from moving a microphone six inches. What actually differs between these products is everything wrapped around the engine: whether a bot walks into your client call uninvited, whether your minutes roll over (they almost never do), whether "unlimited" survives contact with your usage, and what happens on month seven when the vendor re-meters the plan you bought.

So this roundup is not a race to the lowest WER. It's a guide to which billing model and which workflow you can live with.

## The one question that splits this category

Before you compare features, answer this: **is your audio a meeting, or is it a file?**

Meeting tools (Otter, Fireflies, Notta) are built around a calendar. They join calls, attribute speakers, email a summary, and push action items into your CRM. They are priced per seat, because they assume a team.

File tools (Sonix, Rev, Descript, Whisper) are built around an upload. You hand them an interview, a lecture, an episode, a deposition, and they hand back text. They are priced per hour or per minute, because they assume volume, not headcount.

Buying a meeting tool for a pile of files means paying for seats you don't need and hitting per-recording caps. Buying a file tool for meetings means manually recording every call. Almost every "this tool disappointed me" review we read in researching this piece was somebody on the wrong side of that line.

The second thing to internalize: **in 2026 the meter is the price, not the sticker.** Otter cut Pro from 6,000 to 1,200 minutes a month without cutting the price. Fireflies added a 200-hour monthly cap to a tier previously sold as unlimited. Descript replaced transcription hours with "media minutes" plus "AI credits," which means an uploaded file burns your allowance whether or not you ever transcribe it. Read the meter before you read the number.

## 1. Otter.ai — the default, with an etiquette problem

**Best for:** solo professionals who want live captions in Zoom, Teams, or Meet with zero setup.
**Price:** Free (300 min/mo) · Pro $16.99/mo, $8.33/mo annual · Business $30/mo, ~$19.99–$20/seat/mo annual · Enterprise custom. Students and teachers with a .edu address get 20% off Pro (~$6.67/mo annual, $79.99/yr).

Otter is the tool most people mean when they say "AI transcription." The live caption stream during a call is genuinely low-latency, the calendar hookup takes about ninety seconds, and the free tier — 300 minutes a month, 30 minutes per conversation, 3 lifetime file imports — is enough to decide whether you like it.

Pro lifts you to 1,200 transcription minutes a month with a 90-minute cap per conversation and 10 file imports; Business goes to 6,000 minutes with a 4-hour meeting ceiling and concurrent meeting support, which matters the moment two people on your team are on calls at the same time. Minutes do not roll over on any tier.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The OtterPilot consent problem.** Auto-join is on by default after setup, and the most-repeated complaint across Reddit and BBB filings is the bot appearing in client or board meetings the account owner forgot it was watching. In all-party-consent jurisdictions that is not an etiquette issue, it's a legal one. Turn auto-join off before your first client call.
- **Multi-speaker accuracy is the weak spot.** Independent March 2026 WER testing puts Otter at 5.8% on clean studio audio but 11.5% on a three-speaker meeting and 15.0% on a phone call — the worst of the mainstream tools tested apart from Notta. Community reports consistently describe overlapping speech being merged into a single speaker.
- **Pro was quietly re-metered** from 6,000 to 1,200 minutes/month at the same price. Heavy users burn it in under two weeks.
- **Billing friction.** BBB and Trustpilot complaints cluster around annual renewals charged without much warning and cancellation that routes through support email rather than a self-serve button.

*No affiliate relationship with Otter.ai — reviewed independently.*

## 2. Fireflies.ai — the best team plumbing, with an asterisk in legal

**Best for:** sales and CS teams that need transcripts inside a CRM, not inside a notes app.
**Price:** Free · Pro $18/seat/mo, $10 annual · Business $29/seat/mo, $19 annual · Enterprise $39/seat/mo annual.

Fireflies wins on everything downstream of the transcript. Business tier adds video recording, CRM sync, conversation intelligence, and team analytics — that's the tier that actually replaces a sales-ops workflow, and it's the tier every "Fireflies is $10" article forgets to mention. Storage runs 800 min/seat on Free, 8,000 min/seat on Pro, unlimited on Business.

Crucially, **transcription and summaries are never billed in AI credits.** Credits only apply to the newer agentic surface: AI Skills, the Personal Assistant, Voice Agents, Sales Assist, AskFred on Live Assist, Autofill CRM. Plans ship one-time credits (Free 20, Pro 20, Business 30, Enterprise 50) that don't stack on upgrade, and top-ups run $5/50 credits up to $600/10,000 credits, with unused purchased credits expiring monthly. If you run out on a paid plan, Fireflies auto-subscribes you to the $5 tier unless you pause it — a default worth knowing about.

**Where it breaks:**
- **"Unlimited" got a number.** In July 2026 Fireflies rolled a 200-hour/month cap onto Pro, previously sold as unlimited under an undefined fair-use policy. Ten people at five hours of meetings a week clears that.
- **The bot is visible.** A "Fireflies Notetaker" participant appears in every call. Prospects notice, and they comment.
- **A pending Illinois BIPA class action** over biometric voiceprint data (filed December 2025) is a real item for regulated buyers to run past counsel before signing.
- **Speaker attribution errors**, not raw word errors, are the loudest quality complaint — a transcript that credits your rep's concession to the prospect is worse than no transcript.
- **Real-time notes are Google Meet-first**; Teams and Zoom were still "rolling out" at the time of writing. Buy for the post-call summary.

*No affiliate relationship with Fireflies.ai — reviewed independently.*

## 3. Descript — the transcript is the editor

**Best for:** podcasters and video editors who want to cut media by deleting words.
**Price:** Free (1 media hour/mo, 100 AI credits) · Hobbyist $24/mo, $16 annual (10 media hours, 400 credits) · Creator $35/mo, $24 annual (30 media hours, 800 credits) · Business $65/mo, $50 annual (40 media hours). Multi-language transcription covers 25 languages.

Nothing else in this list does what Descript does: you edit the audio and video by editing the text. For interview podcasts and talking-head video, it collapses transcription and editing into one job. Accuracy is solidly mid-pack — 4.5% WER on clean studio audio, 9.4% on a three-speaker meeting in March 2026 testing — which is fine, because you're going to read the transcript anyway.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The November 2025 re-metering was brutal.** Transcription hours were replaced by "media minutes" (consumed on *upload or recording*, transcribed or not; still images count as one second each) plus AI credits for Underlord, Studio Sound, Eye Contact, and generative media. Legacy plans were sunset on November 5.
- **Users reported price increases up to ~26% alongside feature removals**, with filler-word removal and timeline exports moving to higher tiers. Descript's own staff response confirms Creator went $30 → $35 and that legacy users were migrated to current pricing.
- **Generative AI features eat credits non-deterministically.** One widely-shared complaint: a single lightly-edited video exhausting a month of credits, with the poster calculating a four-figure monthly bill to keep working at that rate. Text-to-speech is the most credit-hungry surface.
- **The annual/monthly gap is enormous** — Hobbyist is $24 monthly but $16 on annual. Monthly billing here is a 50% premium.

*No affiliate relationship with Descript — reviewed independently.*

## 4. Rev — buy the humans, not the AI

**Best for:** depositions, research interviews, journalism, anything where an error costs more than a dollar a minute.
**Price:** Human transcription $1.99/min (Spanish $3.25/min) · AI transcription $0.25/min · Subscriptions: Essentials $29.99/seat/mo, $25.49 annual (5,000 AI min/seat/mo, EN+ES) · Pro $59.99/seat/mo, $47.99 annual (10,000 verbatim AI min, 37+ languages) · Unlimited tier available. Add-ons: rush +$1.25/min, premium +$1.75/min, verbatim +$0.50/min, timestamps +$0.30/min.

Rev's human service is the only thing in this roundup with a real accuracy guarantee: 99%+, in 12 hours or less, with no surcharge for accents, crosstalk, or multiple speakers. Benchmarked against AI, it isn't close — 1.2% WER on clean audio and 3.1% on a three-speaker meeting, versus 4–12% for the machines. Subscribers get 3–15% off human orders depending on plan and billing cycle.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The AI tier is not a bargain.** At $0.25/min ($15/hr), Rev AI is 25–80× the cost of running the same audio through the Whisper API, for 5.1% clean-audio WER — mid-pack.
- **$1.99/min is $119 per hour of audio.** A single 90-minute research interview costs more than a year of Notta Pro. Human transcription is a line item you budget per project, not a subscription you forget about.
- **Turnaround is a real constraint** if you want it same-hour; rush pricing adds 63% to the base rate.
- **Legal-formatted transcripts are priced per page**, not per minute, and are excluded from subscription discounts entirely.

*No affiliate relationship with Rev — reviewed independently.*

## 5. Sonix — the best per-hour deal for a pile of files

**Best for:** researchers, agencies, and anyone with an archive rather than a calendar.
**Price:** Pay-as-you-go $10/hr (5 GB storage, single user) · Core $25/mo or $275/yr (5 hrs/mo transcription + 5 hrs AI workspace, 25 GB) · Advanced $50/mo or $550/yr (20 hrs + 25 hrs AI, 50 GB) · Pro $80/mo or $880/yr (40 hrs + 100 hrs AI, 100 GB). Extra seats $25/mo each. Overage $10/hr ($8/hr on education/nonprofit rates). 54+ languages.

Sonix is the accuracy leader among the self-serve AI tools we could benchmark — 4.2% WER clean, 9.0% on a three-speaker meeting, 8.0% on accented English — and its editor (word-level timestamps, speaker labels, translation, burned-in subtitles included on subscription) is the most professional in the group. Billing by the audio-hour, prorated to the second, means a 12-minute file costs $2 and no one is paying for an empty seat.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Sonix publishes two pricing structures that don't reconcile.** The main pricing page lists Core/Advanced/Pro at $25/$50/$80 per month with bundled hours; the detailed comparison page describes a $22/seat/mo ($16.50 annual) platform fee plus $5/hr transcription. Get your rep to confirm in writing which one you're on before committing to volume.
- **Hours are use-it-or-lose-it** on subscription, and the AI workspace allowance is a *separate* meter from transcription hours — easy to exhaust one while the other sits idle.
- **The "99% accuracy" marketing claim** applies to clean audio only. On phone-quality audio the same independent test measured 11.8% WER. That's normal for the category; the claim is not.
- **No meeting bot, no calendar integration.** It will not join your Zoom call. That's a deliberate design choice, but confirm it matches your workflow.

*No affiliate relationship with Sonix — reviewed independently.*

## 6. Notta — the most minutes per dollar

**Best for:** budget-conscious individuals who transcribe a lot and don't need a team layer.
**Price:** Free (120 min/mo, 3 min per conversation, 50 file uploads, 10 AI summaries) · Pro $8.17/mo annual ($97.99/yr), $13.99/mo month-to-month (1,800 min/mo, 5 hrs per recording, 100 uploads, 100 summaries) · Business $16.67/seat/mo annual ($199.99/yr), $27.99 monthly (200 uploads, 200 summaries) · Enterprise from 51 seats.

1,800 minutes a month for $8.17 is 30 hours of audio for the price of two coffees — the best raw minute-per-dollar ratio of any subscription here, and 50% more monthly minutes than Otter Pro at roughly the same price. Real-time and bilingual translation are separate add-ons ($9/mo annual, $15 monthly for one tier; a second at higher volume).

**Where it breaks:**
- **Accuracy is the lowest in the group.** 6.5% WER on clean studio audio, 12.8% on a three-speaker meeting, 16.2% on a phone call. You are trading correction time for money.
- **Quotas don't carry over**, and Notta bills the *full duration* of an uploaded file including silence, rounded up to the nearest minute. Long, sparse recordings — lectures, stakeouts, ambient interviews — cost far more than their speaking time.
- **The free tier's 3-minute-per-conversation cap** makes it a demo, not a plan.
- **Every summary regeneration burns a quota unit**, and deleting the summary doesn't refund it. Three attempts at a decent summary is three units.
- **Pricing pages disagree on the month-to-month Pro rate** ($10 in one footnote, $13.99 in third-party coverage). Verify at checkout.

*No affiliate relationship with Notta — reviewed independently.*

## 7. OpenAI Whisper API — the option that makes the rest look expensive

**Best for:** anyone technical, or anyone with more than ~40 hours of audio a month.
**Price:** `gpt-4o-mini-transcribe` ~$0.003/min ($0.18/hr) · `whisper-1` and `gpt-4o-transcribe` $0.006/min ($0.36/hr) · `gpt-realtime-whisper` ~$0.017/min ($1.02/hr) for live streams. Self-hosting (faster-whisper on your own GPU) trades cost for infrastructure.

The number that should reframe this entire category: **one hour of audio costs 18 to 36 cents.** Three thousand minutes a month — 50 hours, more than most teams generate — runs $9 to $18 at API rates. Sonix would charge $500 for the same volume pay-as-you-go; Rev AI would charge $750. Whisper Large v3 also sets the accuracy benchmark for clean English at ~3–5% WER across 99 languages. If you have a developer and a batch of files, everything above is a convenience tax.

**Where it breaks:**
- **There is no product.** No speaker diarization out of the box, no editor, no summaries, no sharing, no calendar. You're buying a transcript string and building the rest.
- **It's batch, not live.** On the AMI multi-party meeting corpus Whisper's WER rises to 12–18%, because it processes complete segments rather than streams. Real-time costs ~5× more per minute.
- **25 MB file cap** means chunking long recordings yourself, and self-hosted deployments carry an 8–12 second cold-start penalty unless you keep a worker warm.
- **Self-hosting rarely pencils out** below thousands of hours a month once you price a dedicated GPU instance. Independent latency testing found Groq's Whisper endpoint roughly 7× faster than OpenAI's API on a 5-minute clip if speed matters more than vendor consolidation.

## What accuracy actually looks like

Independent March 2026 benchmarking across identical files (clean studio, three-speaker meeting, phone call, accented English) — word error rate, lower is better:

| Tool | Clean studio | 3-speaker meeting | Phone call | Accented English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rev (Human) | 1.2% | 3.1% | 4.8% | 2.9% |
| Sonix | 4.2% | 9.0% | 11.8% | 8.0% |
| Descript | 4.5% | 9.4% | 13.2% | 8.5% |
| Rev AI | 5.1% | 10.8% | 14.1% | 9.2% |
| Otter.ai | 5.8% | 11.5% | 15.0% | 10.1% |
| Notta | 6.5% | 12.8% | 16.2% | 11.0% |

Two conclusions. First, every AI tool loses roughly half its accuracy going from studio audio to a phone call — your microphone is a bigger lever than your vendor. Second, the spread between the best and worst AI engine is about 2.3 points on clean audio. Choose on billing model and workflow; choose on accuracy only if you're deciding between AI and humans.

## What we'd actually buy

**The $8 stack — solo, occasional.** Notta Pro annual at $8.17/mo for 30 hours a month, and accept that you'll fix names and jargon by hand. If live captions matter more than volume, swap in Otter Pro at $8.33 and take 1,200 minutes instead of 1,800.

**The $34 stack — a working podcaster.** Descript Creator annual ($24/mo) for the text-based edit, plus Sonix pay-as-you-go (~$10 for the month's overflow files and any translation work). Descript alone will strand you the first month you run past 30 media hours.

**The $19 stack — a five-person sales team.** Fireflies Business annual at $19/seat. Not Pro: CRM sync, video recording, and conversation intelligence are the entire reason to buy Fireflies, and they're all above the $10 tier. Budget one $5 credit top-up per month and pause auto-upgrade so it stays your decision.

**The ~$20-a-month stack — a research shop with 60 hours of audio.** A weekend of developer time on `gpt-4o-mini-transcribe` at $0.003/min: 60 hours = $10.80. Add Rev human transcription à la carte at $1.99/min for the three interviews that go in the published paper. Everything else in this roundup would charge you $300–$600 for the same month.

**When to skip all of it.** If you're in a Google Workspace shop, Gemini Notebook transcribes imported audio files across 60+ languages at import time as part of the plan you already pay for. It is not a meeting recorder and gives you no speaker diarization or editor — but for "turn these ten interview files into searchable, citable sources," it's already on your invoice.

## The bottom line

The engines have converged. What hasn't converged is the metering, and 2026 was the year every vendor in this category tightened it: Otter cut Pro's minutes by 80% at the same price, Fireflies put a 200-hour number on "unlimited," Descript re-metered uploads instead of transcriptions and raised legacy users up to 26%. Assume your plan gets worse, and prefer per-hour or per-minute billing (Sonix, Rev, Whisper) over allowances you can't carry over, unless the workflow around the transcript is the thing you're actually buying.

Pick by workload: calendar → Fireflies or Otter. Files → Sonix. Editing → Descript. Evidence → Rev humans. Volume plus a developer → Whisper, at about 3% of everyone else's price.
