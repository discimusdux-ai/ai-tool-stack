There is no best AI note-taking app in 2026, and anyone who hands you a single winner has not used more than two of them for longer than a week.

The category has quietly split in half. One group of tools is trying to capture more for you — meeting audio, calendar context, browser clips, email — on the theory that the bottleneck is getting things written down. The other group assumes you already have too much written down and is competing on getting the right sentence back out of the pile eighteen months later. Buying from the wrong half is why so many people churn through three of these apps a year and end up back in a plain text file.

We ran seven of them against the same workload for the last several weeks: client calls, research reading, half-formed product ideas, and the deeply unglamorous job of finding something we wrote in March. Pricing below was checked against vendor pricing pages and help docs in August 2026, and where a vendor's own numbers disagree with what the review sites are publishing, we say so.

## The question that actually decides this

Before comparing features, answer one thing: **in two years, do you need to be able to open this note without paying anyone?**

If the answer is yes — research, writing, anything you intend to build on for years — you want a tool whose notes are files you control: Obsidian first, Apple Notes second, anything with clean Markdown export third. The AI in those tools is bolted on and slightly worse, and that trade is deliberate.

If the answer is no — most meeting notes, most standups, most sales calls, most of the daily churn of a job — then durability is worth nothing to you and you should buy the tool with the best capture and the best search. That's Granola, Notion, Mem or Gemini Notebook, and you should feel no guilt about the lock-in, because you were never going to reread those notes anyway.

Almost every furious one-star review in this category is someone who bought from the wrong side of that line: a knowledge worker who wanted a permanent archive and got a subscription-gated meeting feed, or a manager who wanted meeting notes and got a plugin ecosystem to configure.

The second framing rule: **in 2026 the meter is the price, not the sticker.** Notion introduced hard AI usage limits on August 3, 2026. Notion's Custom Agents moved to credits on May 4, 2026. Granola cut free-plan history to 30 days in its February 2026 rebrand. Capacities' AI assistant runs on a "dynamically adjusted" daily budget. Read the meter, not the headline number.

## 1. Notion AI — best overall for teams

**Best for:** teams already living in Notion who want AI without a second subscription
**Price:** included in Business, $20/user/mo annual ($24 monthly)

Notion spent 2024 and 2025 selling AI as a $8–10 per-seat add-on and taking abuse for it. That add-on is gone. As of 2026, the full AI suite — Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes, Enterprise Search across connected tools like Slack and GitHub, Research Mode, AI blocks — is bundled into the Business seat at $20/user/month billed annually, or $24 billed monthly. Free and Plus workspaces get a small trial allocation that any real user exhausts in days.

That bundling is the strongest thing about it. If you already pay for Notion Business, your marginal cost for competent AI notes is zero, and the AI is looking at the same database of specs, meeting notes and project pages your team already maintains. No other tool here starts with that much context.

AI Meeting Notes is the feature that surprised us. It runs in the desktop app (macOS 13+ or current Windows, app version 4.7.0 or later), captures system audio from Zoom or Teams without sending a bot into the call, and drops a transcript and summary straight into a Notion page where the follow-up tasks already live. Limits worth knowing: 10 hours of transcription per user per day, one-minute minimum before a summary generates, 15 transcription languages but speaker labelling in English only, and the browser version captures microphone audio only.

**Where it breaks**

- **The meter arrived.** On August 3, 2026 Notion started enforcing AI usage limits on Business and Enterprise workspaces, tracked both in a rolling six-hour window and across the monthly billing cycle. Notion has not published how many Agent requests or images a plan includes. When you hit a wall, features pause until usage ages out or you buy credits — and "we don't publish the number" is not a great answer for a workspace budget.
- **Custom Agents cost extra.** Scheduled, trigger-based agents run on Notion credits at $10 per 1,000 credits as an add-on to Business or Enterprise, with no public per-run credit breakdown. Everything else on the seat is flat-rate; agents are not.
- **Q&A degrades in big workspaces.** The complaint we saw most often, and reproduced ourselves: in large workspaces, Notion AI answers confidently from stale or archived pages. It is a retrieval problem, not a model problem, and it gets worse as the archive you're paying to search grows.
- **Third-party pricing pages are wrong.** Multiple sites still describe a "$10 per member add-on billed separately." That is the retired 2025 model. Check notion.com/pricing before you budget.

## 2. Obsidian — best for owning your data

**Best for:** long-horizon knowledge work, writers, researchers, anyone allergic to lock-in
**Price:** free; Sync $4/user/mo annual ($5 monthly); Publish $8/site/mo annual ($10 monthly); optional commercial licence $50/user/year

Obsidian is not an AI app. It's a Markdown editor over a folder of plain files on your disk, and that is precisely why it belongs in an AI roundup: it is the only tool here where the AI is a component you choose rather than a vendor decision you inherit. Community plugins — Copilot for Obsidian, Smart Composer and others — let you point chat, summarization and semantic search at your vault using your own OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or local model key.

The economics are hard to argue with. The app is free, including for commercial use; the optional $50/user/year commercial licence is a support gesture, not a requirement. Sync at $4/month is the only thing most people pay for. Your AI cost is metered API usage, which for typical note-taking volumes runs a couple of dollars a month — less than every subscription in this roundup.

**Where it breaks**

- **You are the integrator.** Plugin quality varies, plugins break on updates, and model support lags. A representative example: Copilot for Obsidian's token-limit slider was hard-capped around 128K for output through mid-2026, which frustrated users trying to work with million-token context models until the maintainer clarified that input context is governed by a separate auto-compact setting. That is the flavour of problem you sign up for.
- **No meeting capture worth the name.** There is no first-party audio capture, no calendar integration, no bot-free meeting notes. If meetings are your main input, this is the wrong tool and no plugin fixes it.
- **Mobile is the weak seat.** Sync is reliable, but heavy plugin setups are slow and fiddly on phones, and that's where quick capture happens.
- **Setup cost is real.** Budget an afternoon, plus recurring maintenance. People who quit Obsidian rarely quit because it lacked features.

## 3. Granola — best for meetings

**Best for:** people in back-to-back calls who want notes, not a transcript archive
**Price:** Basic free; Business $14/user/mo; Enterprise from $35/user/mo

Granola's trick is that it doesn't send a bot into your meeting. It listens to system audio on your Mac or PC, merges what was said with the rough notes you typed yourself, and produces something that reads like notes a competent colleague took — not a wall of transcript. After weeks of use it remains the best meeting artifact in this roundup, and the absence of a "Granola Notetaker has joined" banner changes the social texture of client calls more than you'd expect.

Per Granola's own billing docs (August 2026): the free Basic plan gives unlimited meetings, AI notes, Chat, shared folders and templates, but you can only see and use the last 30 days of notes in the app. Older notes are stored, not deleted, and come back if you upgrade. Business at $14/user/month adds unlimited history, manual model selection in Chat (including Claude Fable 5), and the integrations that make notes useful elsewhere: Notion, Slack, HubSpot, Attio, Affinity, Zapier, plus MCP. Enterprise from $35 adds SSO, admin policy and org-wide training opt-out.

**Where it breaks**

- **The 2026 rebrand cost them goodwill.** February 2026 brought the redesign, the 30-day free-plan history limit and a visible pivot toward team buyers. The backlash on Reddit, Threads and LinkedIn was loud, and individual users are plainly no longer the primary audience.
- **Free-tier limits are misreported everywhere.** Third-party sites variously claim "25 lifetime meetings," "14-day history" and an "$18 Individual plan." Granola's own docs and pricing page say unlimited meetings, 30-day in-app history, and Basic/Business/Enterprise only. Trust the docs; the aggregators are stale.
- **Sharing defaults are loose.** The Verge reported in 2026 that shared notes are viewable by anyone with the link by default, and that non-enterprise users' anonymized data may be used to improve the service unless they opt out in settings. Fixable in settings; still a bad default for sensitive calls.
- **Support has thinned.** Multiple long-time users report multi-day first-response times and script-driven answers since the v3 platform launch, particularly on integration edge cases.
- **No annual discount.** You pay the same monthly rate year-round, which is unusual and, if you're used to a 20% annual haircut, worth pricing in.

## 4. Gemini Notebook — best for research and reading

**Best for:** turning a stack of sources into something you actually understand
**Price:** free (Standard); higher limits via Google AI Plus / Pro / Ultra plans

NotebookLM was renamed **Gemini Notebook** on July 16, 2026, which means half the tutorials you'll find are filed under a name the product no longer uses. The premise is unchanged and still the best in the category: you load sources — PDFs, docs, pasted text, URLs, YouTube transcripts — and everything the model says is grounded in those sources with inline citations. For reading-heavy work it is the only tool here that reliably doesn't invent things, because it isn't drawing on open-web memory.

Free Standard limits, per Google's help docs: 100 notebooks, 50 sources per notebook, 500,000 words or 200MB per source, 50 chat queries a day, 3 audio and 3 video overviews a day, 10 reports/flashcards/quizzes/mind maps a day, and 10 Deep Research runs a month. Google AI Plus raises that to 200 notebooks and 200 chats a day; Pro to 500 notebooks, 300 sources per notebook and 500 chats a day; Ultra tiers go considerably further.

**Where it breaks**

- **It is not a note-taking app.** There is no daily note, no quick capture, no inbox. It reads sources you give it. Using it as your primary notes system means manually feeding it, forever.
- **Notes are the weakest object in it.** Your own notes are only used in a prompt when you explicitly select them — sources are always in context, notes are not. And per Google's FAQ, there is no way to recover a deleted note. None.
- **YouTube sources are fragile.** Transcript-only, captions required, and the source dies if the video goes private.
- **The daily meters are low on free.** Fifty chats a day sounds generous until a single research session eats thirty of them.

## 5. Mem — best for pure retrieval

**Best for:** heavy personal capture where finding things again is the whole job
**Price:** free tier; Pro $12/mo; Proactive $99/mo (7-day trial, 30-day money-back on first month)

Mem is the clearest expression of the retrieval half of this category. You dump things in without folders, and the product's entire promise is that asking for them later works. When it works, it is the fastest "where did I write that" experience of the seven — near-instant, and genuinely better than keyword search at fuzzy recall.

Pro at $12/month covers unlimited notes, collections and templates, 250 Mem Agent messages, unlimited PDF understanding, connected email, API access and meeting briefings. Mem Proactive at $99/month is the ambitious tier: an agent that surfaces things before you ask, catches commitments you never flagged, and reaches you in Slack, iMessage and WhatsApp, with unlimited messages. Mem 2.0, a full rebuild launched in late 2025, added offline support on web, desktop and mobile.

**Where it breaks**

- **Self-organization is oversold.** Reviewers consistently report the AI links notes well only if you already write in consistent formats. Dump unstructured thoughts and the connections are close to random — which is the exact use case the marketing targets.
- **No Android app in 2026.** For a tool that lives on frictionless capture, that disqualifies a large share of buyers outright.
- **The editor is thin.** No highlighting, limited rich formatting, awkward handling of long notes. Fine for capture, painful for writing.
- **Recurring support and momentum complaints.** Unanswered emails, including to founders, and a widely-shared sense that development stalled between big releases. There's also a $12-to-$99 pricing chasm with nothing in between, which makes the upgrade decision unnecessarily binary.

## 6. Reflect — best single-price simplicity

**Best for:** networked-notes people who want Obsidian's model without the assembly
**Price:** $10/mo billed annually ($120/year); 14-day trial; no free plan

Reflect is backlinks, daily notes and a graph — the Roam lineage — with hosted AI built in and end-to-end encryption on by default. One plan, one price, no credits, no seat tiers, no AI meter to reason about. In a category where every vendor is busy inventing a new unit of consumption, that is worth something.

The AI features are sensible rather than flashy: transcription and voice capture, writing improvement, auto-summary of notes, and an AI-powered daily note. Calendar integration pulls in meetings; Readwise sync pulls in highlights; a browser extension captures snippets.

**Where it breaks**

- **No free tier at all.** Fourteen days, then $10/month. For a personal notes app competing against a free Obsidian and a free Apple Notes, that's a hard sell unless the encryption is a requirement.
- **Thin integrations.** Google Calendar, Apple/Google contacts, Readwise, browser capture. That's most of the list. No CRM, no Slack, no meaningful automation surface.
- **Small ecosystem.** No plugin community to fill gaps, so what ships is what you get.
- **AI stack visibility.** Reflect's own marketing still leads on GPT-4-era model branding. It works fine, but you have no model choice and limited transparency about what's running.

## 7. Apple Notes — best free default

**Best for:** iPhone and Mac users who want zero cost, zero setup and no new subscription
**Price:** $0

Apple Notes is the tool most people should try before paying anyone. In 2026 it records audio in a note, transcribes it, and generates an Apple Intelligence summary of the transcript locally — the single feature most people are paying $10–$17 a month for elsewhere. iOS 27 added automatic Markdown handling and Markdown copy-paste (a real export improvement), divider lines, section links, and Siri AI note creation and editing on supported devices.

It is free, it syncs, it's already installed, and the notes are trivially exportable now that Markdown round-trips.

**Where it breaks**

- **Apple-only, and not even all Apple.** Siri AI and several Apple Intelligence features require recent hardware (iPhone 15 Pro and newer), launched as a waitlisted English-first beta, and there is no meaningful Windows or Android story.
- **No retrieval intelligence.** Search is search. There is no semantic "what did we decide about pricing in March," which is the thing the paid tools are actually selling.
- **No system-audio meeting capture.** It records the room, not your Zoom call.
- **Organization tops out fast.** Folders and tags only. Past a few thousand notes it becomes a shoebox.

## Honourable mention: Capacities

Capacities is a genuinely good object-based PKM tool with a complete free tier, Pro at $9.99/month annual ($11.99 monthly) adding AI, calendar, smart queries, table formulas and API access, and a Believer tier around $12.49/month annual. It missed the main list for one reason worth naming: its AI assistant runs on a **dynamically adjusted daily budget**, and the company has said it intends to move toward cost-per-use or bring-your-own-key. That's honest, and it may end up cheaper — but it means the AI allowance you buy today is not a fixed quantity.

## What we'd actually buy

**If you're solo and cheap ($0/month).** Apple Notes for capture and voice summaries, plus Gemini Notebook's free tier for anything research-shaped. That covers the two jobs most people are trying to buy, at zero, and it's a genuinely defensible stack in 2026. Start here for a month before spending anything.

**If you're solo and serious ($4/month).** Obsidian with Sync, plus a Copilot or Smart Composer plugin on your own API key. Call it $4 for sync and $2–3 of API usage. You own every file, you can change models when the frontier moves, and nothing you write is hostage to a pricing change. The cost is an afternoon of setup and occasional plugin maintenance.

**If your calendar is the problem ($14/month).** Granola Business, on its own. Don't stack it with Notion AI hoping for a super-system; the meeting summary is the deliverable and Granola's is the best one here. If your history is under 30 days of relevance — most sales and support work — the free tier is legitimately enough.

**If you're a team of five ($100/month).** Notion Business at $20 a seat, and stop there. The AI is included, meeting notes land next to the projects they're about, and one subscription beats five people each expensing a different note app. Watch the new usage limits in your first full billing cycle before committing to Custom Agent credits on top.

**If retrieval is the whole job ($12/month).** Mem Pro — but only if you're on iOS, and only if you're willing to write with some consistency. Skip Proactive at $99 unless you've already proved to yourself that the $12 tier changed how you work.

## The bottom line

The honest state of AI note-taking in 2026 is that the capture problem is solved and the retrieval problem is not. Every tool here will faithfully record what happened in your meeting. None of them will reliably tell you, unprompted, what you decided about a topic across eleven months and four hundred notes — Notion's Q&A degrades in exactly the large workspaces that need it most, Mem's linking depends on you writing tidily, and Gemini Notebook only knows what you hand it.

So buy for the job you actually have. Meetings you'll never reread: Granola, free tier first. A team that already pays Notion: you already own the answer. Notes you want to still have in 2036: Obsidian, and the AI is a plugin, deliberately. Everything else in this category is a preference, and preferences are cheap — but subscriptions and lock-in are not.

One last thing to check before you pay: whether the tool's meter changed in the last six months. Four of the seven here re-priced or re-metered something in 2026 alone. In this category, the sticker price is the least stable number on the page.
