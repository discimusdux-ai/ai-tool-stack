# OpusClip vs Descript 2026: Clip Machine or Editor You Drive?

Both tools will take a long recording and hand you short vertical clips. That is why people compare them, and it is also why the comparison is slightly misleading. OpusClip is a **clipping machine with an editor bolted on**. Descript is an **editor with a clipping button added**. They overlap on one feature and are built for different jobs.

The question that settles it is not "which finds better clips." It is **"do you want to supervise or to author?"** If you want twenty candidate clips by morning and you're happy to bin most of them, that's OpusClip. If you were going to re-cut whatever the AI picked anyway, and you also edit the long episode, that's Descript.

The second question is the meter. The two bill differently, and that difference decides the real price more than the sticker does.

## What Each One Actually Is

**OpusClip** takes a long video (upload or link) and returns a set of clips, each with a Virality Score, reframed to 9:16, 1:1 or 16:9, with animated captions. Paid plans post to YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Instagram Reels, and Pro adds a scheduler and more networks. It also exports to Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve on Pro, which is an admission that the final polish happens elsewhere. We covered it in full in our [OpusClip review](/blog/opus-clip-review-2026).

**Descript** made its name by turning media into a document. The transcript is the timeline: delete a sentence and the video cuts with it. Around that sit Studio Sound, filler-word removal, screen and Rooms recording, and an AI co-editor called Underlord. Its **Create clips** tool lives in the AI Tools panel under Repurpose. You pick the number of clips (1 to 20) and a length (10 seconds to 5 minutes), optionally apply a portrait or square layout, and optionally type a topic or goal to steer the selection. Each clip lands as its own editable composition in a "Clips from..." folder. Our [Loom vs Descript comparison](/blog/loom-vs-descript-2026) covers the rest of the product.

Notice the difference in posture. OpusClip decides, then lets you adjust. Descript generates drafts, then expects you to finish them.

### The meter is the price, not the sticker

This is where the two diverge, and where most people pick wrong.

**OpusClip bills by minutes of source video imported.** One credit equals one minute of the original, partial minutes round down, and anything under a minute still costs one. The number of clips you keep does not change the cost. A 60-minute podcast costs 60 credits whether you export 2 clips or 20. Pro's 300 monthly credits cover roughly **five hour-long episodes a month**. Credits roll over for two months on Pro, then expire.

**Descript uses two currencies.** Media minutes track how much you transcribe and record. AI credits are spent on generative features, and Descript's help center lists Repurpose tools such as Create clips as ones that draw on AI credits on current plans. Both allowances reset at each billing cycle and **do not roll over**. Top-ups exist on Creator and Business.

Descript moved to this model in September 2025, and the complaints followed. A long-time Creator subscriber wrote on Trustpilot in February 2026 that they had edited three podcast episodes with little AI use and were already out of credits. Independent reviews also report that top-ups expire 12 months after purchase and that credit consumption is not always clear before you run a feature. That is one reviewer plus roundups, not a verdict, but it is the pattern to test on the free plan before paying.

Rough workload math, four 60-minute episodes a month:

- **OpusClip:** 240 credits. Fits inside Pro (300) with room to spare. Starter's 150 credits would not cover it, which is why Pro is the right tier.
- **Descript:** 4 hours of media, well inside Hobbyist's 10 media hours. The unknown is AI credits: Create clips plus Studio Sound plus anything generative draws from a 400-credit pool. Run a real episode on the free plan's 100 credits and see how far they go.

Descript's own comparison page claims 30 processing hours a month against OpusClip's 5. That compares Descript's Creator plan to OpusClip's monthly Pro, and "hours of media" is not the same unit as "clips you can afford to generate." Read it as vendor marketing.

## Clip Quality: Who Picks Better Moments

We would be guessing if we gave you a number here, and so would anyone else. Neither vendor publishes an accuracy benchmark, and the "virality" in both marketing pages is hedged. Descript's own clips page says its clips "(might) go viral (maybe)." What we can say from the products and reviews:

- **OpusClip is stronger on varied footage.** ClipAnything analyses visuals, audio and sentiment, not just a transcript, so it handles vlogs, gameplay and low-dialogue video. Descript's clipping works from the transcript of your composition, so it is best on talking content: podcasts, tutorials, interviews.
- **OpusClip gives you a ranking.** The Virality Score is OpusClip's own estimate of hook, flow and topic. Use it to sort twenty clips into a review order. It is not a view forecast.
- **Descript gives you a steering wheel.** The topic or goal box lets you say what you want ("the part about pricing") before it cuts, and the output is already an editable composition, not a finished export.
- **Both miss.** Reviewers report OpusClip clips that end too early or skip the best beat. Treat both tools' output as a first draft.

One more point: OpusClip users have noted **template fatigue**. When a lot of creators use the same caption style, clips start to look alike. Descript's open editor makes it easier to look different.

## Where OpusClip Breaks

1. **Processing failures.** Projects hang or never finish, especially at busy times. Reviewers say reliability has worsened for some long-time users.
2. **Billing disputes.** Users report charges after cancelling and credits that expire. OpusClip does not refund subscription fees.
3. **You pay for the upload, not the output.** An unedited two-hour livestream costs 120 credits even if only three minutes are usable. Trim first.
4. **Light editing.** You can trim and style, but frame-accurate work means exporting to Premiere or Resolve on Pro, or finishing in a tool like Descript.
5. **Caption typos** on names, brands and jargon need hand-fixing.

OpusClip's App Store rating is around 4.9 from roughly 7,000 ratings and Trustpilot around 4.0 from about 500, with about a fifth of Trustpilot reviews at one star (as reported by an independent review, September 2026). The split is the story: happy when it works, angry when it fails near a deadline.

## Where Descript Breaks

1. **The AI-credit meter.** Allowances don't roll over, top-ups reportedly expire, and heavy AI use can drain a plan fast. Creator is the first tier with a genuinely large pool.
2. **Feature bloat and a learning curve.** It grew from a transcript editor into recording, avatars, voice cloning and an AI co-editor. New users describe the interface as cluttered.
3. **Stability reports.** Independent roundups cite lost edits and performance problems on long projects. Save and back up long projects.
4. **Clipping is not its strength.** There is no virality ranking, no built-in scheduler like OpusClip's that we could find, and you do the finishing.
5. **Paying for an editor you may not use.** If your long-form is already edited elsewhere, Descript's price buys features you will not touch.

## Who Should Pick Which

**Pick OpusClip if:**
- You already have finished long-form video (podcast, webinar, stream, YouTube) and need a week of Shorts, Reels and TikToks with minimal effort
- You publish on a schedule and want auto-posting
- Your footage isn't only talking heads
- You are comfortable reviewing twenty clips and discarding fifteen

**Pick Descript if:**
- You edit the long episode yourself, and clips are the last 10% of the job
- You want to type a topic and get a draft you can reshape
- You value a real timeline, Studio Sound and recording in the same tool
- You'd rather author clips than supervise them

**Pay for both if** you produce a weekly show: edit the episode in Descript, export the finished video, and run it through OpusClip for the clip batch. At annual pricing that is Descript Hobbyist ($16/mo) plus OpusClip Pro ($14.50/mo), about **$30.50 a month, roughly $366 a year**. Month to month the same pair is $24 plus $29, or $53. Many creators run exactly this sequence because each tool does what the other does badly.

**Pay for neither if** you post a few clips a month. Both free plans will cover that, with watermarks, and a manual editor is faster.

## FAQ

**Is OpusClip or Descript better for podcasts?**
If the podcast is already edited, OpusClip gets you clips fastest. If you edit the episode too, Descript covers both jobs, and plenty of podcasters use it for the edit and OpusClip for the clip batch.

**Is Descript's Create clips as good as OpusClip?**
It does the same basic job, finding self-contained moments and cutting them into compositions. OpusClip adds scoring, auto-reframing that tracks the speaker, and direct posting. Descript gives you the editor to finish each clip.

**How are OpusClip credits counted?**
One credit per minute of the original video you import, rounded down, minimum one. The number of clips exported doesn't change the cost.

**Do Descript credits roll over?**
No. Media minutes and AI credits reset each billing cycle and unused amounts do not carry over.

**Which is cheaper?**
OpusClip, for clips alone: $14.50 a month on annual Pro. Descript's cheapest paid plan is $16 a month annual and $24 monthly, and it buys you an editor too.

## Bottom Line

Don't ask which tool is better. Ask which job you're hiring for. For turning finished long videos into a stream of posted shorts, OpusClip is the cheaper, faster, more automated pick, as long as you trim before uploading and keep a backup plan for failed renders. For anyone who edits the long video and wants clips as part of that edit, Descript is the better home, as long as you test its AI-credit meter on your real workload first. And if you run a weekly show, the honest answer is both, for about $30 a month on annual billing.
