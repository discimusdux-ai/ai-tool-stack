Most HubSpot vs ActiveCampaign comparisons line the two up feature by feature and call it a draw. That misses the point. On the pricing pages, both sell email, automation, landing pages, a CRM and AI agents. In practice they are different purchases.

**ActiveCampaign is an email and automation engine that comes with a CRM. HubSpot is a CRM and customer database that comes with an email engine.** Work out which one you are actually buying and the rest of the decision mostly follows. The price gap explains why this matters: ActiveCampaign Pro starts at $79/month. HubSpot Marketing Hub Professional starts at $800/month on annual billing, plus a required $3,000 onboarding fee.

We checked both vendors' live pricing pages and help docs on October 2, 2026, and cross-checked them against third-party reviews and user complaints. Here's how the two compare.

## The framing question: email tool or database?

Ask one question: **which system holds your record of the customer?**

If your sales team lives in the CRM, deals move through a pipeline, and marketing has to prove which campaigns produced pipeline, you are buying a database. HubSpot's main advantage is that marketing, sales and service all work from the same contact, company and deal records. Practitioner write-ups keep coming back to this point. An agency comparison from May 2026 (INSIDEA) puts it bluntly: HubSpot "wins decisively for any B2B team beyond solo founder" because ActiveCampaign's CRM is "light."

If revenue comes from email sequences (ecommerce flows, course launches, nurture for a service business) and the CRM is mostly a list of contacts with tags, you are buying an email tool. HubSpot's database does little for you there, and you would pay roughly ten times more for it.

Because the two products start from different places, the comparison feels lopsided. A martechspace analysis (Aug 2026) described it as comparing ActiveCampaign at $49 with a platform that costs $800 and "also includes email automation."

## What each one is in 2026

**HubSpot Marketing Hub** is one "Hub" in HubSpot's Customer Platform. The others are Sales, Service, Content, Data and Commerce. Paid plans have run on a seats-plus-credits model for a while now. You buy Core Seats, a marketing-contact tier, and you receive a monthly allowance of **HubSpot Credits** that pays for AI agents and usage-based features. In 2026 the AI work has centred on Breeze agents (Customer Agent, Prospecting Agent, Data Agent) and a new **HubSpot AEO** product that tracks how your brand shows up in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.

**ActiveCampaign** now calls itself an "autonomous marketing platform." Its AI layer is **Active Intelligence**. At the Spring 2026 keynote (April 8) it added "agent-to-user" AI, meaning the system proactively suggests actions based on performance signals. March 2026 updates let you build automations by chatting, with triggers from Calendly, Stripe, Salesforce, DocuSign, Facebook/Google/LinkedIn lead forms and more. You can also ask the Insights agent natural-language questions about contacts and campaign engagement. Underneath all that, ActiveCampaign is still mainly an automation builder. Most reviews, including critical ones, still say its visual workflow builder is the best in its price range.

## Pricing: the sticker vs the bill

### HubSpot Marketing Hub (live page, Oct 2, 2026)

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | Up to 2 users | — | — | — |
| Starter | $7/seat/mo promo; $20/seat list | Per seat | 500 | 1,000 | None |
| Professional | $800/mo annual ($890 monthly) | 3 Core Seats (+$45/seat) | 3,000 | 2,000 | **$3,000 required** |
| Enterprise | $3,600/mo (annual) | 5 Core Seats (+$75/seat) | 5,000 | 10,000 | **$7,000 required** |

Notes from reading the fine print:

- **The $7 Starter price is a promotion.** HubSpot advertises "save up to 65% on Starter" for new customers, "for a limited time." Budget on the $20/seat list price, since that's what you'll pay at renewal.
- **The Professional jump is very large.** Going from Starter at $20/seat to Professional at $800/month is about a 40x increase. This is where most HubSpot complaints come from, and Professional is where workflows, multi-channel campaigns and serious reporting become available.
- **Onboarding is required and charged once.** $3,000 for Professional and $7,000 for Enterprise. Reddit has several threads asking how to get the Enterprise fee waived. Common suggestions are buying through a Solutions Partner or negotiating. Third-party pricing guides also report that discounts of around 30% off list are common at Professional and above. Treat that as a negotiating position, not a promise.
- **Contacts are billed separately from seats.** Professional includes only 2,000 marketing contacts. Once you go over, you move to a higher contact tier, which HubSpot prices in its calculator rather than publishing a flat rate. A real Year 1 cost for a 3-seat Professional team with 10,000 marketing contacts is well above $800 × 12 + $3,000 = $12,600. Use HubSpot's calculator for your exact contact count before you sign.
- **AI runs on credits.** Customer Agent costs **50 credits ($0.50) per resolved conversation**. This replaced $1.00 per conversation on April 14, 2026, when HubSpot moved to outcome-based pricing. Prospecting Agent costs 100 credits ($1.00) per recommended lead. Data Agent costs 10 credits per answer. Extra credits run about **$10 per 1,000** according to third-party breakdowns. HubSpot's help docs (updated Sept 16, 2026) describe auto-upgrades and spend limits, so check those settings on day one.

## Automation and email

**ActiveCampaign wins on automation, and the gap is real.** Its builder handles branching, goals, wait-until conditions, split tests and site/event tracking with less setup than HubSpot workflows. From Plus upward, automation actions are unlimited, so a $49 account can run the same complexity of sequences as a $145 one. Predictive sending and conditional content start at Pro. The 2026 conversational builder ("create an automation that tags anyone who books on Calendly and pays via Stripe") is useful for routine setups. You'll still want to check the result in the visual builder.

**HubSpot's workflows are good, and they work across your whole data model.** A HubSpot workflow can act on deals, companies, tickets and custom objects (Enterprise), not just contacts. If your logic is "when a deal moves to Closed Won, enroll the contact's company in onboarding, notify the CSM and create a ticket," HubSpot handles it natively. ActiveCampaign would need Enhanced CRM or an integration.

**The Starter tiers aren't comparable.** ActiveCampaign Starter's **5-actions-per-automation** cap makes it a broadcast tool with a few autoresponders. HubSpot Starter doesn't include the workflows you'd buy HubSpot for. If automation matters to you, the real comparison is ActiveCampaign Plus/Pro against HubSpot Professional.

## CRM and reporting

HubSpot clearly wins here. Contacts, companies, deals, lifecycle stages, lead scoring, sequences (with Sales Hub), and attribution that connects marketing touches to closed revenue all live in one system. Its free CRM is still the most generous in the category.

That said, HubSpot's reporting isn't universally loved. An analysis of practitioner sentiment from 42 Agency (June 2026) found that above roughly $30M ARR, native attribution and reporting become a major frustration. Some teams now treat HubSpot as the "system of record, not a system of intelligence" and export to a warehouse.

ActiveCampaign's CRM handles a small sales team running a pipeline. Once you need company-level records, multiple pipelines with a lot of reporting, or support tickets tied to the same record, you'll start working around it.

## AI: credits vs bundled limits

- **HubSpot:** agents are metered. Professional's 3,000 included credits cover roughly 60 resolved Customer Agent conversations a month. Over that, you pay per resolution, per recommended lead or per answer. The April 2026 move to charging per resolved conversation is better for buyers, but the meter is now part of your bill. HubSpot AEO costs $45–$50/month standalone and is included in Professional and Enterprise. That's genuinely useful as search moves toward AI answers.
- **ActiveCampaign:** Active Intelligence is included in every plan, marked "limits apply" on Starter and Plus. You don't buy credits, but you also don't get published per-action limits. Its agents focus on marketing work: drafting campaigns, segmenting, building automations, answering reporting questions. It has nothing comparable to HubSpot's Customer Agent for support deflection.

Our view: if you want an AI agent answering customer chats and email, HubSpot has the product and ActiveCampaign doesn't. If you want AI that speeds up building campaigns, both are capable, and ActiveCampaign's is cheaper to access.

## Pros and cons

### HubSpot Marketing Hub

**Pros**
- One shared database across marketing, sales and service, with the strongest CRM at this price level
- Generous free CRM, and Starter is cheap (especially on the promo)
- Customer Agent with outcome-based pricing at $0.50 per resolution
- HubSpot AEO included in Professional and Enterprise
- Easy to learn compared with Salesforce or Marketo, consistently its top-rated trait in reviews

**Cons**
- **Steep jump from Starter to Professional.** $20/seat to $800/month, plus **$3,000 required onboarding** ($7,000 on Enterprise).
- **Contact-tier pricing penalizes list growth.** Professional includes only 2,000 marketing contacts.
- **The real bill has several parts:** seats + contact tier + credits + onboarding. A RealCostLabs review (June 2026) called the gap between advertised and actual price the most common complaint.
- **Advanced reporting and attribution frustrate larger teams.** Many export to a warehouse.
- **Starter pricing is a promotion**, so expect a higher renewal.

### ActiveCampaign

**Pros**
- Best automation builder for the money; unlimited actions from $49
- AI included in every plan with no separate credit system
- 30-day money-back guarantee and free migration
- Strong ecommerce integrations and conditional content on Pro
- Low cost at small list sizes

**Cons**
- **Billing for contacts you can't email.** Several 2026 reviews (SaaS Scored, Stackcut, StartupOwl) report that since November 2025, newer accounts are billed for *all* contacts, including unsubscribed and bounced ones. Clean your list before you migrate.
- **Price increases.** Reviews cite long-time customers seeing bills roughly double over a few years without a plan change. Trustpilot sits around 2.8/5 (per SaaS Scored, March 2026).
- **Starter is too limited for real automation.** Five actions per automation.
- **The CRM is basic.** B2B teams with real sales processes will outgrow it.
- **Big jumps between contact bands.** Third-party math shows Plus going from roughly $59 to $179/month between 1K and 5K contacts on monthly billing.
- **Support quality varies by plan.** Reviewers say it depends on your tier and the rep you get.

## Who should pick which

**Pick ActiveCampaign if:**
- Email is your main revenue channel: ecommerce, info products, coaching, local services with nurture sequences.
- Your "sales team" is a few people, or no one.
- You want sophisticated automations without spending $12,600 in the first year.
- You can keep your list clean. Given the contact-billing change, list hygiene now directly affects your bill.

**Pick HubSpot if:**
- You're B2B, have a sales team, and need marketing attribution that connects to closed deals.
- You plan to add Sales Hub or Service Hub. The shared database is the main reason to choose HubSpot.
- You want an AI agent handling customer conversations, billed per resolution.
- You have budget for Professional plus onboarding, and you'll negotiate. Talk to a Solutions Partner before you sign.

**Run both if:** you're a B2B company whose sales team is already in HubSpot (free or Sales Hub) but whose email automation needs exceed HubSpot Starter. ActiveCampaign's native HubSpot integration lets you keep HubSpot as the record and ActiveCampaign as the email engine. At Plus or Pro that's often cheaper than upgrading to Marketing Hub Professional. The downside is two systems syncing data, and therefore two sources of disagreement.

**Start with HubSpot Free/Starter if:** you're unsure. It costs almost nothing, and moving up the tiers within HubSpot is easier than migrating platforms later. Just don't assume Starter includes workflows. Professional is where those become available.

## Bottom line

Don't decide between HubSpot and ActiveCampaign on email features. They're close enough on email that the difference won't matter. Decide on where your customer record lives.

If your CRM is the core of the business, HubSpot is worth paying for, and the onboarding fee is the price of getting a system that large set up correctly. If your email list is the core of the business, ActiveCampaign gives you the better automation engine for a small fraction of the cost. Just audit your contact count before every renewal.
