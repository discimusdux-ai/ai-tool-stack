Every "best AI tools for small business" list has the same problem: it treats a three-person company like a small enterprise. Buy the assistant, buy the writer, buy the meeting notetaker, buy the CRM, buy the automation platform, buy the design tool. Seven subscriptions, three seats each, and you have quietly built a $600/month software bill to support a business that bills maybe $40k a month.

There is no single winner here, because "small business" is not a category — it is a set of jobs. What there *is* is a right order to buy in, and a surprising number of AI features you are already paying for and not using.

## The verdict, by job

| Job to be done | Our pick | What it costs (3 people) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thinking, writing, analysis | ChatGPT Business | $60/mo annual ($75 monthly) |
| Email, docs, storage — with AI included | Google Workspace Business Standard | $42/mo annual ($50.40 monthly) |

## The question nobody asks first: is this a *new* tool, or a feature you already bought?

Before you compare any two AI products, answer one binary question about each job on your list: **does this job need a new vendor, or is it already sitting unused inside software you pay for?**

In 2026 that question decides most of the budget. Google folded Gemini into every Workspace tier in 2025 and raised the base price instead of selling a $20 add-on — so if you pay for Workspace, you already own AI in Gmail, Docs and Sheets. HubSpot ships Breeze credits with the free CRM. QuickBooks includes Intuit Assist at no extra charge on every plan. Canva includes a monthly allowance of AI generations in Pro.

Most small businesses that feel "behind on AI" are not missing tools. They are paying for four AI products whose jobs overlap, while the AI inside their accounting and CRM sits switched off. Work the list job by job, buy the standalone tool only where the built-in version is genuinely worse, and the stack gets cheap fast.

## 1. ChatGPT Business — the one seat-based subscription worth defending

**Best for:** the general-purpose thinking, drafting and analysis job
**Price:** Standard seats $25/user/month, or $20/user/month billed annually. Premium seats, added 10 August 2026, cost $125/user/month monthly or $100 annually and include 5x the usage with no 5-hour limit. You can mix seat types in one workspace.

This is the tool that replaces the widest range of small tasks: proposal drafts, contract summaries, spreadsheet formulas, customer email triage, "explain what this invoice dispute actually means." Buying it as Business rather than three individual Plus seats gets you a shared workspace, admin controls, and data that is not used for training by default.

The premium-seat launch matters for budgeting reasons more than product ones. Standard seats carry a 5-hour rolling usage limit; heavy users hit it, and the fix OpenAI now sells is a 5x seat at five times the price. For a three-person business the honest configuration is three standard seats at $60/month annual and an acceptance that one person may occasionally wait.

**Where it breaks**
- The 5-hour rolling limit on standard seats is real, and the upgrade path from $20 to $100 per seat has nothing in between.
- Codex/coding allowances on Business seats are widely reported (OpenAI developer forum, June 2026) as less durable than on a personal Plus plan — a genuine downgrade if your "business" is a dev shop.
- Annual billing is where the $20 price lives; month-to-month is $25, and mixed-seat workspaces make invoices harder to read than they need to be.

## 2. Google Workspace Business Standard — AI you are probably already buying

**Best for:** email, docs, storage, video calls, and the AI attached to all of them
**Price:** Business Starter $7/user/month annual ($8.40 monthly), Business Standard $14 ($16.80), Business Plus $22 ($26.40). Gemini features are included in the plan price — the standalone $20 Gemini add-on was retired when Google raised base prices in 2025.

For three people, Business Standard is $42/month on annual commitment. That buys 2TB per user, Meet recordings, and Gemini in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides and Meet.

The catch is quotas. Google publishes AI usage limits per edition on its Workspace Help pages, and they are specific: single-digit monthly allowances for some features, per-day caps for others (Drive audio overviews at 20/day on one tier, 40 on another), with an "AI Expanded Access" add-on that multiplies each quota by roughly 5x. Enforcement for Drive's "organize files" feature — previously promotional and unmetered — starts 15 August 2026. Google also states explicitly that limits are not pooled across your team, so one heavy user cannot borrow another's headroom.

**Where it breaks**
- The per-edition AI quotas are low enough that daily heavy users will hit them, and the only fix is a paid add-on per seat.
- Google reserves the right to change limits when usage spikes — stated plainly in its own business FAQ.
- Gemini in Docs is still a weaker writer than ChatGPT or Claude on anything structurally complex; it wins on context (your mail, your files), not on prose.

## 3. Canva — the design job, and a pricing page that contradicts itself

**Best for:** social posts, decks, one-pagers, simple video, brand consistency without a designer
**Price:** Free (genuinely usable). Pro is quoted at **US$180/year on Canva's own US pricing page and US$144/year on its UK-facing page** as of August 2026 — same product, same currency, two numbers. Business is $25/user/month or $250/user/year with no seat minimum.

Canva's free tier remains the single best free product in this roundup, and for a three-person business that publishes a few posts a week, one Pro seat plus two free accounts is a defensible setup at roughly $12–15/month.

Note the pricing history before you commit annually. Canva moved Teams from a flat $119.99/year for five users to per-seat pricing in 2024 — around a 317% increase for a five-person team — raised its top tier from $15 to $20/month in July 2026, and renamed Teams to Business. Third-party trackers logged 13 pricing changes. SMB spend data (Spendhound, February 2026) put year-over-year Canva cost growth for small business at roughly 69%.

**Where it breaks**
- Two live vendor pages quote different Pro prices; always screenshot what you were charged.
- AI generations are metered per plan and the meter has been repackaged twice, most recently behind a separate AI Pass add-on.
- Repeated, steep price increases with short notice are the dominant complaint theme; annual prepayment is protection for one year only.

## 4. Fireflies Pro — meeting notes without the per-minute anxiety

**Best for:** capturing calls when nobody in the company has time to write notes
**Price:** Free tier; Pro $18/seat monthly or $10/seat annually; Business $29 ($19 annual); Enterprise $39.

Fireflies is the pick over Otter for a small team on pure arithmetic: transcription and summaries never consume credits (credits are only for AI Skills and the Assistant), and Pro is $10/seat annually against Otter Pro's $16.99 monthly / $8.33 annual with a 1,200-minute cap that was cut from 6,000 at the same price. Otter also gives you no minute rollover.

Both vendors have been quietly tightening. Fireflies added a 200 hours/month ceiling to its "unlimited" Pro plan in July 2026.

**Where it breaks**
- "Unlimited" now means 200 hours a month on Pro — fine for three people, not fine for a call center.
- A December 2025 Illinois BIPA class action over voice data is pending; if you operate in Illinois, read it before rolling out auto-join.
- Auto-joining bots create consent problems with clients. Turn auto-join off and invite deliberately.

## 5. Make Core — automation at a price a small business can absorb

**Best for:** connecting the tools above so nobody re-types anything
**Price:** Free 1,000 credits/month; Core $9/month annual; Pro $16; Teams $29 — all with 10,000 base credits on paid tiers. Zapier's comparable Starter is $19.99/month annual for 750 tasks; Professional $49 for 2,000.

Per unit of work, Make is roughly an order of magnitude cheaper, and its free tier is a real plan rather than a demo (1,000 operations vs Zapier's 100 single-step tasks). One shared Make account at $9/month covers the automation needs of most three-person businesses.

Two honest caveats we have written before and will keep writing: Make renamed operations to credits on 27 August 2025, and polls, filters, iterators and failed retries all consume credits, with a hard stop at zero. It is entirely possible for "Make is 13x cheaper per operation" and "our bill went up after migrating from Zapier" to both be true. Zapier's advantage is coverage — 9,000+ apps against Make's 3,000+ — and its overage behaves gracefully at 1.25x, capped at 3x, where Make simply stops.

**Where it breaks**
- Credit consumption on polling scenarios is the classic bill shock; use webhooks wherever the app supports them.
- Hard stop at zero credits means a scenario can silently die mid-month.
- The visual builder is more powerful than Zapier's and correspondingly harder for a non-technical owner to maintain.

## 6. HubSpot — start free, and know exactly where the cliff is

**Best for:** contact records, deal pipeline, and marketing email in one place
**Price:** Free CRM up to 2 users. Starter Customer Platform $20/seat/month, $15 annual, with promotional first-year rates around $7–9/seat. Professional starts around $800–$890/month plus a mandatory onboarding fee reported at $3,000.

For most three-person businesses the free CRM plus its included Breeze AI credits (500/month on Starter tiers) is enough for a year. When you outgrow it, Starter at $45/month for three annual seats is fair value: 1,000 marketing contacts, 5,000 marketing emails a month, two deal pipelines.

What you must know going in is the shape of the ladder. The jump from Starter to Professional is roughly $20 to $800+ per month with nothing between — the single loudest complaint in HubSpot's own community. In 2026 HubSpot also capped Starter at five core seats (extra users become view-only), and moved quoting out of Sales Starter into a separately-priced Commerce Hub seat, which caught existing customers mid-contract.

**Where it breaks**
- No middle tier: the Starter→Professional cliff is a 40x price step plus onboarding fees.
- Features have migrated out of Starter into paid seats after purchase (quotes being the notorious case).
- AI credits are capped at 500/month on Starter and expire monthly; the credit model changes often enough that budgeting a year ahead is guesswork.

## 7. QuickBooks + Intuit Assist — the AI you are least likely to switch off

**Best for:** bookkeeping, invoicing, expense categorization
**Price:** Solopreneur $20/month, Simple Start $38, Essentials $75 (3 users), Plus $115, Advanced $275. Intuit Assist is included on every plan at no extra cost.

Intuit raised prices 15–20% across the board in 2026 (Essentials $65→$75, Advanced $235→$275), and bundled its AI in rather than charging separately — the same play Google made with Gemini. For a three-person business, Simple Start at $38 usually suffices until you need multi-user access, at which point Essentials at $75 is the real number.

Treat Intuit's efficiency claims carefully. The widely-quoted 95% categorization accuracy, "12 hours saved per month" and "invoices paid five days faster" figures all originate in Intuit's own July 2025 launch materials and have not been independently verified.

**Where it breaks**
- Vendor-stated accuracy numbers with no third-party audit.
- Assist overwrites user defaults in places — the invoice subject-line replacement complaint has been open in Intuit's community since 2025.
- Query limits on the included AI are low; Xero's equivalent is a $5–10/user add-on with higher allowances.

## Sidebar: the writing tools we did not put on this list

Dedicated AI writing platforms have repositioned hard toward "AI search visibility," and the prices moved with them. [Writesonic](/blog/writesonic-review-2026) — where we do hold an active affiliate relationship (affiliates.writesonic.com) — now runs $79/$199/$399 per month annually, or $99/$249/$499 month-to-month, for 1, 2 and 3 users respectively. That is a legitimate spend if ranking inside ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews is a core channel for you. It is not a general-purpose small business writing tool at that price, and we would rather tell you that than sell you the click. Jasper's affiliate program has been dead since January 2025, which is worth remembering whenever you see it topping a "best AI writer" list.

Similarly, Microsoft 365 Copilot Business went to $21/user/month on 1 July 2026, with Business Standard + Copilot bundled at $23.50 and Business Premium + Copilot at $32, annual commitment only — no month-to-month. If you are already a Microsoft shop, that bundle is the honest comparison to Google Workspace Business Standard at $14. If you are not, Copilot is not a reason to become one.

## What we'd actually buy

**The $89/month starter stack (solo or two founders, revenue under $20k/mo)**
Google Workspace Business Standard, 2 seats ($28) + ChatGPT Business, 1 seat ($20) + Canva Pro, 1 seat ($15) + Make Free ($0) + HubSpot Free ($0) + QuickBooks Simple Start ($38 — minus one Workspace seat if you are truly solo). Everything else waits.

**The $179/month working stack (three people, actual customers)**
Workspace Standard x3 ($42) + ChatGPT Business x3 ($60) + Canva Pro x1 ($15) + Fireflies Pro x1 ($10) + Make Core ($9) + HubSpot Free ($0) + QuickBooks Essentials ($75 — three users). That is under $180/month for a fully AI-equipped three-person business, and it is roughly what we would defend line by line to a skeptical accountant.

**The $310/month growth stack (three people, sales-led, marketing calendar)**
Workspace Standard x3 ($42) + ChatGPT Business x3 ($60) + Canva Business x2 ($42) + Fireflies Pro x3 ($30) + Make Pro ($16) + HubSpot Starter x3 ($45) + QuickBooks Essentials ($75). Add a premium ChatGPT seat only when someone can name the specific week they hit the limit.

## The bottom line

The three-person AI stack that works costs about $180/month, and more than half of that is Workspace and QuickBooks — infrastructure you would buy with or without AI. The AI-specific spend is closer to $90.

Buy in this order: the general assistant first, because it improves every other job. Then the automation glue, because $9 removes hours. Then meetings, if you take a lot of calls. CRM stays free until a deal falls through a crack. Design stays at one seat until two people are genuinely designing at once. And before you buy anything, check whether the vendor already gave it to you and raised the base price to pay for it — in 2026, three of the seven tools here did exactly that.
