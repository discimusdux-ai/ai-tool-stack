Fliki and InVideo AI land on the same shortlist for a simple reason: you type a script or an idea, and each one hands back a finished video with narration, visuals, captions and music. Both start around $20-28 a month. Both bill through a monthly credit allowance that doesn't roll over.

But the two meters measure different things, and that's the whole comparison. Ask one question before you open either pricing page: **are you paying for the voice, or for the footage?**

- **Fliki prices the voice.** Its credit guide bills narration by the minute (0.25 to 2 credits depending on the voice), charges 0 credits for stock or uploaded media, and adds 0.1 credits per exported minute. A narrated video over stock footage is cheap. Generated clips and avatars are where the meter jumps.
- **InVideo prices the footage.** Since its November 2026 relaunch around Agent Two, InVideo is a front end to third-party generation models: Seedance 2.5 and 2.0, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1, Nano Banana Pro. Each generated clip is priced by model, resolution and length. Edits and exports on its timeline are free; the generations are what you pay for.

If narration carries your video (faceless YouTube, narrated blog posts, explainers, multilingual social clips), the meter favours Fliki. If the footage carries it (ads, product teasers, narrative shorts that need shots that don't exist in any stock library), InVideo is the tool built to make and cut that footage. The rest of this article puts numbers on that.

## What each one actually is in late 2026

**Fliki** is a voice-first text-to-video tool. You start from an idea, a script, a blog URL, or a PowerPoint/Google Slides file. Fliki splits the script into scenes, matches a stock clip or image to each, puts an AI narrator over it, and adds word-by-word captions. On top of that it has stock avatars, voice cloning, custom characters, a Series feature for planning episodes on a posting cadence, and an AI Playground (added January 2026) for testing generation models before you spend credits on a full video. Generated AI video clips exist, but only from Premium up.

**InVideo AI** has rebuilt itself twice in a year. The v4 product of early 2026 ("Agent One", the Magic Box chat editor) was replaced in November 2026 by **Agent Two**, which comes in three intelligence levels: Lite, Pro and Ultra. In August, InVideo added **Slate**, an editor inside Agent Two that can assemble approved shots into a sequence on command. Behind the agent sits a menu of outside models. Sora 2 Pro was on that list until OpenAI closed the Sora API on 24 September 2026, so check that any model you're buying InVideo *for* is still on the menu.

The practical difference shows up in a test GenMediaLab ran (updated 18 November 2026): the same 115-word script went through both free plans. Fliki split it into ten scenes and exported a 48-second watermarked 720p video using 0.8 of its 3 monthly credits. InVideo routed the script through Agent Two, recorded narration and started picking footage, then **ran out of its 10 free credits before assembling a final cut**. That's one test on free plans, but it is a fair picture of how each meter behaves.

## Pros and cons

### Fliki

**Pros**
- **Cheapest per finished narrated video.** Stock media is free and the voice rate is the same on every plan, so cost per video is predictable before you hit Generate.
- **Fast from text you already have.** Blog-to-video and slides-to-video are one paste, not a chat session.
- **Voice library depth.** 1,000+ voices on Standard is more than most faceless creators will ever audition.
- **Stable pricing.** The $28/$88 ladder has held all year.

**Where it breaks**
- **Credits evaporate on revisions.** A Capterra reviewer (Feb 2026) spent 6 credits on one 1.5-minute video; G2 reviewers say previews and re-renders while experimenting draw credits, not just the final export.
- **Stock matching is literal.** In GenMediaLab's test, four of ten scenes got a clip matched on a single word ("cut back on water" got a wall fountain). Plan to swap clips by hand.
- **Generation glitches cost you.** Trustpilot reviewers in September and October 2026 report failed script-to-video runs, unrequested on-screen text after an update, and 24h+ support waits; a G2 reviewer says Fliki refused to restore credits lost to voices skipping words.
- **AI video clips are Premium-only.** If you need generated footage regularly, you're looking at $66-88 a month, and the most expensive models burn 180 credits in about a minute.

### InVideo AI

**Pros**
- **Many frontier video models behind one subscription.** Seedance 2.5, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1 and Nano Banana Pro, billed at what InVideo says is the models' original API pricing.
- **A real editor.** Multitrack timeline, color and audio tools, multiplayer editing, and project export to Premiere, Final Cut and Resolve. Edits and exports cost no credits.
- **Team economics on Plus.** Ten free guest seats with 190 credits each is generous for a small agency.
- **Lowest sticker price.** Starter at $10/month yearly is the cheapest way in, if Lite and Seedance 2.0 Fast are enough.

**Where it breaks**
- **The agent spends credits while thinking.** InVideo's own help page says each Agent Two response may be reasoning, generating images, clips and voice at once, and each action draws credits. Trustpilot reviewers report 750 and 2,500 credits gone with no usable video (Sept-Oct 2026). Turn on "ask before generating" and set a per-project credit cap before your first brief.
- **The refund window is tiny.** InVideo's current Terms of Service (section 3(d)) allow a refund request **within 24 hours** of purchase or renewal, and only if no credits or premium features were used. The August 2026 version said seven days. Generating one test video forfeits the refund.
- **Monthly vs annual checkout mistakes.** Multiple November 2026 Trustpilot reviews describe being charged for a year when they meant to buy a month ($126 upfront in one case), with refunds slow or refused. SaaS Critique (21 Nov 2026) summarises InVideo's Trustpilot as 1.8/5 across 1,024 reviews with 32% one-star.
- **Pricing churn.** Eight pricing changes this year. A plan you budget for in November may not be the plan that renews.

## Who should pick which

**Pick Fliki if** your videos are mostly a voice over stock footage, images or slides: faceless YouTube, narrated blog posts, course explainers, multilingual social clips. Standard at $21/month yearly (about $14 with the Black Friday code while it lasts) covers dozens of narrated videos a month. Start on the free plan, make one video with the voice you'd actually use, and check its color band before you upgrade.

**Pick InVideo if** the footage is the product: ads, product teasers, narrative shorts, anything that needs shots no stock library has. Plus at $30/month yearly is the real entry point, because Starter can't reach Seedance 2.5. Budget around 100 credits per 5-second 1080p clip, cap credits per project, and buy monthly first so you aren't locked into a year under a 24-hour refund window.

**Pay for both if** you run a faceless channel that wants a few generated hero shots per video. Generate the 3-4 key clips in InVideo, then build the narrated body in Fliki with free stock. At yearly prices that's about $51/month ($21 + $30), and each tool does only what it's cheap at.

**Pick neither if** you need a presenter on screen; that's HeyGen or Synthesia territory (see our [Fliki vs HeyGen comparison](/blog/fliki-vs-heygen-2026)). If you mostly cut clips from long recordings, a clipping tool like OpusClip is a better fit.

## Bottom line

Fliki and InVideo both promise "script in, video out", but they charge for different halves of the video. Fliki's credit buys **a minute of narration** over free stock. InVideo's credit buys **a fraction of a second of generated footage** from someone else's model. Decide which half your videos depend on, then read the meter for that half.

For narrated, faceless content, Fliki Standard is the cheaper, more predictable tool and the one we'd buy. InVideo earns its place when you need generated footage and a real timeline, as long as you set credit caps on day one and accept that its prices will probably change again before your renewal.
