Most "Loom vs Descript" comparisons start in the wrong place: they line up two price ladders and declare a winner on cost per month. That framing fails within a week of real use, because these tools do not compete for the same minute of your day.

The question that actually decides this is simpler and more brutal: **does your video get watched once, or does it get published?**

If the answer is *watched once* — a bug walkthrough, a client update, a "here's why the invoice looks like that" — you want the recording on someone's screen in ninety seconds and you will never open it again. That's Loom. If the answer is *published* — a podcast episode, a YouTube video, a course module, a webinar cut into clips — the recording is raw material and the work starts after you stop talking. That's Descript.

Almost everyone who researches this comparison already knows which one they are. What they don't know is what each tool now costs in 2026, and both vendors changed their billing in ways that punish the assumptions you probably still hold.

## Quick verdict

| If you need | Winner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Record and send in under a minute | **Loom** | One click to a shareable link with view tracking |
| Cut a 60-minute recording into a finished episode | **Descript** | Edit the transcript, the media follows |
| Cheapest usable free tier | **Loom** | 25 videos, 5-minute cap, no watermark tax on sharing |
| Removing "um" and dead air | **Both** — Descript wins on control | Loom's is a toggle; Descript's is reviewable |
| Multi-person teams sending internal video | **Loom** | Per-seat, workspace analytics, comment threads |

| Predictable monthly bill | **Loom** | Flat per seat; Descript meters two separate currencies |
| Never trusting a vendor with your archive | **Descript** | Local project files and exports, not a link-hosted library |

## What each one actually is

Loom is a video *messaging* tool. The unit of work is a link. You hit record, talk over your screen, stop, and the URL is already in your clipboard — the upload finishes in the background. Everything downstream (comments, emoji reactions, view counts, who watched how much) exists to replace a meeting or a long Slack thread. Atlassian bought it for roughly $975 million in late 2023, and in 2026 it is being absorbed into Atlassian's billing and identity platform. That absorption is the single most important fact about Loom this year, and we'll get to why.

Descript is an *editor*. It transcribes what you import or record, then lets you edit the media by editing the text: delete a sentence in the transcript and the corresponding audio and video disappear. Around that core sit multitrack editing, Studio Sound cleanup, filler-word removal, voice cloning, green screen, eye contact correction, and Underlord — Descript's agentic co-editor, which in 2026 runs on a model picker that includes Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 and Sonnet 5 and Google's Gemini line. The August 5, 2026 release added scheduled recording Rooms, AI YouTube thumbnails, and filler-word removal in Spanish, German, French, Portuguese, and Italian. The April 2026 release put the Descript API into open beta and made uploads resume after a network drop.

That release cadence tells you something the feature tables don't: Descript is still being built as a production tool. Loom's 2026 changelog is mostly about Jira, Confluence, and Atlassian identity.

## The price ladder

Both published prices below are per-user, and both vendors quote monthly and annual rates on the same page. Read the billing view carefully — annual is the number the marketing site defaults to.

**Loom (per user / month)**

| Plan | Monthly | Annual (billed yearly) | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | $0 | $0 | 25 videos per person, 5-minute recording cap, unlimited screenshots, transcripts, auto titles |
| Business | $18 | ~$15 (17% saving) | Unlimited recordings and length, trim/stitch editing, custom branding, engagement analytics |
| Business + AI | $24 | ~$20 | Everything in Business plus the AI suite: summaries, chapters, tasks, AI workflows, filler-word and silence removal, edit by transcript, auto meeting notes |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom | SSO/SCIM, advanced content privacy, custom retention, Salesforce integration |

Loom advertises "save up to 17%" with annual billing but does not publish a per-user annual figure on the pricing page — confirm the exact total in checkout before you budget against it. Note also *who* gets billed: on Business and Business + AI, pricing is based on Creators and admins; on Enterprise it's based on Members and admins.

**Descript (per person / month)**

| Plan | Monthly | Annual | Media minutes | AI credits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | $0 | 60 min/month | 100 (one-time, not monthly) |
| Hobbyist | $24 | $16 | 600 min (10 hrs)/month | 400/month |
| Creator | $35 | $24 | 1,800 min (30 hrs)/month | 800/month |
| Business | $65 | $50 | 2,400 min (40 hrs)/month | 1,500/month |

Annual Creator and Business subscribers get a one-time bonus top-up (500 credits + 5 media hours on Creator; 1,000 credits + 10 hours on Business), valid for a year.

On sticker price alone, a solo user pays $16–24/month for Descript versus $15–20/month for Loom. That looks close. It isn't, because only one of these bills is fixed.

## The meter is the price, not the sticker

Descript re-metered in the 2025 pricing overhaul, replacing straightforward transcription hours with **two** separate currencies:

- **Media minutes** count every minute of media you *import or record* into Descript — whether or not you transcribe it. Bring in a three-camera shoot and you're billed three times over for the same hour of content.
- **AI credits** are spent on Underlord, Studio Sound, filler-word removal, green screen, eye contact, AI speech, avatars, and generated video. Features that felt free on old paid plans now decrement a balance.

Run out and you buy top-ups, which are only available on Creator and Business (not Free or Hobbyist), are shared at the Drive level, and expire 12 months after purchase. Current top-up pricing: AI credits at $35 / 350, $80 / 1,000, $200 / 4,000; media minutes at $25 / 5 hours, $80 / 20 hours, $150 / 50 hours. Purchased top-ups are only consumed after your monthly allowance is gone.

The predictable consequence is bill shock, and it's the loudest complaint against Descript in 2026. One Trustpilot reviewer on the Creator plan reported burning through a month's credits after three podcast episodes "with little to no AI usage." Long-time subscribers who paid a steady ~$30/month for years describe much larger charges once a busy production cycle blows past the new limits.

Loom's meter is different and, for now, gentler: seats. But Atlassian is tightening that too.

## The Atlassian problem

If you are shopping Loom in 2026, this section matters more than any feature.

**Creator Lite is being eliminated.** Loom historically had a free seat type inside paid workspaces — people who could watch, comment, and record in a limited way at no cost. Per Atlassian's own migration documentation, that role is discontinued: existing Creator Lite users are converted to full paid Creator seats on your workspace's integration date, with a grace period until your next billing date to deactivate anyone you don't want to pay for. Miss the window and they're on the invoice. Workspaces created after February 2026 never get Creator Lite at all.

**The free tier shrank for integrated accounts.** Loom's Starter plan still lists "up to 50 members," but Atlassian's Starter plan FAQ states that if your account has been integrated with Atlassian systems — automatic if you signed up after February 2026 — you are limited to **10 users** on the free plan.

**The migration has been rough.** Trustpilot reviews through 2026 cluster around the same failures: uploads stuck in "Uploading," Google sign-in broken after the switch to Atlassian identity, out-of-sync audio, lost recording progress, and refund requests declined on annual renewals. One Atlassian community thread from a paying annual customer describes losing access to their entire recording library after the identity migration, with no recovery path offered. That's a single report, not a pattern — but it's a single report about the thing Loom is supposed to be good at.

Also worth knowing before you commit: users migrating from legacy pricing report jumping from $10 to $18 per month, and Atlassian's support documentation is explicit that you cannot stay on old pricing.

## Where each one wins on capability

**Loom's real wins.** Time-to-link is the whole product and nothing else matches it — Descript's project loading alone exceeds Loom's entire record-and-share cycle. Engagement analytics (who watched, how far) is a genuine sales and support feature Descript simply doesn't have. And Loom AI, on Business + AI, is more useful than the marketing suggests: AI workflows turn a recording into an SOP, step-by-step guide, PR description, QA steps, or a Jira/Linear bug ticket with steps-to-reproduce prefilled. If your team lives in Jira, that's a real workflow, not a demo. Loom AI now also runs on externally uploaded video, not just Loom recordings. Auto titles are free on every plan, including Starter.

**Descript's real wins.** Transcript editing is still the fastest model on the market for spoken-word content, and nothing Loom offers is in the same category — Loom's "edit by transcript" is trimming; Descript's is editing. Multitrack, Studio Sound, and real export control mean the output is a finished asset you own, not a hosted link. Underlord's 2026 additions (letterboxing detection, previews before generation, memory and custom instructions, reasoning models for multi-step edits) push it from novelty toward something that saves an hour per episode. And the Drive-level Media Library added in March 2026 lets you reuse files across projects *without re-spending media minutes* — the single most valuable cost-control feature in the product.

## Where each one breaks

**Loom**

1. **Reliability regressed after the Atlassian migration.** Stuck uploads, login loops, and A/V sync drift are the dominant complaint themes across Trustpilot and Atlassian's own community forums. For a tool whose entire pitch is frictionless speed, this is existential, not cosmetic.
2. **The free-seat economics changed against you.** Creator Lite is gone, and integrated free workspaces are capped at 10 users. Audit your member list *before* your integration date.
3. **Refund policy is inflexible.** Multiple reviewers report annual charges applied without clear warning and refunds declined outright.
4. **Editing is shallow by design.** Trim, stitch, filler-word removal. If you need b-roll, layered audio, or precise cuts, you'll export and finish elsewhere.
5. **AI is gated behind the top self-serve tier.** Summaries, chapters, and workflows require Business + AI at $24/user/month, and summaries and chapters only apply to videos recorded *after* you buy.

**Descript**

1. **Two meters, no forecast.** Media minutes bill on import — including footage you never transcribe or use — and AI credits drain on operations that used to be included. Budgeting is genuinely hard.
2. **Top-ups are gated.** They're unavailable on Free and Hobbyist, so the cheapest paid plan is also the one you can't rescue mid-project without a full upgrade.
3. **Transcription still needs a human pass.** Proper nouns, brand names, technical jargon, and non-American accents are where errors cluster. Anything client-facing gets proofread.
4. **Performance limits are real.** It's desktop-and-Chrome-shaped, there's no meaningful mobile editing, and large multitrack projects lag on lighter machines.
5. **Legacy plan confusion persists.** If you subscribed before the overhaul, legacy and sunset plans meter differently and can't buy top-ups — read your plan page before you renew.

## Who buys which

**Buy Loom** if video is *communication*: async standups, customer walkthroughs, support replies, design handoffs. Start on Starter and stay there if you record under 25 videos and nothing runs past five minutes. Go Business ($18/user, ~$15 annual) the moment the 5-minute cap bites. Only add AI ($24/$20) if your team files Jira or Linear tickets from recordings — that's the feature that pays for the $6 gap; summaries alone don't.

**Buy Descript** if video is *product*: podcast, YouTube, courses, client deliverables. Skip Hobbyist unless you publish rarely — 10 media hours goes fast and you can't buy top-ups on it. Creator at $24 annual is the tier that matters, and the annual bonus (500 credits + 5 hours) effectively covers your first busy month. Business at $50 is for teams sharing a Drive pool.

**Buy both** — and a surprising number of solo operators do — if you send client updates *and* publish content. Loom Starter (free) plus Descript Creator ($24 annual) is $288/year and covers both jobs properly. That beats forcing either tool to do the other's work, which is the actual mistake most people make.

## Bottom line

Loom is the better *tool* and the worse *vendor* right now. The product still does the fastest thing in async video, and Atlassian is actively making it more expensive, less free, and — by the weight of user reports — less reliable. If you're on legacy Loom pricing, audit your seats before your integration date; if you're evaluating fresh, price it at $18–24 per person and assume the free-seat cushion no longer exists.

Descript is the worse *bargain* and the better *investment*. Its billing model is the most confusing in the category and will occasionally produce a bill you didn't plan for. But it's the only one of the two still shipping serious capability, and it produces assets you own rather than links you rent.

If you're forced to pick one and your video is watched once, pick Loom and stay on the free tier as long as you can. If your video gets published, pick Descript Creator on annual billing, turn on the Media Library, and treat media minutes like a budget rather than a background process.
