Most people searching for an OpusClip alternative aren't unhappy with the clips. They're unhappy with the meter. OpusClip charges one credit per minute of *source* video, so a two-hour weekly podcast burns 120 credits before you've kept a single clip. If you're on Starter's 150 credits, that's most of your month gone after one episode.

So the real question isn't "which clipper is smartest?" They all find a decent hook in a talking-head video now. The question is **what are you paying per: source minute, clip generated, or finished video?** Get that right and the cheapest tool for you becomes obvious. Get it wrong and you'll switch tools and hit the same wall a month later.

We compared six alternatives against OpusClip on that axis. Pricing comes from vendor pricing pages and help docs, checked against third-party trackers in October–November 2026. Where they disagree, we say so.

## The framing: what's your meter?

Every AI clipper sells the same demo: drop in a long video, get ten vertical clips with captions. Underneath, they charge on three different units, and that difference matters more than any feature list.

**Per source minute (OpusClip, Vizard, Descript).** You pay for what you upload, whether you keep zero clips or thirty. Great if your source is dense (an interview where every five minutes has a moment). Expensive if you upload three-hour streams with 40 minutes of dead air.

**Per clip generated (Klap).** You pay for output. A three-hour stream and a 20-minute video cost the same if both yield ten clips. Great for long, sparse footage. Worse if you like to generate 30 candidates and keep four.

**Per video / bundled (Submagic, CapCut, Riverside).** Submagic counts videos and caps their length. CapCut and Riverside fold clipping into a bigger subscription. Their meter is really the *other* thing you're paying for (editing, recording).

Here's the practical math for a creator doing one 90-minute podcast a week (~6 hours of source a month):

- **OpusClip:** 360 credits needed. Pro gives 300. You're short, or buying a second pack.
- **Vizard:** 360 of 600 minutes on Creator. Comfortable.
- **Klap:** ~40 clips if you take ten per episode. Basic (100 clips) covers it.
- **Riverside:** free, if you recorded it in Riverside.

The meter is the price, not the sticker. Keep that in mind as you read the list.

## 1. OpusClip (the benchmark)

**Best for:** podcasters and YouTubers clipping under ~5 hours of source per month who want the best automatic hook selection.
**Price:** Free (60 credits/mo, watermark) · Starter $15/mo, monthly billing only, 150 credits · Pro $29/mo or $14.50/mo billed yearly ($174/yr), 300 credits/mo, 2 seats · Business custom. 1 credit = 1 minute of source video.

OpusClip is still the tool the others are measured against, and for good reason. Its ClipAnything model and virality score do the one job that actually saves time: picking which 45 seconds of a long conversation will hold attention. Reframing to keep the speaker centered is reliable, caption templates are good out of the box, and Pro adds scheduling and a two-seat team workspace.

Annual Pro is the best deal in the category on price per *month* ($14.50). On price per *source minute*, it's middle of the pack.

We're an OpusClip affiliate (it's one of our featured partners), which is exactly why we're telling you where it doesn't fit.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The source-minute meter punishes long streams.** A two-hour weekly show eats 480+ credits a month; Pro gives 300.
- **Free-tier clips expire.** Free exports have a watermark, no editing, and clips can't be exported after 3 days.
- **Billing complaints are real.** OpusClip scores 4.0 on Trustpilot from ~510 reviews, but 20% are one-star, and the recurring themes are charges after cancelling, hard-to-reach support and constant upsells for more credits (Cliptude, read 2026-10-22). Read the cancellation terms before you buy.
- **It's a first-draft machine.** Comedy, nuanced arguments and multi-speaker crosstalk still need a human cut.

## 2. Vizard: the volume pick

**Best for:** creators and agencies clipping 10+ hours of source a month.
**Price:** Free (60 credits/mo, 720p, 1 social account, 3-day storage) · Creator $29/mo or $14.50/mo billed yearly ($174/yr), 600 credits/mo · Business $39/mo or $19.50/mo yearly ($234/yr) · Enterprise custom. Credit tiers scale up to 74,400/mo on paid plans (Fastlane, read from vizard.ai 2026-11-01).

Vizard's pitch is simple: same annual price as OpusClip Pro, double the source minutes. For a weekly podcast or a daily stream clipper, that's the whole decision. Creator also unlocks 4K export, no watermark, scheduling to six social accounts and 100 GB storage, and the API is included in every paid plan with no separate subscription (Vizard API docs).

The editor is a full transcript-based editor rather than a trim-and-caption box, which makes fixing a clip's start or end faster than in OpusClip.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Clip selection is a step behind.** In our experience Vizard surfaces more candidates and you discard more of them. You save credits and spend minutes.
- **Free tier is a trial in disguise.** 720p and 3-day storage mean you can't build a library on it.
- **Business is seat-priced.** Extra members bill at $5/month per seat, prorated mid-term on yearly plans (Vizard help center), so agency costs creep.
- **Still a source-minute meter.** It's a bigger bucket, not a different bucket.

## 3. Klap: pay per clip, not per minute

**Best for:** long, sparse footage (streams, webinars, lectures) where most minutes aren't clip-worthy.
**Price:** Basic $14/mo yearly (100 clips/mo) · Pro $39/mo yearly (300 clips) · Pro+ $94/mo yearly (1,000 clips). Klap's own machine-readable page lists monthly billing at $29 / $79 / $189 with yearly 50% off. New users get one free video.

Klap is the only tool here that flips the meter. "Only pay for clips you generate," says the pricing page, and it means it. A three-hour webinar costs the same as a 15-minute YouTube video if both produce ten clips. For streamers, that alone can cut costs by more than half versus OpusClip.

It handles 50+ languages for captions, includes unlimited social accounts and analytics on every plan, and Pro adds AI dubbing into 29 languages.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Pricing pages disagree.** The main page shows $14/$39/$94 yearly, the llms.txt file says $29/$79/$189 monthly, and Klap's embedded checkout shows $23/$63/$151. Confirm in checkout before you pay.
- **Source caps hide in the embed plan.** The embedded pricing lists upload limits (10 videos/mo and 45-minute max on the entry tier, 2 hours on the middle tier). A three-hour stream may need the top plan.
- **Over-generating gets expensive.** If your habit is "make 30, keep 5", the per-clip meter bills all 30.
- **No free tier** beyond a single trial video.

## 4. Submagic: the finisher

**Best for:** creators who already know which clip they want and need captions, B-roll and hook titles that perform.
**Price:** Free (3 videos/mo, watermark) · Starter $19/member/mo or $12/mo yearly ($144/yr), 45 credits ≈ 15 videos · Pro $39/member/mo or $23/mo yearly ($276/yr), 120 credits ≈ 40 videos · Business + API $69/member/mo or $41/mo yearly ($492/yr). Source: Submagic pricing page and help center.

Submagic isn't really a clipper. It's what you run *after* a clipper, or on a phone video you shot already vertical. Its animated captions are the most "TikTok native" of anything here, and it stacks on AI B-roll, silence and bad-take removal, audio cleanup, hook titles and caption translation. Pro adds Storyblocks stock footage.

Plenty of creators pair a cheap finder (Klap, CapCut) with Submagic for polish.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Source length is capped.** Starter allows 2-minute videos and Pro 5 minutes, according to third-party reads of the pricing page (MobilesTalk, checked 2026-10-21). Don't plan to upload a podcast.
- **Per-member pricing.** Every seat pays full price; teams add up fast.
- **One credit pool for everything.** Uploads and AI features draw from the same balance (Submagic help center), so heavy B-roll use eats your video count.
- **1080p/30fps ceiling** on the listed plans.

## 5. Descript: clips as a side effect of editing

**Best for:** podcasters who edit the full episode anyway and want clips from the same project.
**Price:** Free (1 media hour/mo, 100 one-time AI credits, 720p watermark) · Hobbyist $16/person/mo yearly or $24 monthly (10 media hrs, 400 AI credits) · Creator $24 yearly / $35 monthly (30 hrs, 800 credits) · Business $50 / $65 (40 hrs, 1,500 credits). Unused minutes and credits don't roll over (Descript help center).

If you already edit in Descript, "Create Clips" is good enough that a second subscription is hard to justify. You're clipping from a cleaned-up, filler-free edit rather than the raw file, which often produces better clips than a dedicated tool running on rough footage. Studio Sound and Underlord (Descript's AI co-editor) come along.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Two meters.** Media hours *and* AI credits, both use-it-or-lose-it each month.
- **Clip selection is basic.** No virality score; it finds moments, not hooks.
- **Expensive if clipping is all you want.** $16–24/mo for a clipper is steep when OpusClip annual Pro is $14.50.
- **Learning curve.** Transcript editing is great once it clicks, but it's a real editor.

We compared the two head-to-head in [OpusClip vs Descript](/blog/opus-clip-vs-descript-2026).

## 6. CapCut: the everything app

**Best for:** solo creators who want a free or cheap editor with a decent long-to-short feature built in.
**Price:** Free (basic editing) · Standard $9.99/mo (App Store, mobile only) · Pro $19.99/mo or $179.99/yr in the US, 1,200 monthly AI credits · Ultra $79/mo. Prices vary by region and show localized after sign-in.

CapCut's Long Video to Shorts tool finds excerpts and reframes them, and you're then sitting inside the most capable free short-form editor available: templates, effects, auto captions, beat sync. If your workflow is "clip, then hand-edit every clip anyway", CapCut collapses two tools into one.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Auto captions are a Pro feature**, per CapCut's own help articles. Free is not really free for clipping.
- **AI credits are shared with generative video.** Pro's 1,200 credits also pay for Seedance generations, so experimenting with AI video drains your clipping budget.
- **Region-variable pricing and a split app/web plan system** (Standard is mobile-only; paid PC features need Pro).
- **Clip selection is weaker** than OpusClip's on long conversations.

## 7. Riverside Magic Clips: free if you record there

**Best for:** remote podcast and interview shows recorded in Riverside.
**Price:** Magic Clips included on every plan, including Free. Pro $24/mo yearly or $29/mo monthly; higher tiers ~$34 (Grow) and $79 (Webinar) yearly; Business custom. Magic Clips doesn't consume Riverside AI credits; Pro and Business can generate extra clips and tune preferences (Riverside help center).

If you already record in Riverside, its separate-track local recordings give Magic Clips better source than anything you'd upload to a standalone clipper, and you pay nothing extra. That makes it the strongest "you might not need an alternative at all" option on this list.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Only works on Riverside recordings or uploads.** It can't take a YouTube link (per KeyMo's comparison, checked 2026-10-11).
- **Limited control on lower plans.** You get an automatic set; regenerating and tuning is Pro+.
- **You're buying a recording studio.** If you record elsewhere, $24/mo for clips alone makes no sense.

## What we'd actually buy

**Weekly podcast, under 5 hours of source a month:** OpusClip Pro annual, $174/yr. Best clips, lowest monthly price.

**Daily streamer or agency with 10+ hours a month:** Vizard Creator annual, $174/yr for 600 minutes a month. If footage is mostly dead air, test Klap Basic ($168/yr) first; the per-clip meter can win.

## Bottom line

OpusClip is still the best at the thing that matters most, picking the right 45 seconds, and annual Pro is the cheapest way in. Leave it when its meter stops matching your footage: for hours of source, Vizard gives you twice the minutes for the same money; for long, sparse streams, Klap's per-clip billing is the smarter meter; and if you record in Riverside or edit in Descript, the clipper you need may already be in a subscription you pay for.

Before switching, total your source hours for last month. That one number picks the tool better than any feature list.
