Calendly and Motion show up in the same "best scheduling tool" lists every year, and that framing sends a lot of people to the wrong checkout page. They are both apps that write events onto your calendar. That is where the resemblance ends.

**Calendly schedules other people into your time.** You send a link (or, since August 2026, you CC an AI assistant), an invitee picks a slot, and a meeting appears. The person the product serves is, in a real sense, the invitee.

**Motion schedules your own work into your time.** You dump tasks, deadlines and priorities in, and an AI planner fills the gaps between your meetings with blocks of work, reshuffling all day as things slip. The person it serves is you, and nobody else ever sees it.

So the sorting question for this comparison is not "which is better." It is: **who is your calendar for, the people booking you, or your own to-do list?** Answer that and 80% of the decision is done. The other 20% is pricing, and in 2026 the two companies have landed on opposite ways to charge you for AI: Calendly bolts it on as a flat per-seat uplift, Motion meters it in credits.

## Quick verdict

| Category | Winner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| External booking (clients, leads, candidates) | **Calendly** | This is the entire product; Motion's booking pages are a side feature |
| Auto-planning your own tasks | **Motion** | Calendly doesn't do it at all |
| Team lead routing / round-robin | **Calendly** | Routing forms, Salesforce handoff, centrally managed event types |
| Project management | **Motion** | Gantt, capacity planning, time tracking on Business AI |
| Free plan | **Calendly** | Free forever (one event type); Motion has only a 7-day card-required trial |
| Pricing predictability | **Calendly** | Flat seats; Motion's AI is metered and the credit cost per action isn't published |
| AI meeting notes | Tie | Both now ship a bot notetaker; Calendly's is newer and gated to Plus tiers |
| Learning curve | **Calendly** | Five minutes vs a 3-4 week ramp users consistently describe for Motion |

## What each one actually is in 2026

### Calendly: the booking link grew an assistant

Calendly is the category-definer for booking links, and for most of its life the product barely changed: event types, availability rules, reminders, round-robin for teams. That changed on **August 19, 2026**, when Calendly launched two AI products and restructured its plans around them:

- **Callie (beta)**, an AI assistant you add to an email thread at callie@calendly.com. It reads the thread, checks your availability and preferences, proposes a shortlist of times, and books once everyone agrees. It asks before making one-off changes. Inside the app, "Ask Callie" preps you for a call from past notes and contact history.
- **Calendly Notetaker**, a bot that joins Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams calls (including ones not booked through Calendly), records, transcribes, and produces a recap with action items and a draft follow-up email.

Both are English-only for now and both live on two new tiers, **Standard Plus** and **Teams Plus**, rather than being spread across the existing plans. Callie is a genuinely good answer to a real problem: the r/sales thread "Do you also feel like sending a Calendly link hurts conversion?" has a 102-upvote top comment arguing that prospects trained by anti-phishing drills now hesitate to click scheduling links. An assistant that negotiates in plain email text sidesteps that. It is still a beta, so treat it as a bonus, not the reason to buy.

### Motion: the auto-scheduler grew into a "SuperApp"

Motion started as the calendar that plans your day for you: give every task a duration, a deadline and a priority, and it places them into free time, re-optimizing whenever a meeting moves or a task runs long. That engine is still the best in the category, and it is the only reason most people should consider Motion.

Over 2025-2026 Motion bolted on a lot around it: AI projects and tasks, docs and wikis, a meeting notetaker (visible bot), an AI writer, dashboards, Gantt timelines, and "AI Employees," agents meant to run whole workflows. Every AI action now draws on a **monthly credit allowance**. Long-time users are split on the expansion; the recurring Reddit line is that the team "decided to spend their time chasing trends" while basic task-manager gaps remain.

Motion's own help center is refreshingly honest about the failure mode. When the planner can't place a task within 31 days (92 for teams), the task gets the state **"Can't Fit"** and is pinned to the top of your calendar with a red exclamation mark. That is the day Motion stops planning and hands the problem back to you, and it happens precisely when you are most overloaded.

## Pricing: flat seats vs a metered seat

### Calendly (per seat, from Calendly's pricing page and help center, checked 2026-09-28)

| Plan | Billed yearly | Billed monthly | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | $0 | One event type, one calendar connection, 1:1 scheduling |
| Standard | $10/seat/mo | $12/seat/mo | Unlimited event types, multiple calendars, automations, HubSpot/Mailchimp, Stripe/PayPal |
| Standard Plus | $18/seat/mo | Not published on page we checked | Standard + Notetaker + Callie |
| Teams | $16/seat/mo | $20/seat/mo | Round-robin, lead routing, managed event types, Salesforce, Marketo/Pardot |
| Teams Plus | $24/seat/mo | Not published on page we checked | Teams + Notetaker + Callie |
| Enterprise | From $15,000/yr | — | 50-seat minimum, SSO/SAML, audit log, Dynamics, Salesforce lookup routing |

Two details the pricing page doesn't shout about. First, Teams and Teams Plus get **volume tiers on annual plans**: seats 1-30 at $16/$24, 31-50 at $14.50/$22.50, down to $12/$20 past 300 seats, with the Plus uplift fixed at **$8 per seat**. Second, Calendly's billing docs are explicit that **you pay for every seat, filled or not**: five seats and four users is still five seats. Enterprise is also the one place where the per-seat price goes *up*, not down: $15,000 across 50 seats is $25/seat/month.

### Motion (per seat, from Motion's pricing page, checked 2026-09-28)

| Plan | Price shown on pricing page | AI credits/seat/month | Overage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro AI | $19/seat/mo | 7,500 | $0.25 per 100 credits |
| Business AI | $29/seat/mo | 15,000 | $0.19 per 100 credits |
| AI Employees tiers | ~$49 to $299/mo (third-party reports) | ~10,000 to 100,000 | — |

Here's where it gets messy, and we'd rather say so than pretend we have a clean number. Motion's page shows **$19 and $29 per seat** by default next to a "Pay annually (Save 33%)" toggle, and has separate Individuals and Teams tabs. Several independent reviews put those as annual rates with monthly at **$29 and $49**. At least one reviewer who went through checkout reports that a **solo** buyer can't actually get the $19 figure: buying alone was $49 monthly or $29 a month annual, charged as **one $348 payment**. Other trackers report different monthly first charges ($34). The AI Employees tiers show up in third-party write-ups but not on the pricing page we loaded. Our recommendation: **toggle both switches and look at the checkout total before you enter a card**, because the trial requires one.

And the seat price isn't the bill. Motion does not publish how many credits a chat message, an auto-reschedule, a generated doc or an AI Employee run costs, so nobody (us included) can tell you in advance whether 7,500 credits covers your month. That is the single most common complaint in 2026 Motion reviews, and it's the structural difference from Calendly: **Calendly's AI costs a known $8/seat. Motion's AI costs "it depends."**

## Usage limits and meters

**Calendly** has no usage meter on scheduling: the limits are structural. Free gets one event type and one calendar. Routing, round-robin and Salesforce sit on Teams; Salesforce lookup routing and SSO sit on Enterprise. Notetaker and Callie are simply on or off by tier. Calendly's AI is flat-priced to you, but its sub-processor list names OpenAI and Recall.ai behind Notetaker and Callie, so the underlying cost is metered by someone; it's Calendly's margin problem, not yours, until the next repricing.

**Motion** meters the AI layer: 7,500 or 15,000 credits per seat, overage at $0.25 or $0.19 per 100 credits, no published per-action rate. The core auto-scheduler is what most people are there for, and there's no public statement about whether ordinary rescheduling draws credits in meaningful amounts, which is exactly the problem. If you only use Motion as a planner and ignore the SuperApp features, you're unlikely to feel the meter. If you lean on AI Employees, you will.

## Where each one wins on capability

**Calendly wins:**
- **Booking UX for invitees.** No account needed, time zone detection, clean pages. Invitees know the pattern.
- **Routing.** Qualify-and-route forms that send leads to the right rep or event type, round-robin, collective events, Salesforce handoff. Motion has nothing comparable.
- **Integrations for revenue teams.** HubSpot and Mailchimp on Standard; Salesforce, Marketo, Pardot on Teams; Dynamics on Enterprise.
- **Payments.** Stripe and PayPal collection at booking on Standard, which is the reason coaches and consultants pay at all.

**Motion wins:**
- **Auto-planning.** Tasks get durations, deadlines and priorities, and Motion places them, then re-places them all day. For deadline-heavy solo work it removes a real daily decision.
- **Project and capacity views.** Business AI adds team capacity planning, Gantt timelines, time tracking and permissions. It's closer to a light PM tool than a calendar.
- **One place for tasks, docs and calendar.** If you want fewer apps, Motion is a consolidation play. Calendly is deliberately one job.

## Cons: where each one breaks

### Calendly

- **Feature gating is the #1 complaint.** On G2, gating has more negative mentions than any other issue: the free plan's single event type makes real evaluation hard, and routing and Salesforce sit behind Teams or higher.
- **Seat math gets silly at scale for a "utility."** A 100-person team on Teams is ~$17,640/yr on annual volume pricing ($19,200 at the flat $16) to send booking links, and unused seats still bill. Cal.com (open-source, free to self-host) is the standard cheaper alternative Reddit points to.
- **Sync and time zone bugs.** Review aggregators collect reports of "phantom appointments" after calendar auth refreshes and invites showing wrong times for international invitees, which is the worst class of bug for a scheduling tool.
- **Support is slow below Enterprise.** Chat and AI for most tiers; reports of 5+ day ticket responses; phone support is Enterprise-only. Trustpilot sits around 3.1/5 and is sharply polarized.
- **The AI is brand new.** Callie is labeled beta, both AI products are English-only, and the Plus tiers are six weeks old. Budget for them only if note-taking alone justifies $8/seat.

### Motion

- **Opaque pricing and a card-required trial.** No free plan, 7-day trial, card up front. Multiple Reddit reports describe missing the day-7 deadline and being charged. Reviewers also flag that cancelling on the website gets no refund for unused time under the standard terms. Set a reminder for day six.
- **The credit meter is unpriced per action.** You know the allowance and the overage rate, not what anything costs.
- **"Can't Fit" and silent slippage.** Long-term users describe empty space in the calendar while tasks go unscheduled, and past-due tasks needing manual rescheduling. The planner is best when you have slack, and least helpful when you don't.
- **Opinionated AI and a 3-4 week ramp.** The most consistent churn story: sign up, find the AI's reshuffles arbitrary (it doesn't know about energy, dependencies or context you haven't typed in), cancel in week two. People who stick past a month often call it transformative.
- **Feature bloat.** The SuperApp push added a lot of surface area, and a vocal group of users think the core got worse for it.

## Who should buy which

**Buy Calendly if** other people book your time: sales, recruiting, customer success, consultants, coaches, agencies. Start on Free, move to Standard ($10 annual) the moment you need a second event type or payments, and to Teams ($16) only when you need routing or round-robin. Add the Plus tier only if you don't already have a meeting notetaker; if you do, $8/seat duplicates it. (We compared seven of those in our meeting assistant roundup.)

**Buy Motion if** your problem is your own workload: too many tasks, hard deadlines, and a calendar full of meetings you have to work around. Solo founders, freelancers juggling clients, and small teams that want planning plus light PM in one app. Commit to the four-week ramp, use the trial seriously, and check the real checkout total before day seven.

**Pay for both if** you're a freelancer or consultant who both takes client bookings and drowns in deliverables. Motion does have booking pages, but for anything client-facing Calendly Free or Standard is cleaner, and the two coexist fine: Calendly books meetings onto your calendar, Motion plans work around them. Roughly $350-$470 a year total.

**Buy neither if** you just want a booking link for free (Cal.com, or the booking pages built into Google Workspace) or just want a solid task list (Todoist at a fraction of Motion's price). Motion's premium only makes sense if you'll actually let it plan your day.

## Bottom line

These aren't competitors so much as two halves of a calendar. Calendly is the best way to let the outside world into your schedule, and in 2026 it finally added an AI layer at a flat, knowable price. Motion is the best way to make your own schedule plan itself, and it charges for that with a meter it hasn't fully explained. Decide who your calendar is for, and the choice mostly makes itself. If the honest answer is "both," buy both and let each do its one job.
