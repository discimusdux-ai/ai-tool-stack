Most "Gamma vs Canva" articles compare slide quality. That's the least useful axis, because both tools now produce a decent-looking first draft and the gap closes every quarter.

The axis that actually decides your purchase is what you get *back*. Gamma hands you a web-native card deck — a link, primarily, with export as a secondary path. Canva hands you a Canva design — an editable object inside a brand system you can resize into an Instagram carousel next week. One is a generator. The other is a design suite that has a generator bolted into it.

So the question is: **are you shipping a link, or an asset?** If your deliverable is a URL you send to a prospect and then watch analytics on, Gamma is built for that and Canva isn't. If your deliverable is a file that other people edit, reuse, and rebrand, Canva is built for that and Gamma structurally isn't.

Everything below was verified against Gamma's live pricing page, Gamma's developer and help documentation, and Canva's live pricing page and Help Centre on 9 September 2026. That matters more than usual here, because the aggregator tables on both products are badly wrong — including on the headline prices. We flag each conflict as we hit it.

We are not in an affiliate program with Gamma, and we do not claim an active Canva affiliate link (Canva's program runs through Impact.com; we've reviewed it independently either way). Nobody paid for placement in this piece.

## Head-to-head: what each one actually is

| | Gamma | Canva |
|---|---|---|
| Core unit of output | Web "card" (scrolling, responsive) | Canva design (fixed canvas, layered objects) |
| Primary delivery | Shareable link with engagement analytics | Exported file or in-Canva collaboration |
| Prompt-to-first-draft | Purpose-built; the whole product | Magic Design, one entry point among many |
| Editability after generation | Bounded by Gamma's template system | Full object-level control, same as any design |
| Charts from real data | Generated/AI charts, limited data import | Spreadsheet import, Magic Charts, Canva Sheets |
| .pptx export | Yes, but historically lossy (see below) | Yes, standard export |
| Google Slides export | Listed on the pricing page, including Free | No native Slides file (via .pptx) |
| Brand controls | Custom branding and fonts on Pro | Brand Kits: 5 (Pro) / 100 (Business) / 1,000 (Enterprise) |
| Cheapest paid entry | $9/seat/month | US$180/year (US$15/month equivalent) |
| API | Yes, on Pro and above | No comparable deck-generation API |
| Where the money meter sits | AI credits, per generation | Shared monthly "AI allowance" across all AI tools |

The row that decides most real purchases is the first one. Gamma's cards are HTML/CSS, rendered for the web. Canva's slides are objects on a canvas. Every downstream difference — export fidelity, editability, resizing, animation — follows from that single architectural choice, not from feature-checklist decisions either company could reverse next month.

## The framing question: are you buying a format or a system?

Gamma sells a format. You describe a deck, it returns a structured, styled artifact in under a minute, and you refine it by talking to it rather than dragging boxes. Its 2026 additions are consistent with that: an agent that revises whole decks on instruction, engagement analytics on shared links, PDF and PPTX import, and a generation API so you can produce decks programmatically. If you need fifty client one-pagers generated from a template, Gamma has an actual endpoint for that. Nothing in Canva does that job.

Canva sells a system. The deck is one artifact inside a library that also holds your social posts, your ad variants, your pitch one-pagers, and — since Canva made the Affinity suite free — professional vector and photo work. AI here is a set of buttons (Magic Design, Magic Write, Magic Media, Dream Lab, Magic Charts) inside an editor most marketing teams already have open. The generated slides come out as ordinary Canva objects, which is why they stay fully editable and why they can be resized into six other formats.

The practical division most teams land on: **working decks in Gamma, brand-carrying decks in Canva.** That's not a compromise; it's an accurate read of what each tool is for.

## Pricing: Gamma

Live from gamma.app/pricing, 9 September 2026. Prices shown are the annual-billing rates the page displays by default; Gamma advertises savings of up to 28% for annual.

| Plan | Price | Monthly AI credits | Slides per prompt | Notable unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 400 **at signup**, do not refresh | Up to 10 | PDF/PPTX import; export to PDF, PPTX, PNG, Google Slides |
| Plus | $9/seat/mo ($108/seat/yr) | 1,000 | Up to 100 | Removes Gamma branding; advanced AI image models |
| Pro | $18/seat/mo ($216/seat/yr) | 4,000 | Up to 100 | Premium image models, custom branding and fonts, analytics, 10 custom domains, API |
| Ultra | $90/seat/mo ($1,080/seat/yr) | 20,000 | Up to 100 | Most advanced text/image/video models, 100 domains |

Two corrections to what you'll read elsewhere. First, aggregators still publish "20 cards per prompt" for Plus; Gamma's own page today says up to 100 slides per prompt on Plus, Pro, and Ultra. Second, several comparison sites — including ones published in the last few months — state that neither Gamma nor Canva exports to Google Slides. Gamma's pricing page lists Google Slides export in the **Free** tier's feature list. Check the vendor page, not the roundups.

The credit mechanics are where Gamma is unusually honest, and you should read them before you budget. Gamma's developer documentation publishes per-action credit costs: 1–3 credits per card for text generation, and images priced by model tier — Standard 2–15 credits, Advanced 20–33, Premium 34–75, Ultra 30–125 per image. It even gives worked examples: a 10-card deck with 5 basic images runs roughly 20–60 credits, while a 30-card social set with 30 ultra-model images runs roughly 930–3,900. That last number is worth staring at. On Plus's 1,000 monthly credits, a single ambitious image-heavy generation can eat your entire month.

Paid credits roll over up to twice the monthly allowance (2,000 on Plus, 8,000 on Pro, 40,000 on Ultra). Free's 400 credits are a one-time grant, not a monthly refresh — the single most common misunderstanding in Gamma reviews, and the source of a lot of "I burned 200 credits in my first hour" frustration.

## Pricing: Canva

Live from canva.com/pricing, 9 September 2026, US pricing, yearly toggle:

| Plan | Price | Standard AI uses/mo | Premium AI uses/mo | Ultra AI uses/mo | Brand Kits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free | US$0 | Up to 200 | Up to 20 | None | 1 |
| Pro | US$180/year for one person | Up to 2,000 | Up to 200 | Up to 20 | 5 |
| Business | US$250/year per person | Up to 4,000 | Up to 400 | Up to 40 | 100 |
| Enterprise | Quote only | Up to 4,000 | Up to 400 | Up to 40 | 1,000 |

The allowance figures come from Canva's Help Centre, which is the only authoritative source: all AI tools draw on one shared monthly allowance, and the three tiers (Standard, Premium, Ultra) drain it at different rates. Canva's own footnote says the published ranges "assume simple tasks and lower-consumption AI tools within each tier," so treat 2,000 on Pro as a ceiling you will never actually reach if you mix tools.

Now the pricing conflict, and it's a bad one. Canva's live US page says **US$180/year** for Pro. A large share of 2026 write-ups say $120 or $144/year. Some of those are stale, some are non-US regional prices reported as global dollars, and Canva's own Pricing Promise states that regional prices are set locally rather than converted from dollars — which is why a Turkish or UK page will not reconcile with a US figure at any exchange rate. If a table quotes one global Canva price, it is wrong by construction. Also note the yearly toggle advertises 16% savings, not the 30%+ some blogs claim, and that prices exclude applicable tax.

The other correction: articles quoting "500 AI credits per month on Pro" are describing a system Canva does not publish. Canva doesn't expose a credit balance in numbers you can budget against; it publishes "up to X uses" ranges per tier. Those two models are not interchangeable, and anyone converting between them is guessing.

The overflow valve is **AI Pass**, an add-on for Pro and Business subscribers. Canva's pricing page describes it as "40x more AI than Canva Pro — or 20x more than Canva Business." Note what that is: a multiplier on an allowance Canva never states in absolute units. You cannot compute what you're buying. Canva's Help Centre confirms the actual number only appears on your Billing page after you purchase, and third-party reports put it around $100/month for heavy users. That is a genuinely poor disclosure practice, and it's the single biggest budgeting risk in Canva's AI stack.

| Marketer who also does social/brand | Pro, $216/yr → **$432** | Pro, $180/yr → **$360** (covers everything, not just slides) |

| Heavy AI generation | Ultra, $1,080/yr → **$2,160** | Pro + AI Pass ≈ $180 + ~$1,200/yr → **~$2,760** |

Gamma is cheaper if slides are the only job. Canva is cheaper the moment a second content type enters the picture, because you were going to pay for Canva anyway. The heavy-AI row is the one to be suspicious of: both of those numbers are estimates against meters the vendors describe in relative terms.

## Where Gamma breaks

**.pptx export has been the product's defining weakness.** Gamma's renderer is web-native, so historically the exporter rasterized layouts to preserve visual fidelity: animations dropped, custom fonts substituted with system defaults, text arriving as an image rather than editable copy. One independent test of 25 decks put the editable-text retention rate at roughly 30%, well behind PowerPoint-native tools. Reviewers report 15–45 minutes of manual repair per exported deck, which erases the time the fast generation saved. In fairness, Gamma is actively fixing this: its 28 August 2026 changelog announced that tables now stay editable in PowerPoint (and in Google Slides after upload) with styling preserved, plus improved PDF compatibility. Treat export as "improving, still not the reason you'd buy it," and test your own deck before you commit a client deliverable to it.

**Design control is bounded by the template system.** You can change themes and some styling, but you can't freely place elements or build custom layouts. What the AI generates is approximately what ships. If a designer needs to intervene, Gamma is the wrong surface.

**Content scaling on fixed-size cards is a real complaint.** In an August 2026 post on Gamma's own community forum, a customer generating decks at volume through the API described dense slides being scaled *down* to fit a fixed 16×9 card rather than being spread across more slides — with a 25-slide, five-table deck coming out visibly squashed, and no API parameter to tune the behaviour. The same post flags that the API can create presentations but not update them: improving your house style only affects future output, because regenerating produces a new ID and URL and breaks links you've already shipped.

**The refund window is three days.** Gamma's Help Centre states that from 1 December 2025, refunds are available only within the first three days of subscribing — monthly *and* annual — only by contacting support, and only if you haven't exceeded a credit-usage threshold. Separately, its cancellation article asks you to cancel at least 30 days before renewal. Gamma's Trustpilot page reflects the predictable result: reviewers describe unexpected auto-renewals, charges after cancellation, and unresponsive support, alongside complaints about export errors and inconsistent formatting. Buy monthly first.

## Where Canva breaks

**The meter is the price, not the sticker.** Canva's answer to AI's variable cost hasn't been a headline price rise; it's been metering and re-gating. Credits burn on every generation attempt, including failed ones, and the only overflow is AI Pass at an undisclosed allowance. That's a rational business response to real GPU costs, but it means your effective cost isn't knowable from the pricing page.

**Features you already paid for can move up a tier.** The clearest 2026 example: QR codes, a long-standing Canva feature, now carry a Canva icon in the middle unless you upgrade to Business. Users report returning after a break to find designs they had already printed under Pro now prompting a Business upgrade to edit. Nobody's bill went up, which is exactly why it's effective — and why it damages trust more than a visible increase would.

**The pricing history justifies caution.** In September 2024 Canva moved Teams customers from a flat $119.99/year to per-seat pricing that could put a five-person team at $500/year — a ~300% jump that triggered a serious backlash, after which Canva grandfathered existing customers and published a public pricing promise. Teams was then closed to new sign-ups in the 30 October 2025 announcement that introduced Business. Existing Teams subscribers keep their pricing and features; everyone else is routed to the more expensive tier. Aggregators still quoting "Teams, 3-seat minimum" are describing a plan you can no longer buy.

**Generative image quality is the weak link.** Reviewers consistently report garbled text rendering, unreliable photorealism, and no way to iteratively refine a generated image without a fresh prompt — and another credit. For image generation specifically, dedicated tools beat it; Canva wins on workflow integration, not output quality. (See our [Canva vs Midjourney](/blog/canva-vs-midjourney-2026) comparison for that head-to-head.)

**Billing and cancellation complaints cluster on Trustpilot.** Sentiment toward the editor is strongly positive; sentiment toward the subscription process is not. The recurring pattern is charges after a trial cancellation attempt and difficulty obtaining refunds, with email-only support on Free and Pro. Canva's trial requires a card on file — diarise the end date.

## Who should buy which

**Buy Gamma if:** your deliverable is a link. Prompt-to-deck speed is the point, you present or share online rather than emailing files, you want engagement analytics on what you sent, or you need decks generated programmatically via API. Plus at $108/year is the best value in this comparison *if* slides are genuinely the only job you're buying for.

**Buy Canva if:** the deck is one of several things you make. You need real charts from a spreadsheet, a brand kit enforced across a team, output that other people can edit at the object level, or the ability to resize a slide into six social formats. Pro at US$180/year is more expensive than Gamma Plus and does vastly more than slides — judge it as a design suite, not a deck generator.

**Buy neither if:** the final deliverable must be a .pptx that a CFO or a VC can red-line. Both tools export .pptx; neither was architected for it. PowerPoint-native AI tools handle that job better, and we cover them in our [best AI presentation tools](/blog/best-ai-presentation-tools-2026) roundup.

**Pay for both if:** you're an agency. Gamma Plus for internal working decks and fast client drafts, Canva Pro for anything brand-carrying and everything that isn't a deck. That's $288/year for a stack that removes the awkward compromises in either tool used alone — cheaper than Gamma Ultra, and far cheaper than Canva Pro plus AI Pass.

## The bottom line

Gamma is the better deck generator and the cheaper entry point, and it's the only one of the two with an API and link analytics. Its weakness is structural, not fixable by a price change: what it produces is a web document, so anything that has to leave the web arrives degraded. Test your export path before you commit.

Canva is the better *system*, and if your team already opens it daily, Magic Design is effectively free capability on a subscription you've already justified. Its weakness is also structural: Canva is metering AI in units it won't state, selling overflow as an undisclosed multiplier, and quietly moving existing features behind a higher tier. Those are trust problems, not product problems, and the product is genuinely good.

Verify prices yourself before you buy. Canva sets prices regionally, Gamma pushes annual billing with a three-day refund window, and this category re-meters roughly every quarter.
