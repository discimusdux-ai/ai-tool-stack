Monday.com and ClickUp both say they do "work management," and from a distance they look the same: boards, timelines, automations, dashboards, and an AI layer stuck on top. Pick one for a real team and you find they bill in opposite ways. **Monday.com sells you seats in buckets.** A six-person team pays for ten. **ClickUp sells you cheap seats, then charges for AI on every one of them.** A six-person team pays for six, and then pays for AI six more times.

That's why sticker prices are useless here. Both tools list a per-seat price that's technically correct and almost never what you'll pay. Below we work out the actual bill for teams of 6 and 11, cover what each tool is better at, and list the problems users keep reporting in 2026.

**Short version:** ClickUp is cheaper and goes deeper for teams that will put effort into configuring it. Monday.com is easier to roll out to people who will never read a help article, and worth the premium when your team size lands on a bucket boundary. It loses badly when your team size sits just past one.

## Head-to-head: who wins what

| Category | Winner | Why |

| Feature depth (docs, sprints, goals, chat) | **ClickUp** | Docs, whiteboards, sprint points, chat and time tracking in one app |
| Automation headroom per dollar | **ClickUp** | 5,000 automations/month on Business ($12) vs 250 actions on monday Standard ($12) |

| Free plan | **ClickUp** | Unlimited members and tasks (60MB storage) vs monday's 2-seat cap |
| Client/guest-heavy agencies | **Depends** | monday counts 4 guests as 1 seat on Standard; ClickUp has reclassified editing guests into paid seats |

## The framing question: who is actually in your workspace?

Before features, write down three numbers: **full-time members**, **occasional members** (they look at a board once a week), and **outside guests** (clients and contractors). These decide which tool is cheaper more than any feature does.

- **monday.com bills by bucket.** Its support center puts it plainly: pricing "works per group of seats and not per seat." Paid plans start at 3 seats, then go up in multiples of 5 (3, 5, 10, 15, 20…), with self-serve stopping at 40. A team of 4 buys 5. A team of 6 buys 10. The 11th hire moves you to 15.
- **ClickUp bills per member.** You pay for the people you add. But its AI add-ons (Brain AI, Everything AI) are billed on **every paid member in the Workspace**, not just the people who use AI. Turn AI on for your two power users and you pay for all 30 members.

So monday punishes headcounts that sit *between buckets*, and ClickUp punishes *buying AI for a mostly non-AI team*. Work out which of those describes you and most of the decision is made.

## What each one is in 2026

**monday.com** started as a colorful spreadsheet-board tool and is now a family of products (Work Management, CRM, dev, service) running on one platform. The big 2026 change is commercial: **for customers who signed up on or after May 6, 2026, AI credits are part of the purchase.** Every paid Work Management plan now includes a monthly AI credit allowance (Basic 1,000, Standard 2,000, Pro 3,000), and credits are shared across monday's agents, meeting notetaker, AI blocks and "vibe" app builder. monday says agent runs cost about 10 to 50 credits for simple tasks and 250+ for complex ones. Accounts created before that date stay on their old model for now.

**ClickUp** started out as the "one app to replace them all" tool and still behaves like one: tasks, docs, whiteboards, chat, goals, time tracking, sprints, forms and dashboards all in one product. Its 2026 story is Brain², the AI rebuild with persistent memory, automatic model routing and "Super Agents." The AI got a public price list in Q1 2026: **Brain AI at $9/user/month and Everything AI at $28/user/month, billed annually** ($18 and $68 billed monthly), plus pay-as-you-go **AI Super Credits at $10 per 10,000**. ClickUp is also testing new plans that bundle AI into the base tiers with pooled credits. Its help center says they're "available to a limited number of Workspaces" and "subject to change," so price today's plans and expect the packaging to change at your renewal.

Some patterns stand out:

1. **Without AI, ClickUp Business costs about 40% less than monday Standard at both team sizes**, and it includes 5,000 automations against monday's 250.
2. **Add Brain AI and the two tools are close.** Six people on ClickUp Business + Brain AI ($1,512) cost about the same as monday Standard ($1,440), which includes its 2,000 pooled credits.
3. **Everything AI is the most expensive option in the table.** At $28 per member it costs more than the base plan it sits on. Only buy it if most of the team will use it every day.
4. **monday's sixth seat is the costly one.** On Pro, going from 5 to 6 people means buying 5 more seats, which adds $1,140 a year for one hire. If your team is 5, 10 or 15 people, monday's numbers look much better than the table above.

## Automation limits: the cap you'll hit first

This is where monday's pricing is hardest to justify. **Standard gives you 250 automation actions per month.** Reviewers, including competitors with an obvious stake (ClickUp's own "monday review" makes this point), note that active teams use that up within weeks. Some say within days. After that, the automations stop until the counter resets, so the work goes back to being manual. Pro raises the cap 100x to 25,000, but you pay 58% more per seat for it.

ClickUp Business includes 5,000 automations a month at the same $12 sticker price, and Enterprise includes 250,000. If automations are the reason you're buying a work-management tool, ClickUp gives you far more for the money.

AI agents add a second meter on top of that in both tools. monday's agents draw from the pooled credits (an "extra complex" run is 250+ credits, so 2,000 credits is fewer than ten heavy agent runs a day over a month). ClickUp's Super Agents, AI Fields and Autopilot Agents draw from per-user credit allowances, and you top up at $10 per 10,000. In both tools the meter is the real price, so check usage after month one.

## Where monday.com wins

- **Adoption.** monday's board UI is still the easiest thing to put in front of a sales VP, a client, or an operations lead who has never used a PM tool. G2 users rate it 4.7/5 across 15,000+ reviews, and ease of use is the most common strength mentioned.
- **Dashboards stakeholders actually read.** Status columns, color-coded groups and roll-up dashboards make sense to people who never open a task.
- **Responsiveness.** You don't see the volume of "it's slow" complaints that ClickUp gets.
- **Guests are cheap on Standard.** monday bills guests at a 4:1 ratio, so four client guests count as one seat. That's generous for agencies, though eight guests can still push you into the next bucket.
- **One credit pool.** Light AI users and heavy AI users share one credit pool instead of each paying a per-head AI fee.

## Where ClickUp wins

- **Price per real human.** No 3-seat minimum and no buckets. A team of 7 pays for 7.
- **Depth.** Native docs, whiteboards, chat, goals, sprint points, time tracking (from Unlimited) and mind maps. monday Work Management has no native Scrum backlog, velocity or burndown at any tier.
- **Free plan.** Unlimited members and unlimited tasks. The 60MB storage cap is the real limit, but a 3-person team can run on it for months.
- **Automations.** 5,000/month on Business, against 250 on monday Standard.
- **AI breadth.** Brain² can search across your Workspace and connected apps, write in docs, fill AI fields and run agents. If you're already a ClickUp shop, Brain AI at $9 is a reasonable add-on.

## Cons: monday.com

1. **Bucket pricing.** A 6-person team pays for 10 seats, so 40% of the bill pays for empty seats. One small-business owner wrote on LinkedIn in August 2026 that he needs 8 users but must buy 10, asks every year to pay per user, and gets "we'll add it to the roadmap."
2. **Automation cap on Standard.** 250 actions a month isn't enough for a team that actually automates.
3. **Mandatory AI credits for new customers.** Since May 6, 2026 you can't buy a Work Management plan without a credit minimum, even if your team doesn't want AI.
4. **Upfront annual billing and renewal complaints.** Complaint aggregators that collect Trustpilot, BBB and Capterra reports describe full annual renewal charges after users believed they had downgraded, and refunds being denied once billing has run. Set a calendar reminder 30+ days before renewal.
5. **Key features behind Pro.** Time tracking, private boards and the 25,000-action automation cap all need the $19 tier.

## Cons: ClickUp

1. **Performance at scale.** ClickUp's own feedback portal has a "Web app performance and speed issues" thread with more than 1,100 votes that has been open since 2020 and was still active in 2026. One January 2026 voter called the app "painfully slow." Small workspaces are mostly fine. Workspaces with thousands of tasks and dozens of views are where the complaints come from.
2. **AI billed on every member.** ZenPilot, a ClickUp partner, called this its "standing gripe": 50 seats on Everything AI is $16,800 a year "whether 50 people use it or 5."
3. **Seat and guest billing surprises.** Reviewers report guests with edit access being reclassified as paid members (one team's bill went from about $150 to nearly $1,200 in a month), seats being added automatically mid-term, and a Trustpilot reviewer who says a free workspace was auto-enrolled into a $1,584 annual plan. Another review warns you need to give 30 days' notice before renewal.
4. **Setup takes weeks.** Reviewers regularly say it takes two to three weeks before a team is productive. If nobody owns ClickUp configuration, it gets messy, and Brain² is only as good as the workspace it reads.
5. **Packaging keeps changing.** AI went from an unlisted add-on to two public tiers in Q1 2026, and new AI-bundled plans are being tested. Plan for the price to change at renewal.

## Who should buy which

**Pick monday.com if:**
- Your team is exactly 3, 5, 10, 15 or 20 people, or you're happy to fill the extra seats.
- Most users aren't technical, and getting people to adopt the tool is a bigger risk than cost.
- You want stakeholders and clients to see clean status dashboards without training.
- You'll pay for Pro anyway (time tracking, 25,000 automations).

**Pick ClickUp if:**
- Your headcount sits just past a monday bucket (6, 11, 16 people).
- Someone on the team will own configuration and enjoys doing it.
- You need sprints, docs and time tracking in one place, or you automate heavily on a budget.
- You'll buy AI only if most members will use it. Otherwise leave the add-on off and use a separate AI tool for the few who want one.

**Pay for both?** We've seen agencies run client-facing boards on monday (guests are cheap, and clients understand it) and internal delivery on ClickUp. It's only worth it if the monday side fits a bucket exactly.

## Bottom line

Neither tool wins outright. The decision comes down to your headcount and how much of the team will use AI. For a team that can configure it, **ClickUp is the cheaper and deeper tool**, as long as you don't put a per-member AI add-on on people who won't use it. **monday.com is the easier tool to roll out.** It's priced fairly when your team fits a bucket and expensive when you're one hire past one. Write down your headcount before you look at the feature pages.
