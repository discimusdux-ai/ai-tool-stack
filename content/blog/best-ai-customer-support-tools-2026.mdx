# Best AI Customer Support Tools in 2026: What a "Resolution" Actually Costs

There is no best AI support tool. There is only the question of which vendor's definition of "resolved" you are willing to be billed against.

That sounds like a semantic complaint. It is a budgeting one. In 2026 the entire category has moved off seat pricing and onto outcome pricing: Intercom charges $0.99 per outcome, Help Scout $0.75 per resolution, Zendesk roughly $1.50 committed and about $2.00 pay-as-you-go, HubSpot cut its Breeze Customer Agent from $1.00 per conversation to $0.50 per *resolved* conversation in April 2026. The unit looks standardised. It isn't. As industry analyst Craig Durr put it to TechTarget in August 2026, "outcome-based pricing is real, but it is being sold ahead of any agreed way to score it" — and he notes Zendesk counts 72 hours of silence as a resolution. Intercom counts a customer walking away without asking a follow-up as an "assumed resolution." Nobody bills you for the moment the customer's problem actually went away, because the only person who knows that has already closed the tab.

This guide is about that gap. Below: what each platform charges, what triggers the charge, where the meter runs faster than the marketing, and which tool we'd buy at three different volumes.

If you want the wider chatbot landscape rather than the money math, our [Best AI Chatbot & Customer Support Tools](/blog/best-ai-chatbots-customer-support-2026) piece covers capability, and [Intercom vs Zendesk](/blog/intercom-vs-zendesk-2026) goes head to head on the two incumbents. This one stays on pricing models and how they fail.

## The one question that decides everything: are you paying for attempts or for wins?

Every tool below sits on one side of a line.

**Wins-only billing** (Intercom, Help Scout, Gorgias AI Agent, Zendesk past its allowance, HubSpot Breeze): you're charged only when the AI finishes the job alone. Escalations are free. This sounds like the buyer-friendly model, and mostly it is — but it has a perverse property that Help Scout's own numbers make obvious. It is success-priced. A better-trained AI resolves more, so your bill goes *up* as the tool gets better. Teams that budget "AI spend" as a fixed line item and then invest in their knowledge base are routinely surprised by their fourth invoice.

**Attempt billing** (Freshdesk's Freddy sessions, Chatwoot's Captain credits, Tidio's Lyro conversations): you're charged when the AI engages, whether or not it helps. Freshdesk sells sessions at $49 per 100 — roughly $0.49 per 24-hour session window — and it bills the same whether Freddy nailed it or produced a wall of text your agent then had to read. That last cost is not hypothetical. In a May 2026 r/Freshservice thread, a 600-person org reported tier-1 MTTR going *up* 20% five months after enabling Freddy at a ~25% autoresolve rate, because agents were spending two to three extra minutes per ticket reading the failed AI context before replying. Two other teams in the thread confirmed the same regression; one capped autoresolve at 18% to fix it.

Pick your side before you shortlist. Then check the second-order terms: does the vendor let you cap spend, do allowances roll over, and does a conversation that the AI resolves *also* count as a billable ticket? (At Gorgias, it can be billed twice — once as a ticket, once as a resolution.)

## 1. Intercom (Fin)

**Best for:** SaaS and product-led teams that want the strongest agent and can absorb variable cost
**Price:** Seats $29 / $85 / $132 per seat/mo (Essential, Advanced, Expert). Fin $0.99 per outcome, 50-outcome monthly minimum (~$49). Copilot $29/agent/mo annual, $35 monthly.

Fin remains the best autonomous agent most teams can buy off the shelf, and Intercom deserves credit for publishing its billing rules in plain language rather than hiding them behind an account executive. Four outcome types exist: a resolution, a procedure handoff, and a disqualification each cost $0.99; a lead *qualification* costs $9.99 — ten times the headline rate, and the number most buyers miss because it isn't on the pricing page hero. You're charged at most once per conversation regardless of how many questions Fin fields, and never for a conversation the customer escalates to a human. Since May 2025, a conversation Fin "resolved" that an agent later replies to is no longer billed.

**Where it breaks.** The definition of resolution is still partly assumed rather than confirmed: if the customer stops replying, that's a billable outcome. A widely-read Intercom community thread documented 16 conversations marked "resolved by Fin" of which the customer only judged 7 genuinely solved — a 40% headline resolution rate against a real 12.5%. Second, the cost is genuinely unbounded relative to margin: a marketplace posted on Intercom's own community in January 2026 that it stopped Fin after $12,000 in spend, because at roughly €1 of margin per user per month the model simply didn't fit. Third, complaint aggregators flag Fin resolution charges routinely exceeding seat costs with limited per-account guardrails. Set hard usage limits in the admin panel on day one — Intercom supports them, and almost nobody turns them on until after the bad invoice.

## 2. Zendesk

**Best for:** larger orgs that need governance, omnichannel and audit trails
**Price:** Support Team $19; Suite Team $55, Suite Professional $115, Suite Enterprise ~$169 per agent/mo, billed annually. Automated resolutions: 5 / 10 / 15 included per agent per month by plan (capped at 10,000/yr), then ~$1.20–$1.50 committed, ~$2.00 pay-as-you-go.

The good news of 2026 is that Zendesk killed the roughly $50/agent Advanced AI add-on: since the May 2026 packaging change, AI agents are included in every Suite and Support plan. The bad news is what replaced it. The included automated-resolution allowance is a rounding error against real volume — a 25-agent Suite Professional team gets 250 ARs a month, and a team genuinely deflecting at scale might do 1,500, which is 1,250 overage resolutions at ~$1.50, or about $1,875 a month on top of seats. Independent teardowns put AR overage at 30–50% on top of seat fees at most team sizes.

**Where it breaks.** Zendesk does not publish the AR rate; every number in circulation comes from customer contract teardowns and Reddit invoice posts. Overage is billed monthly regardless of your annual contract, so a Black Friday or refund-event spike lands in full the following month and the only lever to stop the meter is switching the AI agent off. Advanced custom reporting still requires Suite Professional at $115, which forces small teams up two tiers just to get SLA and CSAT dashboards. And the 72-hour-silence resolution rule means a channel where customers habitually don't reply generates revenue for Zendesk that looks like nothing on your side. Setup is a 4–12 week project for most orgs, and the contracts have a persistent reputation for being hard to exit.

## 3. Freshdesk (Freddy)

**Best for:** cost-sensitive teams that want a competent help desk cheaply
**Price:** Free (1–2 agents, 6 months), Growth $19, Pro $55, Enterprise $89 per agent/mo annual. Freddy AI Agent: 500 one-time complimentary sessions on paid plans, then $49 per 100 sessions. Freddy Copilot $29/agent/mo.

Freshdesk is the value play on seats, and the 500 free sessions are a genuinely useful pilot budget. Then the model changes character. A "session" is all interactions between one end user and the agent inside a 24-hour window, at roughly $0.49 — billed on engagement, not on success. Packs are tied to your billing cycle, don't roll over, and unused sessions are lost. Auto-recharge is on by default at a 50-session threshold, which is convenient right up until it isn't.

**Where it breaks.** Freddy trains on Freshdesk's own knowledge base. If your real documentation lives in Notion, Confluence or Google Drive, you get to maintain a parallel KB — which eats the productivity gain you bought it for. When you run out of sessions, the AI simply stops. And Freshdesk is a legacy ticketing platform with AI layered on top, which shows in both the add-on-heavy pricing and the integration limits; reviewers also report reopened tickets, misfired auto-resolves, and slow, occasionally abrupt account handling.

## 4. Gorgias

**Best for:** Shopify and ecommerce brands
**Price:** Helpdesk by ticket volume, never by seat: Starter $10/mo (50 tickets), Basic $60 (300), Pro $360 (2,000), Advanced $900 (5,000); overage $0.32–$0.40/ticket. AI Agent sold alongside: about $0.90 per resolved interaction on most plans, $1 on Starter, $1.50 overage past your included interactions.

Gorgias's moat was never AI — it was plumbing. Order data, refunds, subscription edits and discount codes live inside the ticket, saving trained agents an estimated 40–60 seconds per ticket, which compounds hard at 500+ tickets a day. The pricing model is also the most structurally honest for support teams that flex headcount: you can add seasonal agents without adding cost, because you pay for tickets.

**Where it breaks.** Volume pricing punishes seasonality. Operators reported Q4 2025 bills running 60–80% above their monthly baseline, and holiday spikes can trigger tier upgrades that arrive without a warning email. Worse, a conversation the AI fully resolves can be billed twice — once as a billable ticket, once as an automated interaction. Gorgias markets 45–60% automation; G2 reviewers routinely land at 20–30% without heavy macro pre-training. And there's the usual irony: chat support is gated to higher tiers, so Starter and Basic merchants wait 24–48 hours on email when a Shopify connection breaks. Trustpilot sits near 2.5 with roughly a third one-star, almost entirely about bills and refunds, against ~4.6 on G2.

## 5. Help Scout

**Best for:** small teams that want the cheapest defensible AI meter
**Price:** Free (5 users, 1 inbox, 100 contacts/mo), Standard $25, Plus $45, Pro $75 per user/mo monthly; annual is 16% off. AI Answers $0.75 per resolution, plus three months free unlimited on signup.

Help Scout has the friendliest AI economics in this roundup and the only spend cap we'd call genuinely protective: set a monthly ceiling and AI Answers disables itself for the rest of the cycle, with email warnings on the way up. A resolution is counted only when the customer doesn't escalate, search the KB, ask more, or signal they need help — one charge per conversation.

**Where it breaks.** Billing is in arrears, and the three free months mean your first real AI invoice lands in month four, describing month three's activity — a lagging surprise by design. AI Answers reads only your Help Scout Docs and configured website URLs; it can't touch Notion, Confluence, your CRM or order data. And Help Scout's pricing history should make you read your contract: in late 2024 it scrapped per-seat billing for "contacts helped," a change that made one solo developer's bill jump from $66/mo to a $266 minimum on unchanged volume. The backlash was loud enough that the public page shows per-user pricing again in 2026 — but contact-based accounts still exist, some plans now carry a "legacy" badge, and you can't switch back once you leave one.

## 6. Tidio (Lyro)

**Best for:** small stores that want a working widget this afternoon
**Price:** Free; Starter ~$24.17/mo (100 billable conversations); Growth from ~$49.17; Plus from $300/mo plus usage; Premium custom. Lyro sold separately from ~$32.50/mo for 50 conversations, scaling to 1,000; 50 free Lyro conversations lifetime.

Twenty minutes from signup to live bot, one-click WordPress and Shopify install, and Lyro runs on Anthropic's Claude rather than the category-default GPT. Tidio publishes a 67% resolution claim and offers a contractual 50% resolution guarantee at the Premium tier — a rare thing to put in writing, and worth asking about.

**Where it breaks.** The 50 free Lyro conversations are lifetime, not monthly, so the free tier is live-chat-only within a week of real traffic. Billing is per conversation with a 15-minute inactivity reset, so one persistent customer can generate several billable conversations. The plan ladder has a brutal gap between the small tiers and the $300 Plus tier with nothing in between. And the Trustpilot record is bimodal — around 3.8 with roughly a quarter one-star, dominated by auto-renewal charges, refused refunds after a December 2024 restructure that doubled some existing bills, and abrupt account suspensions with a 24-hour SLA as the only recourse.

## 7. Chatwoot

**Best for:** technical teams that would rather run infrastructure than a meter
**Price:** Self-hosted Community Edition $0/agent (Premium Support $19, Enterprise $99 per agent/mo). Cloud: Hacker $0 (2 agents, 500 conversations, 30-day retention, no AI), Startups $19, Business $39, Enterprise $99 per agent/mo annual, with 300/500/800 Captain AI credits respectively and extra credits at $20 per 1,000.

Including a free, self-hostable option reframes this entire price ladder. Chatwoot is open source (32k+ GitHub stars), covers email, WhatsApp, Instagram, SMS and Telegram in one inbox, and the self-hosted Community Edition costs nothing per agent forever. If your objection to the rest of this list is philosophical — you don't want a third party deciding what "resolved" means and invoicing you for it — this is the exit.

**Where it breaks.** Captain AI is credit-metered rather than outcome-priced, so you're back to paying for attempts, just cheaply. Self-hosting means you own upgrades, backups, deliverability and uptime; the true cost is engineering hours, not licence fees. Captain requires connecting OpenAI models, so "self-hosted" doesn't mean the AI stays on your metal. Cloud Hacker at $0 is a side-project tier, not a starter plan, and Startups at $19 still lacks teams, automation rules and voice.

## Sidebar: the two vendors rewriting the meter

**Salesforce Agentforce** is the clearest illustration of how unsettled this market is. It launched at $2 per conversation, took criticism for charging on interactions that delivered nothing, added Flex Credits at $500 per 100,000 credits plus a $5/user Agentforce licence and a $125/user flat-fee option, and then in June 2026 announced pay-per-resolution for the Help Agent — $2, charged only when the agent resolves autonomously — available from July 2026. Flex Credits don't roll over past the order end date, and Salesforce says Flex Credits and Conversation pricing can't coexist in the same org. If you're mid-negotiation, you are negotiating against a price list that moved three times in two years.

**HubSpot** went the other way and cut. Breeze Customer Agent dropped from $1.00 per conversation to $0.50 per resolved conversation on 14 April 2026, and the Prospecting Agent moved from monthly contact-based fees to $1 per qualified lead. HubSpot claims 65% resolution and a 39% cut in resolution time; treat both as vendor-stated. At $0.50, it is currently the cheapest per-resolution rate from a major platform — worth a quote if you're already on the Smart CRM.

## What we'd actually buy

**A five-person SaaS support team, ~800 tickets/month.** Help Scout Standard at 5 users is $125/mo monthly billing, and at a realistic 45% AI resolution rate that's ~360 resolutions × $0.75 = $270, so about $395/mo, roughly $4,740 a year — with a cap you control. The same team on Intercom Advanced is $425 in seats plus ~$356 in Fin outcomes: about $781/mo, $9,372 a year. You are paying roughly double for a materially better agent. That is a defensible trade; just make it deliberately.

**A Shopify brand doing 2,000 tickets/month.** Gorgias Pro at $360/mo (annual ~$471/mo bundled with AI) beats anything seat-priced once you have more than four agents, and the order actions inside the ticket are worth real money. Budget for December: assume your peak month runs 60–80% over baseline and price the annual plan against your *average*, not your best month.

**A 25-agent enterprise on Zendesk Suite Professional.** $2,875/mo in seats, 250 included ARs, and at 1,500 real resolutions another ~$1,875 in overage: about $4,750/mo, $57,000 a year, before Copilot at $50/agent adds $15,000 more. Before you renew, get the AR rate in writing, get a committed-volume discount, and ask specifically how the 72-hour silence rule is applied to your channels.

**Anyone whose margin per customer is under about €1/month.** Don't buy outcome pricing at all. Self-host Chatwoot, or use a cheap attempt-priced agent with a hard cap. The marketplace that burned $12,000 on Fin didn't have a bad tool; it had a business model the pricing model was never designed for.

## The bottom line

The published rate is the least important number in this category. What matters is the definition behind it, and the definitions are not comparable: Intercom bills an assumed resolution when a customer goes quiet, Zendesk waits 72 hours and calls silence success, Freshdesk bills the attempt, Gorgias can bill the same conversation twice, and Help Scout charges you more precisely as your knowledge base gets better.

Run a pilot before you sign anything, on your own tickets, for one full month — the field median AI resolution rate across 195 real deployments and 38 vendors is about 70%, but the range runs from 15% to 98%, and every number a vendor quotes you is drawn from the top of that range. Then read your invoice line by line against what the AI actually solved. If those two numbers don't match, you have found your negotiating position.
