Every AI company wants students this fall, and almost all of them are paying for the privilege. Google is giving US college students a year of Google AI Pro. OpenAI is giving four months of ChatGPT Plus. Perplexity halves its price. Grammarly takes 50% off. What these offers share is that each one asks for a card, and each one renews at full price once it ends.

So the question we used to judge this list was **"free for a semester, or free for good?"** A semester-long trial is a fine way to try a tool. If you build your whole study routine on it, though, the bill shows up in month five or month thirteen, usually during finals. Below are seven tools that cover the jobs students actually have: understanding readings, researching, memorizing, writing, and polishing. We sorted each one by what it costs after the promotion ends.

## How we chose and checked

We kept tools that a student can sign up for personally, without the school buying a license. Every price comes from the vendor's own pricing page or help center, checked in October 2026, and we say which billing view (monthly or annual) each number uses. We left out "AI homework solver" apps whose main pitch is producing answers to submit. That's partly an integrity issue, but the bigger reason is that they don't help on the exam, which is where your grade comes from.

A ground rule for the whole list: **check your course's AI policy before you use any of these on graded work.** Policies change from one syllabus to the next. AI detectors are also less trustworthy than many instructors think, which we cover in the sidebar below.

## The framing: semester offers vs. permanent tiers

There are two kinds of "free" in this market.

- **Promotional free** means a paid plan with a timer on it. Google's offer needs a valid payment method and "will automatically charge $19.99/month after the trial ends." OpenAI's needs one too and "renews at $20 per month unless you cancel." Both use SheerID to verify that you're a student.
- **Permanent free** means a tier the vendor sells as a product: Gemini Notebook Standard, Rytr Free, Quizlet's (reduced) free plan, Grammarly Free. These have limits, but they don't send you a bill.

The smart setup is to build your routine on permanent tiers and use the promotional ones on purpose. Set a calendar reminder the week before each one renews.

## 1. Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM): best for studying your own material

**Best for:** turning readings, slides and lecture transcripts into something you can question, quiz yourself on, and listen to.
**Price:** Standard is free with any Google account. Higher limits come with Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra.

Most student AI use should look like this. You upload the sources your course actually assigns, and the model answers *from those sources*, with inline citations back to the page. That's very different from a chatbot drawing on the open internet. When you're preparing for an exam on a specific textbook, you want the textbook's framing, not a general-web paraphrase.

Google renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook in July 2026. The free tier didn't change much, and it's generous. According to Google's help center, Standard gets 100 notebooks, 50 sources per notebook, 50 chats per day, 3 Audio Overviews and 3 Video Overviews per day, and 10 flashcard sets, quizzes, reports and mind maps per day. Deep Research is capped at 10 per **month** on Standard. On Pro it's 20 per day, which is the biggest difference between the tiers.

**Where it breaks:**
- Google changed the usage limits on **September 2, 2026**, and the help page still says "subject to change." Limits on a free tier can move without warning.
- 50 sources per notebook is tight for a thesis literature review. On Standard you'll end up splitting by chapter.
- Audio Overviews sound convincing, which makes it easy to listen to one and skip the reading. They also simplify. Use them to review, not as a replacement for the material.
- Its strength is also a limit: it stays inside your sources, so it won't tell you when a reading is outdated or disputed.

## 2. Google AI Pro (student offer): best value all-round assistant, if you're in the US

**Best for:** US college students who want a frontier assistant plus Gemini inside Docs and Gmail, Notebook Pro limits, and 5 TB of storage.
**Price:** $0 for 12 months for eligible US college students (redeem by **December 31, 2026**), then $19.99/month. Outside the US, eligible students in 140+ markets get a year of Google AI **Plus**, which renews at $4.99/month or the local equivalent.

On paper this is the best deal on the list: twelve months of a $19.99 plan, which works out to roughly $240 of value. Google's August 19, 2026 announcement lists 4x higher Gemini usage limits than non-subscribers, Gemini in Gmail and Docs, 5 TB of storage, and a new student hub for notes, flashcards and study plans. It also lifts your Gemini Notebook limits to Pro (500 chats per day, 300 sources per notebook, 20 Deep Research runs per day).

**Where it breaks:**
- **Card required, auto-renews at $19.99.** If you forget, month thirteen costs you.
- **Personal accounts only.** Google's terms say it is "not available on school-issued accounts," so you'll be keeping coursework in a personal account.
- **Geography gaps.** The Plus version for non-US students excludes Canada, Hong Kong, Macau, Albania, Bolivia and Tunisia entirely.
- Storing 5 TB with Google is convenient, and it also makes leaving harder. Once your photos and files live in that storage, cancelling becomes a real decision.

## 3. ChatGPT Plus (student offer): best if you already live in ChatGPT

**Best for:** students who already rely on ChatGPT and want Plus limits through midterms.
**Price:** four free monthly billing periods for eligible US college and graduate students. You must **claim by October 31, 2026**, and it renews at $20/month. After that, ChatGPT's own ladder is Free ($0, with ads), Go (price varies by country and also shows ads), and Plus $20/month with no annual option.

OpenAI's 2026 Back to School offer covers full-time and part-time students at US degree-granting institutions, with SheerID verification. Existing monthly Plus subscribers who pay ChatGPT directly can apply it too. App Store and Google Play subscriptions can't.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Cancelling early forfeits the remaining free months.** OpenAI's help center says remaining promotional months "are forfeited when the cancellation takes effect." You can't cancel on day one to protect yourself from the renewal. You need a reminder instead.
- **Four months doesn't cover a full academic year.** If you claim it in October, it runs out in February, in the middle of the spring semester.
- **Limits have been shifting.** In early October 2026, OpenAI forum users reported shorter maximum conversation lengths on Plus and more rate-limit complaints. That matters if you paste in long readings.
- **Ads on Free and Go.** If you go back to the free tier after the promo, you'll be using an ad-supported product.

## 4. Perplexity Education Pro: best for research you have to cite

**Best for:** literature searches, quick fact-checks with sources, and getting oriented in a new topic.
**Price:** $10/month for verified students and educators, half the standard Pro price of $20/month (about $17/month billed annually, per Perplexity's pricing page in September 2026).

Perplexity's strength is that every answer comes with clickable sources, and Education Pro includes Learn Mode, unlimited Pro Searches, file uploads and premium models. For the "what are the main arguments about X, and where do they come from" stage of an essay, it's faster than a search engine and more honest about where its claims come from than a plain chatbot. We cover it in more depth in our [Perplexity review](/blog/perplexity-ai-review-2026).

**Where it breaks:**
- **Unlike the Google and OpenAI offers, this one is a discount, not free.** You pay $10 from day one.
- **Checkout can be fiddly.** Perplexity's own help center tells verified students to use the desktop web flow, not the mobile app, and warns that an unpaid earlier subscription can send you to a **full-price** payment page. Don't pay that full price by accident.
- **Citations aren't verification.** A cited source can still be a blog post, a press release or something the model misread. Open the source before you put it in a bibliography.
- Many universities give you library databases (JSTOR, Scopus, PubMed) for free. Perplexity is useful for finding your way into a topic, but those databases are where you should get the sources you actually cite.

## 5. Quizlet: best for memorization

**Best for:** vocab, languages, anatomy, definitions, anything that comes down to spaced repetition.
**Price:** Free with ads and limited Learn/Test modes · Plus $7.99/month or **$35.99/year** · Plus Unlimited $9.99/month or $44.99/year · Family $83.99/year (annual only). 7-day trial with card.

Quizlet is still the default because everyone's classmates already made the flashcard set. Its AI features (study guides and practice tests generated from your notes) are reasonable, and the shared library is the real advantage. PCMag still rates it an Editors' Choice in 2026.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The free plan has been cut back.** You can still create unlimited sets, but you'll "quickly run into limitations" in Learn and Test modes, according to PCMag. And there are ads.
- **Monthly billing is a bad deal.** At $7.99/month you'd pay $95.88 a year, against $35.99 billed annually. The difference is more than the annual price itself.
- **The trial needs a card** and converts to a full paid month if you don't cancel within seven days.
- It's built for recall, not understanding. Pair it with Gemini Notebook for the "why" questions.

Free alternative worth knowing: Gemini Notebook's own flashcards and quizzes (10/day free) and Anki (free on desktop) cover much of the same ground.

Rytr is the only writing tool on this list with a **permanent free plan you can use without a card**. Rytr is also our affiliate partner, so here's why it made the list on its own merits. It's template-driven: you pick "email," "cover letter" or "outline," set a tone, and get a usable draft in seconds. For students, that structure is often more useful than a blank chat box, because you don't have to work out how to prompt it. The free 10,000 characters is about 1,500 words a month, which is plenty for the occasional email to a professor or scholarship cover letter. If you write constantly (a student newspaper, a club with a social account, job applications every week), $9/month Unlimited still costs less than any of the big assistants after their promos end.

## 7. Grammarly Pro: best for polishing, but check the renewal price

**Best for:** catching errors and fixing tone in writing you've already done yourself.
**Price:** Free tier available · Pro $30/month, $60/quarter or **$144/year** ($12/month average) · students get **50% off for one subscription period** through SheerID (save up to $72).

Grammarly now belongs to Superhuman. The company renamed itself in October 2025, and Grammarly Pro replaced the old Premium and Business plans. Pro adds full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustments and 2,000 AI prompts. Grammarly Free still catches spelling and grammar, and that covers most of what students need.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The discount lasts one period, then renews at full price.** Superhuman's help center says so: "your subscription will be set to renew at a standard rate." If you take the 50% off on an annual plan, year two costs the full $144.
- **Pro rewrites can make your writing sound like everyone else's.** If you hand in rewritten sentences, a detector can flag them. That's the opposite of what you want (see the sidebar).
- **Monthly is expensive:** $30/month is 2.5x the annual average.
- Grammarly's affiliate program (through Impact) is frozen for us, so this pick has nothing to do with commission.

## Sidebar: the AI detector problem nobody mentions in the ads

If you use AI tools, your biggest risk usually isn't getting caught cheating. It's being **wrongly accused**. In February 2026, Washington State University cancelled its Turnitin AI-detection contract. Its provost memo points out that even Turnitin's own claimed 1-2% false-positive rate meant roughly 1,485 of WSU's 148,547 Fall 2024 assessments were probably flagged wrongly. It also says that between 2023 and 2025, **33%** of WSU's AI-related integrity hearings ended in "not responsible" when detector output was the only evidence. The memo names UC Berkeley, Indiana, Michigan State, Oregon State, Colorado State and the University of Washington as schools that also banned its use in integrity cases.

What protects you, whatever tool you use:
1. **Write in Google Docs or Word with version history on.** In a widely shared 2026 case, a student flagged as "100% AI" got cleared using drafts, timestamps and writing-center records.
2. **Keep your notes and outlines.** Gemini Notebook and Quizlet sessions show you were studying.
3. **Ask before using AI to rewrite.** Grammar fixes are usually fine. Wholesale rewrites usually aren't, and they're also what detectors pick up.

## What we'd actually set up

**The $0 student (permanent):** Gemini Notebook Standard + Rytr Free + Grammarly Free + Quizlet Free (or Anki). **$0/year, and nothing renews.** This covers about 80% of what students actually do.

**The US college student this year:** Claim Google AI Pro (12 months free, redeem by Dec 31) and add a calendar reminder for month eleven. Skip ChatGPT's offer unless you already depend on ChatGPT, because a second assistant running at the same time is mostly redundant. Add Rytr Free for emails and applications. **$0 for 12 months**, then decide whether $19.99/month is worth it.

**The heavy researcher (grad student, thesis year):** Perplexity Education Pro ($120/year) + Gemini Notebook (free, or Pro limits via the Google offer) + your library databases. **About $120/year.**

**The language or pre-med memorizer:** Quizlet Plus annual ($35.99) + Gemini Notebook Free. **$35.99/year.** Don't pay monthly.

**The student with a side hustle or busy club role:** add Rytr Unlimited at $7.50/month billed annually, or **$90/year**. That's still less than five months of any big assistant at full price.

## Bottom line

The best AI tool for most students in 2026 is **Gemini Notebook**: it's free, it works from your own sources, and studying is the main job you have. The best *deal* is Google's 12-month AI Pro offer for US students, as long as you treat it as a 12-month trial and not a permanent free plan. For everything you write outside graded assignments, **Rytr** is the cheapest permanent option, with a no-card free tier and a $9 ceiling. For the rest, mark the renewal dates. Every "free" student offer on this list except the permanent tiers ends in a bill.
