There is no single best AI SEO tool in 2026, and every list that names one has quietly decided which half of the job you care about. The category split in two over the last eighteen months. One half still does what SEO software has always done — keywords, backlinks, crawls, rankings. The other half watches what ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews say about you when nobody clicks anything at all. A few vendors do both. Most charge you twice.

Before you compare features, answer one question: **are you optimizing to rank, or to be cited?**

Ranking is a link position you can measure with a rank tracker you already own. Citation is a sentence inside somebody else's answer, generated fresh each time, in a system with no public index and no ranking report. They overlap — pages that rank tend to get cited — but they are bought differently, priced differently, and metered differently. Almost every buying mistake in this category comes from paying enterprise money for the half you didn't need.

## The meter changed, and so did the sticker

For a decade this category metered *keywords tracked*. In 2026 it meters **prompts tracked** — one question, on one AI platform, in one location, checked at some frequency. That unit is more expensive than a keyword and far less stable, because the same prompt returns different answers on different days. Vendors know this, which is why almost every AI-visibility tier is sold as a coverage ladder: pay more, watch more engines.

Two consequences follow. First, entry prices that look cheap are usually single-engine. Profound's $99 Starter tracks ChatGPT and nothing else; multi-engine work starts at $399. Ahrefs' Brand Radar is $199/month for one platform index and $699/month for all of them. Second, the incumbents raised their base prices to fund it. Semrush reorganized around Semrush One after Adobe's November 2025 acquisition, and the entry point into the new AI-inclusive line landed at $199/month against the legacy SEO-only Pro plan at $139.95 — an increase of roughly $60/month for users who wanted the new visibility tracking. That went down exactly as well as you'd expect on r/SEO, where the loudest fear is that the classic SEO plans get sunset entirely.

The honest framing for 2026: this is not a new tool you're buying. It's a new meter attached to a tool you already owned, and in most cases the vendor raised the base price to pay for it.

## 1. Semrush One

**Best for:** teams that want organic rankings and AI visibility in the same dashboard and the same invoice.
**Price:** Semrush's SEO + AI Search pricing page lists four tiers at $117.33, $165.17, $248.17 and $455.67 per month on annual billing (save up to 17%), plus a free tier. Monthly billing on the new Starter line has been widely reported at $199. Legacy SEO-only Pro remains purchasable at $139.95/month. The AI Visibility toolkit can also be bought standalone at $99/month per domain, with add-ons for extra users (from $45/mo) and reports ($10–$20/mo).

Semrush is still the broadest data set in the category — a keyword index in the tens of billions, competitive traffic estimates, PPC data, and now prompt-level AI visibility with competitor comparison and share-of-voice on the upper tiers. Starter monitors 5 sites, 50 prompts and 500 keywords with daily tracking; Pro+ takes that to 15 sites, 100 prompts and 1,500 keywords and adds historical data and content optimization; Advanced reaches 40 sites, 200 prompts, 5,000 keywords and API access.

If you manage more than one brand and you need one system of record, this is the least painful place to put it. The AI visibility data sits next to the ranking data, which matters more than it sounds: the interesting question in 2026 is not "am I cited?" but "am I cited on the queries where I also rank, or on queries where a competitor outranks me?" Very few tools let you ask that in one place.

**Where it breaks**

- **The price jump is real and unpopular.** Moving from legacy SEO Pro to the AI-inclusive Semrush One line costs meaningfully more for features many teams consider table stakes now.
- **Feature gating is aggressive.** Historical data, content optimization, share of voice and API access are each parked a tier apart, and the tier you need is rarely the one you priced.
- **Site Audit is shallow** compared with dedicated crawlers. Technical SEO teams routinely pair Semrush with Screaming Frog rather than trusting the built-in crawl depth.
- **Adobe now owns it.** The roadmap serves an enterprise marketing suite, and there is no version of that story where entry-level prices fall.

## 2. Ahrefs (plus Brand Radar)

**Best for:** backlink and crawl data, with AI visibility priced separately so you can decline it.
**Price:** Lite $129/mo, Standard $249/mo, Advanced $449/mo, Enterprise $1,499/mo, with up to 17% off annual and additional users at $40–$100/mo depending on tier. Brand Radar is a standalone or add-on product: $199/month per platform index, $699/month for all platforms including 2,500 custom prompt checks. Custom prompt packages are sold separately at $100/mo and $250/mo. Ahrefs Webmaster Tools remains free for your own verified site.

Ahrefs' pitch for Brand Radar is the strongest technical argument in the category: rather than running synthetic prompts you invented, it works from a database of hundreds of millions of *observed* prompts, so you can research any brand's AI visibility with zero setup — including competitors who never opted in. If you want to know what the market is actually asking rather than what your team guessed it might ask, that distinction is the whole ballgame.

The core platform still has the crawler, the link index and the cleanest UI in the category, and Standard and above now include unlimited report credits, which removes the single worst thing about using Ahrefs.

**Where it breaks**

- **Lite is credit-crippled.** 500 credits a month, one credit per report opened or filter applied — experienced users report exhausting a month's allowance in a single research session. Unlimited credits start at $249/mo.
- **No free trial.** Webmaster Tools is not a trial; it shows your site only.
- **AI visibility is an expensive bolt-on.** Full-platform Brand Radar at $699/mo can cost more than the SEO subscription it attaches to.
- **Pay-as-you-go auto-charges.** Enable overage and you're billed automatically past plan limits; PAYG credits expire after three billing months.
- **Legacy-plan grievances persist.** Forced migrations and 2024's 12–50% price rises still dominate the complaint threads, and the Trustpilot profile reflects it.

## 3. Surfer

**Best for:** writing and optimizing a specific page against what currently ranks.
**Price:** Surfer's own pricing FAQ names five plans — Discovery, Standard, Pro, Peace of Mind and Enterprise — at roughly $49/$99/$182/$299 per month on annual billing ($59/$119/$219/$359 monthly), with Enterprise from about $999/mo. Seats run 1/3/5/10. The AI Tracker is also sold standalone as **AI Search Analytics**: per Surfer's docs, $95/mo for 50 daily prompts, $185/mo for 100, $365/mo for 200, custom above that, with yearly billing at $82/$158/$308.

Surfer's Content Editor is still the fastest way to take a keyword and turn it into a brief a writer can execute against, with live term guidance as they type. In 2026 the guidance covers AI answers as well as classic SERPs, which is a smaller change than the marketing implies but a real one.

Note a live research trap: several widely-cited "Surfer pricing 2026" articles still describe an **Essential/Scale** ladder at $99/$219 with a $95 AI Tracker add-on. Surfer's own documentation uses the Discovery/Standard/Pro/Peace of Mind names. Price from surferseo.com, not from a roundup — including this one.

**Where it breaks**

- **No free plan.** The 7-day Pro trial requires a card and converts automatically.
- **Discovery is a trap tier.** One seat and no AI tracking; most people who buy it upgrade within two months.
- **"Unlimited" isn't.** The top tier's unlimited documents are governed by a fair-usage policy reported at around 500/month.
- **Overage is pay-per-article** once you pass your monthly document allowance, which punishes exactly the high-volume teams the tool is sold to.

## 4. Clearscope

**Best for:** editorial teams who want one number that says "this draft is ready."
**Price:** Essentials $129/month, Business $399/month, Enterprise by quote. Unlimited users and projects on every plan, 14-day trial, no free tier, no annual contract. Extra pages cost $25/mo on Essentials and $15/mo on Business; extra drafts $50 and $20 respectively.

Clearscope charges by inventory, not by seat, which is unusual and mostly good: you can put your whole freelance bench in the tool without a budget conversation. Essentials includes 50 tracked prompts, 50 pages, 20 topic explorations and 20 drafts a month; Business scales those to 300/300/50/20 and adds an account manager. The grading model remains the most trusted in the category by editors specifically — reviewers score it near 4.9 on G2 and Capterra, with *value for money* consistently the weakest sub-score.

Worth flagging: entry pricing came **down** in 2026, from $189 to $129, but the plan came down with it — old Essentials included 100 inventory pages against today's 50. Any review quoting $189 is stale in both directions.

**Where it breaks**

- **It does one job.** No crawler, no backlink index, no rank tracker, and keyword research thin enough that reviewers call it basic. It is a companion to a platform, not a replacement.
- **$1,548/year minimum** with no free tier for a tool most teams open a few hours a week.
- **The cliff to Business is $270/month** — though at roughly 11 extra pages the add-on cost matches the upgrade, so do that arithmetic before buying overages.
- **Draft allowances are small** (20/month on both self-serve tiers), which quietly caps the AI writing half of the product.

## 5. Frase

**Best for:** brief-driven content operations that need SEO and GEO scoring without a four-figure budget.
**Price:** Starter $39/mo billed yearly ($49 month-to-month), Professional $103/$129, Scale $239/$299, Enterprise custom. Extra seats $29/mo. Free 7-day trial, no credit card.

Frase is the cheapest tool here that credibly covers both halves of the job. Starter gives one seat and one site, 10 articles and 50 audit pages a month, SEO and GEO scores, AI visibility on ChatGPT and Google AI, and Content Guard watching 10 pages for decay. Professional adds a content calendar, internal linking, AI crawler monitoring and Perplexity; Scale adds Claude and Gemini, 100 articles, 1,000 audit pages and client-ready exports. The SERP dissection — competitor outlines, harvested questions, topic coverage scoring — is still the fastest keyword-to-brief path in the category.

Credit where due on billing design: Starter simply stops at its limits, and pay-as-you-go on higher tiers is off by default. In a category built on overage revenue, that is a deliberate choice and worth paying for.

**Where it breaks**

- **The AI writer is the weak part** and has been for years. Serviceable first drafts, flat next to dedicated generators. Most teams brief in Frase, draft elsewhere, paste back for scoring — meaning part of your subscription pays for a writer nobody uses.
- **Three separate meters** — articles, audit pages and AI prompts — each with its own monthly allowance. Running out of one while the others sit unused is the normal experience.
- **Reputation drift.** Frase has fallen off most best-of lists over two years, and the GEO pivot reads partly as an exit from a writing fight it lost.
- **Stale reviews everywhere.** Posts still quoting a $19.99 solo tier are describing a product that hasn't existed for years.

## 6. Writesonic

**Best for:** teams that want AI-search visibility tracking *and* the fixes in the same dashboard.
**Price:** $79, $199 and $399 per month billed annually (Writesonic advertises savings of $240, $600 and $1,200/year respectively), plus custom Enterprise. Monthly rates run roughly $99/$249/$499. Agency Starter is $200/mo with client workspaces from $150/project/mo; white-label sits on Agency Enterprise.

Writesonic has completed one of the more total repositionings in this market — from a generic AI writer to an AI-search-visibility platform tracking up to ten AI surfaces (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Copilot, Meta AI, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews). The differentiator against pure monitors is the Action Center: it doesn't just report that a competitor owns a prompt, it proposes and drafts the page changes. Sentiment analysis, brand and prompt explorers, page tracking and Looker Studio integration sit on the upper tiers; Claude tracking is excluded below the top plan.

**Disclosure:** Writesonic runs the one affiliate program in this roundup we actually participate in (affiliates.writesonic.com). It didn't buy the placement and it doesn't buy the paragraph below.

**Where it breaks**

- **Credits don't roll over.** The single most consistent complaint across review platforms — unused allowance vanishes at reset, and different models burn credits at different rates.
- **Documented billing complaints.** Multiple Trustpilot and Reddit accounts describe charges after cancellation and unanswered refund requests. The aggregate rating is high (4.7/5 across 5,900+ reviews) and these are a minority — but on an annual plan the stakes justify caution. Start monthly, keep your cancellation confirmation, screenshot your billing settings.
- **It is no longer a cheap writer.** If you came for content generation, this is expensive for the job.
- **Tiered engine access.** "Track all the AI platforms" is a top-tier promise, not a $79 one.

## 7. Profound

**Best for:** funded brands and in-house teams treating answer-engine optimization as a program, not an experiment.
**Price:** Starter $99/mo (ChatGPT only, 50 prompts, 100 agent credits, one seat, one region), Growth $399/mo (3 engines, 100 prompts, 400 credits, 3 seats), Enterprise custom (up to 9–10 engines, multi-brand, SSO/SAML, SOC 2). Self-serve billing is annual, with two months free. Agency Growth starts at $99/mo with 10 pitch workspaces; full client workspaces are $399/mo each. There is no free trial.

Profound is the best-capitalized company in the category — a $96M Series C at a $1B valuation in February 2026, after a Sequoia-led Series B — and it is one of the few tools that acts rather than only reports. Agents generate and optimize content, Sheets runs them at scale, Aim organizes the work. Its Answer Engine Insights dashboard and built-in sentiment mapping are the features competitors are visibly copying.

**Where it breaks**

- **$99 is a doorway, not a price.** One engine. Real multi-engine work starts at $399/mo, or $4,788/year, and full coverage is a custom quote.
- **Annual-only self-serve.** No monthly toggle on either published tier — you commit for a year at signup.
- **Agencies are poorly served.** Reviewers repeatedly note the absence of multi-account support; five clients means five accounts, no shared dashboard, no per-client permissions.
- **Stability complaints.** Detailed reviews document upgrade bugs that deleted or duplicated prompts, 10–15 second load times, and billing failures that froze accounts. One concludes it is "over-marketed for its actual stability."
- **Nothing reaches your live site.** A human still publishes every change.

## Sidebar: the cheap end, and the free floor

If all you want is a weekly answer to "does ChatGPT mention us," you do not need a platform.

**Otterly.AI** starts at $29/month for 15 prompts across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Copilot ($25 on annual, 15% off), with Standard at $189 and Premium at $489 and extra prompt packs at $99/100. **Peec AI** publishes Starter at $95/mo (50 prompts, 3 selected models, 1 project), Pro at $245 (150 prompts), Advanced at $495 (350 prompts), all with unlimited users; API, SSO and all-model coverage require the custom tier, and Peec advertises ~15% annual savings without publishing per-plan annual rates.

And the free floor moved this year. In June 2026 Google shipped **generative AI performance reports in Search Console**, giving every site owner impressions, pages, countries, devices and dates for AI Overviews and AI Mode. It is impression data, not citation share, and — a wrinkle worth knowing — Google confirmed in early August 2026 that AI Mode *query* data lands in the main performance report rather than the separate generative-AI view, with each conversational turn counted as a new query. So those odd fragment queries in your keyword data ("yes, pricing") are AI Mode turns hiding in plain sight. Read that report for a month before you spend $399 on a dashboard.

Why any of this matters, in one number: Pew's analysis of real browsing data found users clicked a traditional result in just **8%** of searches that showed an AI summary, against 15% without one — and clicked a link *inside* the summary in 1% of visits.

## What we'd actually buy

**Solo operator or one-person content business — about $39/month.** Frase Starter, plus Search Console's generative AI report, plus Ahrefs Webmaster Tools for your own site. You get briefs, scoring, decay alerts and basic AI visibility for less than one hour of a freelancer's time. Add Otterly at $29 only if a client is asking about ChatGPT specifically.

**In-house content team at a growing company — about $348/month.** Ahrefs Lite ($129) for links and crawl data, Clearscope Essentials ($129) so editors ship consistent drafts, Otterly Lite or Frase Starter (~$29–39) for AI monitoring, and Search Console for free. This stack beats any single $400 platform because each piece is best-in-class at its job and you can cancel one without losing the others.

**Agency or multi-brand team — $600–900/month.** Semrush One Pro+ ($248.17) as the system of record so client reporting lives in one place, plus Surfer Pro ($182) for production, plus Writesonic ($199) or Peec Pro ($245) for GEO tracking and fixes across clients. Avoid Profound here until multi-account support ships, no matter how good the dashboard looks in the demo.

**Brand where AI answers are a board-level topic — $399+/month.** Profound Growth, budgeted annually, with someone whose actual job is spending the agent credits. If nobody owns that, buy the $95 Surfer AI Search Analytics tier instead and keep the difference.

## The bottom line

The uncomfortable truth of 2026 is that the AI-visibility half of this category is selling measurement of a surface nobody has learned to control yet. The tools that justify their price are the ones that do something after they measure — Surfer's editor, Clearscope's grading, Frase's agent, Writesonic's Action Center, Profound's Agents. Dashboards that only report are the easiest line item to cancel in Q1, and plenty of them will be.

Buy in this order: Search Console first, because it's free and it's your own data. A content optimizer second, because pages that earn citations are still just good pages. A crawl-and-links platform third, because technical debt caps everything above it. An AI-visibility tracker last, at the smallest tier that covers the engines your customers actually use — and on monthly billing until you've proven the reports change what you publish.
