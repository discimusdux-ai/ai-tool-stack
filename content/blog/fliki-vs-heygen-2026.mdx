Fliki and HeyGen show up on the same shortlists because both take a script and give you back a finished video. Both have free tiers, both start at roughly $28-29 a month, and both now run everything through a credit meter. Read the two pricing pages side by side and they look like competitors.

They aren't, really. The decision comes down to one question: **does a person need to be on screen?**

- **Fliki is built around a voice.** You give it an idea, a script, a blog URL or a PowerPoint; it writes or splits the script into scenes, picks stock or AI visuals for each one, and lays an AI narrator over the top. The output is a faceless explainer, a listicle Short or a narrated slideshow.
- **HeyGen is built around a face.** Its whole product is the presenter: stock avatars, a custom digital twin of you, lip-synced translation into 175+ languages, and now a shelf of generative video models (Seedance, Veo) bolted onto the same subscription.

Both tools can technically do the other job. Fliki sells avatars; HeyGen can make B-roll. But each one charges you a lot for the thing it wasn't built for, and that's where the money is in this comparison.

## The meter is the price, not the sticker

This is the section that should decide your purchase.

**Fliki charges by audio minute.** Its credit guide (updated 2026-11-03) prices voiceover by a color band next to each voice: blue voices cost 0.25 credits/minute, green 0.5, yellow 1, purple 2. Stock visuals are effectively free on top. So a 4-minute blog-to-video explainer with a green voice costs about 2-2.5 credits including the import. On Standard's 180 credits, that's dozens of videos a month.

Fliki's own FAQ is far more conservative: it says 180 credits is "ideally" 60-90 minutes of finished video because you re-spend credits every time you edit the script and it regenerates audio. Treat that FAQ number as the realistic floor and the color-band math as the ceiling. The gap between them is your editing habit.

**Where Fliki's meter turns on you:** avatars and AI clips. A stock avatar on screen is charged per second; one third-party calculation (MarketingUpgrade, 2026-10-26) put a 4-minute on-screen avatar at about 60 credits, a third of Standard. AI-generated clips default to Seedance Pro Fast at 0.1-0.15 credits per second, which adds up fast if you generate a clip for every scene.

**HeyGen charges by avatar engine.** Per HeyGen's credit help article, as reported by third-party reviewers in October-November 2026: Avatar III costs about 4 credits/minute on a video look (7 on a photo look), Avatar IV about 16 credits/minute on a photo look and roughly 31 on a video look, and Avatar V about 48 credits/minute on a photo look. One Reddit user reported 650 credits for 9:17 of Avatar V output.

Run that through Creator's 600 credits:

| Avatar engine (HeyGen) | Approx. credits/min | Minutes on Creator (600 cr) |
|---|---|---|
| Avatar III, video look | 4 | ~150 |
| Avatar IV, photo look | 16 | ~37 |
| Avatar IV, video look | ~31 | ~19 |
| Avatar V, photo look | ~48 | ~12 |

So HeyGen's "600 credits" is 150 minutes if you're happy with the older engine and about 12-19 minutes if you want the avatars in HeyGen's marketing. That's the single most important number in this comparison, and it's not on the pricing page.

The mirror image is true too: a presenter is cheap in HeyGen and expensive in Fliki; narration is cheap in Fliki and largely beside the point in HeyGen.

## What each one is actually good at

### Fliki: the faceless production line

Fliki's best feature is how little it asks of you. Paste a blog URL and it pulls the text, splits it into scenes, picks stock footage for each scene and narrates it. The setup before you hit generate doesn't cost credits. For a faceless YouTube channel, a TikTok listicle account, or a marketer turning last month's blog posts into LinkedIn videos, that's a full production line for $21-28 a month.

The voice library is the other reason to pick it. Standard includes 1,000 voices (500 of them flagged "ultra-realistic") and one voice clone; Premium goes to 2,000+ voices and multiple clones. If you publish in several languages, script translation into 80+ languages is built in.

Fliki is an active affiliate partner of ours, and it's the tool we recommend most often for faceless channels. We've also covered it in depth in our [Fliki review](/blog/fliki-review-2026).

### HeyGen: the presenter studio

HeyGen is the better tool the moment someone needs to be on camera. Its avatars, especially the newer engines, have the most convincing lip-sync in the consumer tier, and every plan (even Free) lets you build a custom avatar of yourself. For founder updates, sales intros, course lessons or personalized outreach, that's the product.

Translation is the other killer feature. Upload a video of yourself speaking English and HeyGen returns it in Spanish, German or Japanese with your lips re-synced. Pro lets you edit and proofread the translated script before rendering, which matters for anything customer-facing.

Pro has also turned into a generative video bundle: Seedance 2.5, Veo 3.1 and Nano Banana are included, plus 4K export. If you'd otherwise pay for a separate gen-video subscription, that changes Pro's value math. We review HeyGen independently; we are not in its affiliate program.

## Pros and cons

### Fliki

**Pros**
- Cheapest way to produce narrated, faceless video at volume
- Imports blog URLs and PowerPoints directly
- Huge voice library with transparent per-voice pricing (the color bands)
- Five-minute videos even on Free

**Cons**
- **Credits burn on edits, not just exports.** Every script tweak regenerates audio and charges again. G2 reviewers (June 2026) call the system "restrictive during heavy production periods" and want cheap top-ups, which don't exist self-serve.
- **Stock matching is literal.** Multiple G2 and Capterra reviewers report mismatched or irrelevant visuals that need manual swapping; one Capterra reviewer (Feb 2026) reported gibberish text artifacts in generated visuals.
- **Refunds are hard.** Trustpilot sits at 4.1 across 3,048 reviews (Oct 2026), but recent 1-star reviews (Sept and Nov 2026) describe support refusing to restore credits lost to system errors and stalling refund requests.
- **Per-user pricing.** Every teammate pays the full plan price.
- **Avatars are an add-on, not a strength.** They're limited on Standard and expensive in credits.

### HeyGen

**Pros**
- Best-in-class avatar quality and lip-sync at this price
- Custom digital twin on every plan
- Lip-synced translation into 175+ languages
- Pro bundles Seedance, Veo and 4K export

**Cons**
- **The headline credits shrink by engine.** 600 Creator credits is ~12-19 minutes on the newest avatars (see table above). A January 2026 r/heygen thread titled "Heygen misleading?" describes running out after about six videos.
- **No top-ups below Business.** On Creator and Pro you upgrade or wait for next month.
- **Plan churn.** Reddit users in May 2026 reported legacy unlimited plans being removed; a November 2026 Trustpilot review describes avatar-slot allowances changing "several times within a single month."
- **Billing complaints.** Trustpilot reviews from early 2026 report unexpected invoices, "unlimited translation" turning out to be capped, and support handled by bots; one April 2026 Reddit thread alleges credits deducted twice.
- **Short free videos.** One minute is enough to test lip-sync, not a workflow.

## Who should pick which

**Pick Fliki if:**
- You run a faceless YouTube, TikTok or Instagram channel
- You repurpose blog posts, newsletters or slide decks into video
- You care more about voice choice than about faces
- You want the most minutes per dollar and can live with swapping some stock clips

**Pick HeyGen if:**
- You, a founder, or a trainer needs to appear on camera without filming
- You need to translate existing talking-head videos with lip-sync
- You'd otherwise pay for a separate generative video tool (Pro)
- You can budget for the older Avatar III engine on routine videos and save the newer ones for hero content

**Pay for both if:** you run a channel where a presenter opens and closes each video and the middle is narrated B-roll. Make the 30-second intro and outro in HeyGen Creator and the narrated body in Fliki Standard, then stitch them in any editor. At yearly billing that's about $45/month ($21 + ~$24), and each tool only does what it's cheap at.

**Neither, if:** you need corporate training with SCORM exports and a consistent presenter across a curriculum. That's Synthesia's lane; see our [Synthesia vs HeyGen comparison](/blog/synthesia-vs-heygen-2026).

## Bottom line

On paper, Fliki and HeyGen are both $28-29 AI video makers with credit meters. In practice, Fliki's credit buys **a minute of narration** and HeyGen's credit buys **a few seconds of a face**. Choose based on whether a face belongs on screen, then check the meter for the specific voice or avatar engine you'll actually use. The sticker price won't tell you that.

For most solo creators building faceless content, Fliki Standard at $21/month yearly is the better buy. For anyone selling with a face, HeyGen Creator is the entry ticket, and Pro is worth it once you'd use the bundled generative models.
