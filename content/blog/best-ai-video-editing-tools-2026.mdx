There is no best AI video editor in 2026, and any list that crowns one is selling something. The category split into two incompatible businesses somewhere around late 2025, and the tool that is obviously correct for a documentary colorist is obviously wrong for someone turning a weekly podcast into fifteen vertical clips.

This is not a guide to *generating* video — no Runway, no Veo, no avatars. This is about footage that already exists and has to be cut, cleaned, captioned, and shipped. Every price below was read off vendor pricing pages and help docs in August 2026, with the billing cycle stated, because half the numbers circulating on aggregator sites describe plans that no longer exist.

## The one question that sorts this category

Forget feature checklists. Ask this instead: **do you want to pay for a timeline, or for a meter?**

A timeline product charges you for access to software. Resolve, Premiere, and Filmora work this way: the bill is the bill, and a fourteen-hour edit day costs the same as a fourteen-minute one. A meter product charges you for what the AI touches — media minutes, AI credits, processing minutes, transcription minutes — and your invoice moves with your output.

Meters are not automatically worse. They are how a $16/month tool can afford to run speech models over your footage. But they change what "cheap" means. A metered tool is cheap in your quiet months and unpredictable in your busy ones, and every meter in this category resets rather than rolls over, which means **you pay for your heaviest month, every month.** If your workload is spiky, buy the timeline. If your workload is small and AI-heavy, buy the meter. Getting this backwards is the single most common way people overspend on video software in 2026.

## 1. Descript — the text editor that happens to output video

**Best for:** podcasts, talking-head video, courses, anything driven by dialogue
**Price:** Free · Hobbyist $16/mo annual ($24 monthly) · Creator $24 ($35) · Business $50 ($65)

Descript's pitch has not changed in five years and does not need to: it transcribes your footage, and you edit the video by editing the words. Delete a sentence in the transcript, the sentence leaves the timeline. For interview and podcast work this is still a genuinely different way to work, not a gimmick, and everything else in the app — filler-word removal, Studio Sound, Underlord's co-editing commands, eye contact correction — hangs off that transcript.

The 2026 allowances, read off the live pricing page: Free gives one media hour a month and 100 one-time AI credits. Hobbyist is 10 media hours and 400 credits monthly. Creator is 30 hours and 800 credits. Business is 40 hours and 1,500 credits, with team scaling to five seats billed separately. Annual billing saves up to 35%.

**Where it breaks.** Descript runs two meters simultaneously and they behave differently. Media minutes are consumed **on import or record** — transcribed or not, used in the final cut or not. Drop three hours of b-roll in to grab a ten-second shot and you have spent three hours of allowance. Second, neither meter rolls over; unused balance resets on your billing date. Third, Descript publishes exactly one per-action credit cost (Studio Sound at 10 credits) and leaves every other AI action, including the Underlord features it markets hardest, undocumented — you learn the price by watching the balance fall. Fourth, the reviews split hard around the September 2025 pricing overhaul that moved AI features from effectively unlimited to credits; the pre- and post-overhaul reviews you read on G2 are describing different products. The one 2026 improvement worth knowing: Media Library moved to Drive level, so shared files can be reused without re-spending media minutes.

## 2. Adobe Premiere — the industry standard, now billing for AI separately

**Best for:** professionals whose deliverables get handed to other professionals
**Price:** Single App $22.99/mo annual, $34.49 monthly · Creative Cloud Pro $69.99/mo annual · Teams from ~$110.49/seat

The 2026 Premiere is meaningfully more automated than the 2024 one. Media Intelligence searches thousands of clips by what is visibly in them and by transcript. Object masking tracks with one click, on a tracking engine Adobe claims is 20x faster than its predecessor. Caption translation covers 27+ languages. Generative Extend adds frames to a clip in 4K when your shot is two seconds short, and the newer Generative Media tool lets you drag across empty timeline space and prompt for video or sound effects directly.

If your work leaves your machine — round-tripping to After Effects, review cycles in Frame.io, client-supplied project files — this is not really a choice, and the $22.99 is the cost of speaking the industry's language.

**Where it breaks.** The headline AI features are metered in generative credits, and the Single App plan's allocation was cut from 500 to 25 monthly credits in June 2025 — a change freelancers still describe as prohibitive. A usable generative allowance means Creative Cloud Pro at $69.99/mo annual (4,000 premium credits), or bolting on a Firefly credit subscription from $9.99/mo. Second, the monthly-billing penalty is brutal: $34.49 versus $22.99 is a 50% premium for the right to leave. Third, annual plans are non-refundable after 14 days and renew automatically. Fourth, if your deliverable is a graded master rather than a generative composite, you are paying a subscription for tools a one-time Resolve Studio license already covers.

## 3. DaVinci Resolve — the free one that is not a trial

**Best for:** anyone allergic to recurring bills; colorists; finishing work
**Price:** Free, permanently · Studio $295 one-time, perpetual, two activations

Resolve deserves its reputation. The free version is not crippleware: no watermark, no export cap, no expiry, 4K, the full Cut/Edit/Color/Fusion/Fairlight page set, multicam, proxies, and collaboration. It is the only serious answer in this roundup to "I want to spend nothing and still ship broadcast-quality work."

Studio's $295 buys the Neural Engine tier: Magic Mask v2 with a paint brush, SuperScale upscaling, temporal and spatial noise reduction, Speed Warp interpolation, IntelliTrack, Face Refinement, plus AI IntelliScript, which takes a plain-text script, matches it against transcribed takes, and assembles a rough-cut timeline with alternates stacked on disabled tracks. AI Dialogue Matcher and Audio Assistant do the equivalent for sound. There are 45-odd extra Resolve FX on top. At a one-time $295 against Premiere's $275/year, the payback period is under thirteen months and then it is free forever.

**Where it breaks.** The learning curve is the steepest here by a distance — Resolve is five applications wearing a trench coat, and the page metaphor confuses people for weeks. It is hardware-hungry; the AI tools want a real GPU, and the free version drops multi-GPU support. There is no meaningful browser or mobile story, so collaborative review workflows mean adding another tool. The AI features are firmly assistive, not generative: nothing here writes clips or captions social posts for you. And the license is per-machine with two activations, not per-person-anywhere.

## 4. CapCut — free, everywhere, and legally more complicated than you think

**Best for:** mobile-first social editing, fast turnarounds, template-driven content
**Price:** Free · Standard $9.99/mo (US App Store) · Pro $19.99/mo App Store, $179.99/yr on CapCut's US-facing page

Nothing else lets you cut, caption, beat-sync, and publish a vertical video this fast on a phone, and the free tier remains genuinely usable: you can finish a normal edit without paying as long as every asset and tool you touch is free. Auto-captions are quick and accurate enough for social. The template library is the real moat.

CapCut restructured its membership in early 2026: the old Pro was "fully upgraded" — AI points from 550 to 1,200, cloud storage from 100GB to 1TB — and repriced, with the previous rate withdrawn from new subscribers, while a cheaper Standard plan appeared underneath.

**Where it breaks.** First, there is no single global price. The US App Store, UK App Store, and CapCut's own comparison page disagree, and some signed-in checkouts show an "Ultra" tier CapCut does not publish globally. Your checkout screen is the only authority. Second — and this is the expensive misunderstanding — **a Pro subscription is not a blanket commercial license.** CapCut's Materials License Agreement grants rights per asset and per exported file: re-edit an export that used a paid asset and you may owe for that asset again. Commercial rights come from the assets, not the plan. Third, the paywall is asset-shaped: a single Pro-badged font or effect can block an export at the last step. Fourth, ownership sits with a TikTok USDS joint venture entity in the US, which is a live procurement question for some businesses.

## 5. OpusClip — one long video in, twenty shorts out

**Best for:** podcasters, streamers, and webinar teams feeding TikTok/Shorts/Reels
**Price:** Free (60 processing min/mo) · Starter $15/mo · Pro $29/mo · Business custom

OpusClip is not a general editor, and pretending otherwise is how people end up disappointed. It ingests long-form video, finds the segments most likely to perform, reframes them vertically with speaker tracking, burns in animated captions, and scores each clip for virality. At volume it is a different job than editing — it is publishing.

Pro at $29 is the tier that makes sense: 300 processing minutes, AI b-roll (50 clips/day versus Starter's 3 per month), a social scheduler, 16:9 and 1:1 alongside vertical, XML export into Premiere or Resolve when a clip deserves real work, a team workspace, and 100GB storage. The credit unit is honest and easy to model: **1 credit = 1 minute of source video**, regardless of how many clips come out. A 60-minute podcast costs 60 credits whether it yields four clips or twenty-four.

**Where it breaks.** Credits expire — 60 days on monthly plans, 12 months on annual — and cancelling does not extend them. The annual discount is real (help docs describe roughly a 50% better cost per minute, with all credits granted upfront) but **is not published on the pricing page**; it only appears inside your account's subscription panel, which is a strange way to hide your best offer. Free-tier projects vanish after three days, Starter after 29. API access is Business-only, so automation has no self-serve path. And the billing complaints are the loudest of any tool here: OpusClip's own public feedback board carries a running thread of renewals charged without reminder, a reported double subscription, and refund requests — worth knowing before you hand over a card.

## 6. VEED — captions and subtitles, priced in credits

**Best for:** marketing teams that need subtitled, brand-consistent video without an editor
**Price:** Free (watermarked) · Creator ~$12/mo, $147/yr · Pro $24/mo, $288/yr · Studio $39/mo, $465/yr — all per seat

VEED is a browser editor whose real product is subtitles: upload, auto-transcribe, style the captions to brand, translate, export. For a marketing team producing dozens of short social cuts a month, it removes an entire freelancer from the loop, and the interface is the friendliest here for non-editors.

The 2026 structure is Free / Creator / Pro / Studio / Enterprise layered over an AI credit pool — 6,000, 30,000, and 180,000 credits a year respectively, with AI-voice hours capped separately (Pro up to 144 hours/year, Studio up to 960). If a review quotes you a "Basic" or "Business" VEED plan, it is describing a lineup VEED retired.

**Where it breaks.** The free tier watermarks every export, and watermark-free plus commercial use starts at Creator — VEED's free tier is a demo, not a product. Reliability is the persistent complaint across years of reviews: buffering, lag, crashes, and export failures on longer files, which is a serious problem for a browser-only tool with no offline fallback. Billing practices draw the sharpest criticism — charges after cancellation, refused refunds, opaque auto-renewal. And AI quality is uneven: voice cloning, background removal, and eye-contact correction get mixed-to-negative marks from paying users. Creator's month-to-month price is displayed ambiguously behind the annual toggle, so read your checkout.

## 7. Kapwing — the collaborative browser editor with a legible meter

**Best for:** distributed teams editing the same project in a browser
**Price:** Free (50 lifetime credits) · Pro $16/member/mo annual, $24 monthly · Business $50/member annual, $64 monthly

Kapwing's advantage is that it is a shared workspace first and an editor second. Invite the team, everyone edits in the browser, no installs, no file handoffs. Free workspaces cap uploads at 250MB and exports at one minute; Pro raises those to 6GB and 120 minutes.

Credit with Kapwing where it is due: **its meter is the most transparent in this roundup.** The help docs publish per-action costs — subtitle transcription 1 credit/minute, translations 2, Repurpose Studio 2, Clean Audio 2, Enhance Voice 4, Split Vocals 10, dubbing 10 per 30 seconds, lip sync 30/minute, eye contact 30/minute, TTS 1 credit per 100 characters, video resize 4. Pro gets 1,000 credits a month, Business 4,000. You can model your bill before you spend a dollar, which is exactly what Descript refuses to let you do.

**Where it breaks.** The free tier's 50 credits are lifetime, not monthly — it is a taster. The expensive actions eat the pool fast: at 30 credits a minute, lip sync exhausts Pro's monthly 1,000 in 33 minutes of footage. Pricing is strictly per member with no free viewer seat, so a five-person team on Business is $250/month annual, $320 monthly. And Kapwing locks plan changes: switching annual-to-monthly or Business-to-Pro requires cancelling and re-subscribing after the cycle ends, which is an unusually rigid policy for a self-serve product.

### Sidebar: why Filmora keeps showing up on lists and not on ours

Filmora is aggressively cheap and heavily marketed — Advanced at $59.99/year, a Perpetual license around $79.99, a Creative Assets tier near $99.99/year, and AI credits sold separately at $9.99/month. On paper it undercuts everything above.

The details erode it. AI credits from a subscription are **valid 30 days and reclaimed on reissue**; unused credits simply vanish each cycle. Perpetual licences cover updates to your major version only, so Filmora 15 to 16 is another purchase. Licences are platform-specific and cannot move between Windows and Mac. And the public review record on Trustpilot and Apple's forums is dominated by refund refusals, crashes, and users who bought a plan only to find the assets they wanted still gated — including one documented case of paying $9.99 for 100 AI credits that never arrived and being denied a refund. A cheap tool you cannot get a refund from is not cheap.

## What we would actually buy

**The solo podcaster — $288/year.** Descript Creator at $24/month annual, nothing else. Your edit is a transcript edit; every other tool here is a detour.

**The YouTuber repurposing to shorts — $348/year.** DaVinci Resolve free for the long-form cut, OpusClip Pro at $29/month for the clips. This pairing beats every all-in-one, because the all-in-ones are weak at exactly one of the two jobs.

**The freelance editor with clients — $275/year.** Adobe Premiere Single App at $22.99/month annual. Add Firefly Standard at $9.99/month only in months you actually need generative credits — it stacks on any Creative Cloud subscription and can be dropped.

**The five-person marketing team — $1,440/year.** VEED Creator at $147/year × 5 seats, plus CapCut Pro on one shared mobile device at ~$180/year for template work. If collaboration matters more than subtitles, swap to Kapwing Pro at $16/member/month annual ($960/year) and accept the credit ceiling.

**The budget of zero.** DaVinci Resolve plus CapCut Free covers a startling amount of ground: professional finishing and fast social output, no watermark, no subscription. Most people reading a list like this could stop here and be fine for a year.

## The bottom line

The interesting change in 2026 is not that AI got better at editing. It is that vendors stopped selling software and started selling throughput. Descript bills your imports. Adobe bills your generations. OpusClip bills your source minutes. VEED and Kapwing bill your AI actions. Only Blackmagic still sells you a program and then leaves you alone — which is why a $295 one-time licence keeps looking better every time somebody's credit pool resets on a deadline.

Pick the meter that matches the shape of your work, and check what happens to your unused balance at the end of the month. That number, not the headline price, is what you are actually signing up for.
