Synthesia and HeyGen are the two names everyone shortlists when they need a talking AI presenter. Both turn a script into a video of a photoreal avatar speaking to camera. Both translate that video into well over a hundred languages. Both moved to a credit meter in 2026. On a feature checklist they look almost identical.

They are not built for the same buyer, though, and that's the whole decision. **Synthesia is a training studio**: it's built around modules, templates, SCORM, surveys and a library of corporate-looking presenters that stay consistent across a 40-video curriculum. **HeyGen is a marketing engine**: it's built around *your* face and voice, fast exports, a Video Agent that drafts from a prompt or a URL, and a growing shelf of generative video models (Seedance, Veo) bolted onto the same subscription.

So the question isn't which avatar looks more real. It's: **is the video going to be watched because someone has to, or because someone chose to?** Mandatory viewing (onboarding, compliance, product training) rewards consistency and structure. That's Synthesia. Optional viewing (ads, social, sales outreach, YouTube) rewards personality and speed. That's HeyGen.

We checked both pricing pages and help centers on November 6, 2026. Both vendors rebuilt their plans this year, so any review older than about two months is quoting a price ladder that no longer exists.

## What each one actually is in late 2026

**Synthesia** is a London company that sold avatar video to enterprise L&D teams long before "AI video" was a consumer category. Its product today is a full authoring environment: a slide-style editor, 60+ templates, PowerPoint import, Express-2 avatars that gesture from the script, Dubbing 2.0, survey questions inside videos, and an AI video assistant on paid plans. On October 1, 2026 it rebuilt its self-serve plans around a single credit pool shared by video, dubbing, surveys and its Syren generative feature. Everything it ships points at one buyer: the person responsible for a training library.

**HeyGen** started as the "make an avatar of yourself in five minutes" tool and has kept moving toward creators and marketers. In 2026 its paid plans gained Avatar IV and V, a Video Agent that builds a draft from a prompt or a web page, HeyGen Voice for text-to-speech, its own HeyGen Video 1 model, and (on Pro) access to third-party generative models: Seedance 2.5, Veo 3.1 and Nano Banana for images. It also moved to one credit balance, replacing the old split between "unlimited" Avatar III videos and a small Premium Credit pool. Creator now includes three times the credits it used to, according to HeyGen's own help center.

The direction of travel matters more than today's feature list. Synthesia is getting deeper into structured learning. HeyGen is getting wider into general-purpose generative video. If you buy one for the other's job, you'll pay for features you don't use and miss ones you need.

### The meter is the price, not the sticker

Both companies changed what a credit buys in 2026. Synthesia replaced its minutes-per-plan model with a shared credit pool on October 1, 2026 and renamed Creator to Pro. HeyGen merged its "unlimited" Avatar III tier and Premium Credits into one balance and rebalanced Creator. Old reviews that say "Synthesia Starter: 120 minutes a year" or "HeyGen Creator: unlimited videos" describe products you can no longer buy.

The practical consequence is different for each. On **Synthesia**, the meter is predictable: about 100 credits a minute of avatar video, so you can work out a curriculum budget on a napkin. On **HeyGen**, the meter depends on which model you pick, and the newest models are the default in the new editor. One November 2026 Trustpilot reviewer complained that HeyGen "make you use their latest model and don't even show how many credits it's using until you've used them." Whether or not that's fair to every workflow, it's the right thing to test in week one: render the same 60-second script on each engine and write down the credit cost.

### Avatar quality

The honest answer is that both are good and both still break in the same places: close-ups, emotional delivery, and long uninterrupted takes. An October 2026 test that ran one 60-second training script through both platforms found HeyGen had tighter lip-sync (especially on P, B and T sounds) and faster exports, while Synthesia's stock presenters looked more consistent in lighting and posture and its Japanese and Spanish narration sounded more natural. G2's hands-on comparison came out the same way, giving HeyGen the realism win with a note that Synthesia's avatars look deliberately "corporate."

That fits the buyer split. A training module wants the presenter in video 1 to look exactly like the presenter in video 40. A LinkedIn ad wants the presenter to feel like a person.

### The persuasion problem

One finding is worth flagging before you buy either for paid ads. A September 2026 review quoted an agency that ran ad creative through Synthesia, HeyGen, Veo and human-shot footage side by side; the avatar versions produced lower click-through and conversion than the alternatives, in some cases lower than poorly filmed human video. The stated reason was trust. That's a single agency's result, not a study, but it matches what we hear: avatars work when the viewer already has a reason to watch (training, onboarding, internal updates) and struggle when they have to win attention cold. HeyGen's self-avatar softens this, because it's *your* face, but it doesn't remove it.

## Where each one breaks

### Synthesia cons

- **Purchases are non-refundable.** Synthesia's Customer Terms say purchases are non-cancelable and non-refundable even if unused. Cancelling stops renewal; it doesn't recover the period. Use the free Basic plan to test before buying a year.
- **The ladder has a big jump.** Starter's ~12 extra minutes a month on top of the free 10 is fine for a small team, but Pro is a 4-6× price step. Reviewers who need 30 minutes a month sit awkwardly between the two.
- **Presenters read as "corporate."** Great for compliance, flat for marketing. Several reviewers describe modules starting to look the same after a few videos with the same template.
- **SCORM is Enterprise-only.** If you need to drop videos into an LMS as packages, the self-serve plans won't get you there.
- **Prices vary by country.** Two people comparing notes may see different numbers on the same page.

### HeyGen cons

- **Credit burn depends on the model.** The newest avatars and generative models cost far more per minute, and the default editor nudges you toward them. Plan your monthly volume on the engine you'll actually use.
- **Creator can't buy top-ups.** HeyGen's help center states Creator doesn't support one-time credit purchases; run out and your only option is upgrading to Pro.
- **Billing and cancellation complaints.** HeyGen holds a strong overall Trustpilot rating across 2,400+ reviews, but the negative ones cluster on billing: a September 2026 reviewer who cancelled in July says they were charged again in September and October because the plan had been "paused," with only October refunded. Others report slow, templated support. Keep cancellation confirmations.
- **"Unlimited" legacy confusion.** Older plan pages and affiliate reviews still describe unlimited Avatar III videos. That model is gone; credits are the meter for everything.
- **No LMS story.** If your videos have to live in an LMS with tracking, HeyGen isn't built for it.

## Who should pick which

**Pick Synthesia if** you're building a training library, onboarding program, compliance course, or internal comms series; you need dozens of videos that look consistent; you want quizzes or surveys inside the video; or you'll eventually need SCORM, SSO and procurement-friendly contracts. Start on Basic, then Starter annual if your volume is under roughly 20 minutes a month.

**Pick HeyGen if** you're a founder, marketer, creator or sales team that wants *your own* face and voice on video; you post to social or run outreach at volume; you want 4K, or generative B-roll in the same subscription. Creator is the floor for a personal avatar; Pro is where most serious users end up once they hit the credit wall.

**Pick neither if** your video doesn't need a face. A lot of explainer, faceless YouTube and blog-to-video content works better as voiceover plus visuals, and that's far cheaper per minute. That's what [Fliki](/blog/fliki-review-2026) is for: script or blog post in, narrated video with stock footage, AI images and captions out, without an avatar meter at all.

## Bottom line

Synthesia and HeyGen have converged on features and diverged on purpose. Synthesia is the better tool when the viewer *has* to watch and you need forty consistent videos with quizzes attached. HeyGen is the better tool when the viewer *chooses* to watch and the presenter needs to be you. Both now charge by a credit meter that changed this year; Synthesia's is easier to predict, HeyGen's buys more cutting-edge output. Test both on the free tier with the same script, record the credit cost, and buy annual only after you know your monthly minutes.
