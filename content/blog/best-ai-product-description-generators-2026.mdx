# Best AI Product Description Generators 2026: 7 Tools, Priced Per SKU

Every AI writer on the market will happily produce a product description. That is exactly the problem. Writing 120 words about a cast-iron skillet stopped being hard in 2023. What is still hard, and what you are actually paying for, is doing it for 40 or 4,000 SKUs without inventing features, without every page sounding the same, and without your feed getting flagged.

So this roundup does not rank tools by how pretty one sample description reads. It ranks them by the question that decides your bill: **are you paying per word, per seat, or per product?** Once you know how many SKUs you have and who on your team fact-checks the output, the right tool picks itself.

## The framing question: per word, per seat, or per product?

Product descriptions are short, repetitive, and numerous. That makes the *meter* more important than in almost any other writing category.

- **Per word / per character** (Rytr Free, most legacy AI writers): cheap until you start regenerating. Every rewrite of a 150-word description eats the same allowance as the first draft.
- **Per seat** (Jasper, Copy.ai Chat, ChatGPT): unlimited-ish generation, but you pay for people, not output. Great for one person grinding through a catalog. Terrible if five people each need a login.
- **Per product** (Describely, Hypotenuse at enterprise scale): you pay for SKUs covered, and regenerations are free inside that window. This is the only meter that maps directly to what you are trying to finish.

Here is the trap: a per-word plan looks cheapest for a 30-product boutique and is genuinely cheapest there. At 3,000 SKUs, the per-product tools cost real money ($2,250 at Describely's pay-as-you-go rate) but also bring CSV import, store sync and audits that a $9 writer does not.

There is a second meter nobody prints on the pricing page: **your review time**. Shopify's own help center warns that generated text "can include things such as product benefits even when you didn't list any explicitly." You are legally responsible for every claim on that page. A tool that invents "BPA-free" or "dishwasher safe" costs you returns, not credits.

## 1. Rytr — best for small catalogs on a budget

**Best for:** solo sellers and small shops with under a few hundred SKUs who will paste in attributes one product at a time.
**Price:** Free (10,000 characters/month), Lite $9/mo ($7.50 billed yearly) with unlimited characters under a fair-usage policy, Personal $19/mo, Pro $49/mo. Yearly billing is advertised as 20% off.

Rytr has quietly reinvented itself in 2026. The pricing page now leads with an "AI relationship manager" (connected inboxes, relationship tracking, daily briefings), and the old $29 Unlimited plan is gone. The part that matters here survived intact: 40+ writing use cases, including product descriptions, 30+ languages, tone selection, and unlimited characters from the $9 Lite plan.

For product copy that is the cheapest real paid option in this list. Feed it the product name, three to six concrete attributes and a tone, and you get a usable first draft in seconds. Because Lite is unlimited, regenerating until a description sounds right costs nothing extra, which is exactly what per-character meters punish.

The free plan's 10,000 characters is roughly 60-80 short descriptions a month, counting both what you paste in and what comes out. That is enough to test it on your real catalog before paying.

**Where it breaks:**
- **No bulk import or store sync.** It is one product at a time. At 1,000+ SKUs you will feel every click.
- **The product is drifting.** Engage, relationships and briefings now headline the plans; the writer is the legacy core. Higher tiers mostly add features a store owner will not use.
- **"Fair usage" is undefined.** Unlimited has a ceiling Rytr does not publish.
- **Generic phrasing.** Without strong inputs, outputs lean on stock adjectives. Give it facts, not vibes.

## 2. Shopify Sidekick — best if you already pay Shopify

**Best for:** Shopify merchants who want drafts inside the product editor without another subscription.
**Price:** included in the Shopify admin (you pay for your Shopify plan, not the AI).

Shopify's generator now lives under Sidekick. Open a product, click the AI icon in the description toolbar, give it the title and keywords, and it drafts copy in place. It works in every language Shopify supports and matches the language of your prompt. Sidekick also does `/product-description` prompts for SEO-flavored rewrites from the chat panel.

The appeal is zero friction. No exports, no copy-paste between tabs, no extra login. For a store adding 10 products a week, that convenience beats any feature list.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Desktop only.** Shopify's help center says text generation is not supported on the iPhone or Android apps.
- **Shopify itself warns about invented claims.** Generated copy can add benefits you never listed and "facts based on other published content related to similar products." Read every line.
- **Not a batch tool.** It drafts per product; there is no proper "rewrite all 2,000 descriptions to this template" job with review queues.
- **Locked to Shopify.** Useless for Amazon, Etsy or a WooCommerce site.

## 3. Describely — best pay-per-product option

**Best for:** stores that want to clean up a backlog of a few hundred SKUs without a subscription.
**Price:** first 5 products free, then $0.75 per product pay-as-you-go. On the Shopify App Store: 50-product plan $28/mo ($280/yr), 250-product plan $138/mo ($1,380/yr). High-volume plans are custom.

Describely is the only tool in this roundup that sells exactly what you want: finished products. One credit covers unlimited generations *and* audits for that product for 30 days after the first job, and purchased credits stay valid for a year. That means you can rewrite a description ten times, try three tones, and still pay 75 cents.

It has also repositioned around "Catalog AI SEO + GEO": audits that score each product page for search and AI-assistant discovery, plus data enrichment and image processing as separate credit types.

The math is clean. 200 products = $150, one time. That is less than two months of Jasper for one seat.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Extras are metered separately.** Data enrichment runs $0.55 per credit and image processing $0.05 per credit; product credits cover content and audits only.
- **The 30-day window.** Unlimited regenerations stop 30 days after you first touch a product. Come back in month three and you pay again.
- **Recurring plans cost more per product.** 50 products for $28/mo is $0.56 each only if you add 50 *new* products every month.
- **Thin public review base.** The Shopify listing showed just three reviews when we checked. Test on your own catalog before committing.

## 4. Hypotenuse AI — best for big, messy catalogs

**Best for:** ecommerce teams with thousands of SKUs, incomplete attribute data, and a PIM or ERP to integrate with.
**Price:** custom. Both current plans (Basic and Ecommerce Enterprise) say "Custom" with no dollar figure on the page.

Hypotenuse used to be the $29 Jasper alternative. That product is gone. In 2026 it is a product-data platform: bulk description generation, attribute enrichment from images, URLs, UPCs or factsheets, tagging against your own taxonomy, an AI quality checker, and a bulk image editor. Basic still includes the product description generator, 20+ other ecommerce content types and 40+ languages on one seat.

This is the right tool when your real problem is not writing but *data*. If half your SKUs are missing materials or dimensions, enrichment fills the gaps before the copy is written, which is the actual fix for hallucinated specs. G2 reviewers rate it highly (4.7/5 across 70+ reviews per one aggregator) and repeatedly praise Shopify batch generation.

**Where it breaks:**
- **No public price.** You cannot budget without a sales call, and "Contact us for more seats or words" means a cap exists that you cannot see.
- **Repetition at scale.** Multiple G2 reviews flag repeated sentences across long batches, which defeats the point of unique copy.
- **Renewal jumps.** One independent review reports renewals rising 15-40% after year one for some users. Negotiate a price lock.
- **Setup cost.** Brand voice training needs real source material and time; it pays back only at volume.

## 5. Jasper — best for brand voice across a team

**Best for:** marketing teams where product copy is one of many content types and brand consistency matters more than price per SKU.
**Price:** Pro $69/seat/mo monthly or $59/seat/mo yearly (1 seat, 2 Brand Voices, 5 Knowledge assets, 3 Audiences). Business is custom and adds Jasper Grid for scaled content, API access and custom agents. 7-day free trial.

Jasper's Brand Voice and Knowledge assets are the real reason to pay. Load your style guide and a few product sheets, and outputs stay closer to how your brand actually talks than a generic writer manages. Jasper Grid on Business is built for exactly the spreadsheet-style "one row per product" job.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The bulk feature is on the custom tier.** Pro users are back to one-at-a-time drafting.
- **Per-seat pricing scales with headcount,** not output. Three writers on Pro is $177/mo even on yearly billing.
- **Annual plans are hard to escape.** Jasper's help center allows a 7-day refund window on annual plans, then no refunds for unused time.
- **Affiliate note:** Jasper's affiliate program has been dead since January 2025. We earn nothing from it.

## 6. Copy.ai — best multi-model chat for a small team

**Best for:** teams of up to five who want one shared chat with OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini models.
**Price:** Chat $29/mo for 5 seats ($24/mo billed yearly, $288/yr). Workflow plans start at Growth, $1,000/mo billed yearly ($12,000/yr) with 20,000 workflow credits. No free plan is listed on the pricing page.

Copy.ai is no longer really a copywriting tool; it is a go-to-market automation platform. But the $29 Chat tier is a bargain for product copy: five seats, unlimited words in chat, unlimited projects, three model families. Build a project with your brand guidelines and a sample description, then generate inside it.

**Where it breaks:**
- **The real bulk engine costs $12,000/yr.** Workflows, the feature that would process a CSV, start at Growth.
- **No product-specific features.** No store sync, attribute fields or feed export. It is a chat box with good models.
- **Positioning drift.** The pricing page is built for enterprise GTM teams; small sellers are an afterthought.

## 7. ChatGPT — best for DIY with your own prompts

**Best for:** sellers comfortable building a prompt template and pasting rows from a spreadsheet.
**Price:** Free tier available; Go around $8/mo in supported regions; Plus $20/mo; Pro tiers far higher.

For anyone willing to spend an hour on a good prompt, ChatGPT is the most flexible option here. Paste a table of 20 products with attributes, give it one gold-standard description, and ask for the rest in the same structure. Upload a CSV on paid plans and it can return one.

**Where it breaks:**
- **You are the workflow.** No queue, no store sync, no audit. Consistency depends entirely on your prompt discipline.
- **Long batches drift.** Quality and format slip deep into a long list; work in chunks of 20-30.
- **Ads and limits on cheap tiers.** Free and Go carry ads in many markets, and usage limits on the free tier change without notice.

## Don't skip this: Google Merchant Center's AI-text rule

If your descriptions feed Google Shopping, read Merchant Center's "AI-generated content" policy. When titles or descriptions are created with generative AI, Google requires you to submit them through the **structured_title** and **structured_description** attributes, with `digital_source_type` set to `trained_algorithmic_media`, instead of or in addition to the plain `title` and `description`. AI-generated images must also keep their IPTC metadata.

None of the general-purpose writers above handle this for you. Plan for it in your feed tool before you bulk-replace 2,000 descriptions.

## What we'd actually buy

**30-product boutique, adding a few a month:** Rytr Free to test, then Lite at $90/yr ($7.50 × 12). If you are on Shopify, use Sidekick for new products and Rytr for rewrites. Total: about $90/yr.

**400-SKU Shopify store with a stale backlog:** Describely pay-as-you-go for the backlog (400 × $0.75 = $300 once), then Sidekick for new products. Total year one: about $300.

**Multi-channel seller (Shopify + Amazon + Etsy), 1,000 SKUs:** ChatGPT Plus ($240/yr) with a strict template for marketplace bullets, plus Rytr Lite ($90/yr) for quick one-offs. About $330/yr, but budget real hours for QA.

**5,000+ SKUs with gaps in product data:** get a Hypotenuse quote and insist on a renewal cap. Enrichment is the feature you are buying.

**Marketing team of three where products are one content type of many:** Copy.ai Chat at $288/yr for all five seats beats three Jasper seats ($2,124/yr) unless you need Jasper's brand-voice controls.

## Bottom line

The best product description generator is the one whose meter matches your catalog. Under a few hundred SKUs, a cheap unlimited writer like Rytr or your platform's built-in Sidekick wins. A one-off backlog is a per-product job, which is Describely's sweet spot. Thousands of SKUs with bad data need a data platform, not a writer.

Whatever you pick, feed it facts. Every tool on this list will invent a benefit if you give it room to, and you are the one liable for it.
