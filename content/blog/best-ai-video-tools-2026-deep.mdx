# Best AI Video Tools 2026: Pick by the Footage You Start With

"Best AI video tool" is the wrong question, because "AI video" now covers three different products that share a label. Some tools **generate** footage from a prompt. Some turn **text into a finished video** with a voice or an avatar. Some take **footage you already shot** and edit or repurpose it.

Choosing between them is easy once you ask one question: **what are you starting with?** This guide sorts nine tools by that question, using pricing checked on October 10, 2026, and explains the meter each one bills you on. In this category, the meter matters more than the sticker price.

## The Framing: What Footage Are You Starting With?

**1. Nothing (generative).** You type a prompt and the model makes moving images. Runway, Kling and Google's Veo models are here. You pay **per second generated**, including the takes you throw away, so this is the most expensive kind of video per finished minute.

**2. Text (text-to-video).** You have words, and the tool supplies voice, visuals or a talking avatar. Fliki, Synthesia and HeyGen are here. Narrated or presenter-led video is the **cheapest per finished minute**. HeyGen's own October 2026 cost analysis puts presenter-style video at roughly $1.50–$3 per minute, against more than $15 for a flagship generative model.

**3. Footage you already shot (repurposing and editing).** You have a podcast, webinar or vlog. OpusClip, Descript and CapCut are here. You pay **per minute uploaded** or per seat, not per second generated.

Most creators only need one category. Many buy a generative tool when what they actually needed was a clipper.

## 1. OpusClip — Best for Repurposing Long Video

**Best for:** podcasters, YouTubers, streamers and webinar hosts
**Price:** Free (60 credits/mo, watermark) · Starter $15/mo · Pro $29/mo or **$14.50/mo billed annually**

Upload an episode and OpusClip returns vertical clips with animated captions, reframing that keeps the speaker in shot, and a Virality Score for ranking them. **ClipAnything** works on non-talking footage too (sports, vlogs, gameplay), and you can prompt it in plain English for specific moments. Pro exports to Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve and includes a scheduler.

**The meter:** 1 credit = 1 minute of source video, whether you keep 2 clips or 20. Pro's 300 credits cover about five hour-long episodes a month. Annual Pro is cheaper than Starter and gives twice the credits.

**Where it breaks:** processing failures and hangs at busy times; billing complaints (charges after cancelling, no refunds); clips that end too early; caption typos on names. [Full OpusClip review →](/blog/opus-clip-review-2026)

## 2. Fliki — Best for Turning Text into Narrated Video

**Best for:** faceless YouTube, explainers, blog-to-video, slide decks
**Price:** Free (3 credits/mo, 720p, watermark) · Standard $28/mo ($21 annual) · Premium $88/mo ($66 annual)

Paste a script, blog URL or PPT, and Fliki builds scenes, narration, stock visuals, captions and music. Its voice library has **2,000+ voices in 80+ languages**, plus voice cloning and translation/dubbing.

**The meter:** voiceover costs 0.25–2 credits per minute depending on the voice tier, stock and uploaded media are free, and **AI video costs 0.1–6 credits per second**. A 5-minute stock-footage video can cost 2.5 credits, while the same month's 180 Standard credits buy as little as 30 seconds of top-tier AI footage.

**Where it breaks:** cancellation and refund complaints, credits lost to voices skipping words, "AI video" that's mostly pans over stills, slow support. [Full Fliki review →](/blog/fliki-review-2026)

## 3. Synthesia — Best for Training and Corporate Explainers

**Best for:** L&D teams, onboarding, internal comms, multilingual how-tos
**Price:** Basic free (10 min/mo, watermark) · Starter **$19/mo or $14/mo billed yearly** · Pro from $59/mo billed yearly · Enterprise custom

Synthesia turns a script into a video with a polished AI presenter (125+ stock avatars on Starter), screen recordings and branded templates. Pro adds personal avatars, interactive video and branded video pages. Enterprise adds 1-click translation into 140+ languages, SCORM export and SSO.

**The meter:** Synthesia recently replaced minutes-per-year plans with a **credit pool**. One minute of video is 100 credits, and every plan includes a 10-minute monthly free allowance on top.

**Where it breaks:** avatars are presentational rather than expressive, which suits training more than social. Personal avatars need Pro. Larger features like SCORM and SSO are Enterprise-only. [Full Synthesia review →](/blog/synthesia-review-2026)

## 4. HeyGen — Best for Marketing and Social Avatars

**Best for:** creators and marketers who want expressive avatar video, UGC-style ads, translation
**Price:** Free (3 videos/mo) · Creator **$29/mo ($24/mo annual)**, 600 credits · Pro from $49/mo · Business $149/mo + $20/seat

HeyGen's avatars are more expressive and creator-oriented than Synthesia's, and its video translation is a standout. Business adds SSO, SCORM, LMS integrations and Zapier/Make/n8n connectors.

**The meter:** credit-based, with one monthly pool shared across all features. Check how many credits your typical video uses before you choose a tier.

**Where it breaks:** credits are shared across features, so translation and avatar generation compete for the same pool, and seats add up quickly on Business. (We don't have an affiliate relationship with HeyGen.)

## 5. Runway — Best Generative Toolset

**Best for:** filmmakers, motion designers, ad creatives who need generated shots
**Price:** Free (one-time 125 credits) · Standard $15/mo ($12 annual), 625 credits · Pro $35/mo ($28 annual), 2,250 credits · Max $95/mo ($76 annual)

Runway is a full creative suite built around its Gen-4.5 model. It also offers other models (including Kling and Seedance variants) and image generation.

**The meter:** Gen-4.5 costs **12 credits per second**. Standard's 625 credits come to about **52 seconds of Gen-4.5 video a month**. Standard and Pro credits don't roll over. Max rolls over one month. Discarded takes cost the same as the ones you keep.

**Where it breaks:** it's expensive per finished minute, credits disappear quickly while you iterate, and the monthly reset punishes irregular use. (No affiliate relationship.)

## 6. Kling — Best Budget Generative Model

**Best for:** experimenting with prompt-to-video on a small budget
**Price:** Standard $6.99 first month, then **$8.80/mo** (660 credits) · Pro $25.99 first month, then $32.56/mo (3,000 credits)

Kling's cost per credit is among the lowest in the category, and Kling 3.0 is competitive on motion quality.

**The meter:** the cheap headline price is a **first-month promotion**. Subscription credits expire one month after they're issued and disappear if you cancel, while purchased credits last two years.

**Where it breaks:** **licensing.** The free tier has no commercial rights, and one October 2026 guide reports that Kling's general terms restrict commercial use without written permission. Read the terms before using it for client work. (No affiliate relationship.)

## 7. Google Veo and Flow — Best If You Already Pay for Google AI

**Best for:** Google AI subscribers and developers
**Price:** Google AI Plus $4.99/mo (200 Flow credits) · AI Pro $19.99/mo (1,000 Flow credits) · Ultra from $99.99/mo · Veo 3.1 API **$0.40/sec** (720p/1080p)

Google's newer Gemini Omni Flash model comes with paid Google AI plans in Flow, its filmmaking studio. Veo 3.1 adds native audio, scene extension and first/last-frame control. If you already pay for AI Pro for Gemini ([our Gemini review](/blog/gemini-advanced-review-2026)), you have a generative video budget included.

**Where it breaks:** the API bills per second with no free tier, and it's easy to overspend while experimenting. (No affiliate relationship.)

## 8. Descript — Best for Editing Footage You Shot

**Best for:** podcast and YouTube editing, talking-head cleanup
**Price:** Hobbyist $16/mo annual ($24 monthly), 10 media hours + 400 AI credits · Creator $24 ($35), 30 hours + 800 credits · Business $50 ($65)

You edit video by editing its transcript. Underlord, the AI co-editor, plus Studio Sound, filler-word removal and Create Clips cover most of what a solo creator needs.

**The meter:** two meters, **media hours** (everything you upload or record) and **AI credits** (Underlord, Studio Sound, avatars, generation). Heavy AI users run out of credits before hours. (No affiliate relationship.) See [Loom vs Descript](/blog/loom-vs-descript-2026).

## 9. CapCut — Best for Hands-On Short-Form Editing

**Best for:** TikTok-native creators who want templates and manual control

CapCut is still the default editor for trend-driven short-form content. Its pricing changed in 2026. Newsweek reported in February that the annual Pro price rose from about $77 to $179.99, and CapCut's help center confirms a new membership structure, with a cheaper **Standard** plan alongside the upgraded Pro. Check the in-app price for your region before you commit. (No affiliate relationship.)

## What We'd Actually Buy

- **Podcaster or YouTuber publishing weekly:** OpusClip Pro annual (**$174/yr**). Add Descript Hobbyist annual (**$192/yr**) if you also edit full episodes. About $366/yr in total.
- **Faceless channel or blog-to-video:** Fliki Standard annual (**$252/yr**), using stock footage and standard voices.
- **Training/L&D team:** Synthesia Starter annual (**$168/yr**) to pilot, then Pro once you need personal avatars.
- **Ad creative needing generated shots:** Runway Standard monthly to test, then Pro. Budget per second, not per month.
- **Curious and on a budget:** start with free plans. OpusClip, Fliki and Synthesia all let you make real videos before paying.

## The Bottom Line

The tool that's best for you depends on what footage you start with. If you already record long videos, a clipper like OpusClip returns more per dollar than any generative model. If you have words but no footage, Fliki (narration) or Synthesia (presenter) will produce publishable video quickly and cheaply. Generative video from Runway, Kling and Veo is impressive, but it's the most expensive per finished minute, so use it for specific shots rather than whole videos.
